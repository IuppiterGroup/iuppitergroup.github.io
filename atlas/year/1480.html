    The chronicle of these years

    • Le Thanh Tong Destroys VijayaA.D. 1471, Vijaya, Vietnam. Le Thanh Tong, the ablest of the Le emperors, led a host said to number a quarter of a million against Champa, stormed its capital of Vijaya, slew tens of thousands, and carried off thirty thousand captives with King Tra Toan. The northern half of Champa was annexed; what remained in the far south survived only as a shrunken vassal.
    • Ivan III Annexes NovgorodA.D. 1478, Novgorod, Russia. After crushing Novgorod's army on the Shelon in 1471, Ivan III of Moscow marched on the republic again and in January 1478 forced its surrender. The veche was abolished, its great bell carried off to Moscow, and its leading families deported. Moscow now ruled the north from the Baltic to the Urals, and Ivan styled himself sovereign of all Rus'.
    • The Great Stand on the UgraA.D. 1480, Ugra River, Russia. Ivan III having ceased to pay tribute, Khan Akhmat of the Great Horde marched on Moscow in 1480 and found the Russian army waiting across the river Ugra. For weeks the hosts faced each other; as winter came and his Lithuanian allies failed him, Akhmat withdrew. The Tatar yoke, laid on Rus' two and a half centuries before, was ended.
    • São Jorge da MinaA.D. 1482, Elmina, Ghana. Diogo de Azambuja landed with some six hundred men and blocks of dressed stone shipped from Portugal, and in a few weeks raised the castle of São Jorge da Mina on the Gold Coast, the first European fortress in Africa south of the Sahara. Through it the gold of the Akan forest flowed to Lisbon, bought in part with slaves whom the Portuguese purchased in Benin and sold to the Akan merchants; in later centuries, under the Dutch, its dungeons held slaves bought from African traders for the Atlantic crossing.
    • Bosworth FieldA.D. 1485, Bosworth, England. On 22 August 1485 Henry Tudor, a Lancastrian exile with French backing, met Richard III near Market Bosworth. When the Stanleys turned against the king, Richard charged at Henry and was hacked down, the last English king to die in battle. Henry VII founded the Tudor dynasty and, marrying Elizabeth of York, ended the Wars of the Roses.
    • Dias Rounds the CapeA.D. 1488, Mossel Bay, South Africa. Driven south out of sight of land by storms, Bartolomeu Dias turned east and found the coast running northeast: he had passed the tip of Africa unawares. He landed at Mossel Bay among Khoikhoi herders, pressed on to the Great Fish River until his crews refused to go farther, and on the voyage home sighted the cape that King John II named Good Hope.
    • A New WorldA.D. 1492, San Salvador, Bahamas. In January, Ferdinand and Isabella take Granada, ending nearly eight centuries of Moorish rule in Spain. In October, sailing under their flag, Christopher Columbus crosses the Atlantic and makes landfall in the Bahamas. The known world is about to double in size.
    • The Fall of GranadaA.D. 1492, Alhambra, Granada. On 2 January 1492, after a war of ten years, Boabdil, last Nasrid emir of Granada, handed the keys of the Alhambra to Ferdinand and Isabella and rode into exile. The silver cross was raised on the Torre de la Vela. Nearly eight centuries after the Muslim conquest, all Spain was Christian, and that same year the queen sent Columbus across the Ocean Sea.
