    The chronicle of these years

    • The Antonine PlagueA.D. 165, Seleucia on the Tigris, Mesopotamia. In the Parthian war of Lucius Verus, Avidius Cassius sacked Seleucia on the Tigris, and the legions carried home a pestilence, probably smallpox, that spread through the empire for twenty years. The physician Galen described its fevers and black pustules; it may have killed a tenth of the people of the empire.
    • The Yellow Turbans RiseA.D. 184, Julu, Hebei, China. Zhang Jue, a faith healer whose Way of Great Peace had won hundreds of thousands of followers, proclaimed that the blue heaven was dead and the yellow heaven would rise in the new cycle. His followers, wearing yellow headcloths, rose in eight provinces at once. The rising was crushed within the year, but the generals who crushed it became the warlords who tore Han apart.
    • Khu Lien Founds LinyiA.D. 192, Xianglin, Vietnam. As the Han dynasty sank into civil war, Khu Lien, son of a local official in the southern district of Xianglin, slew the magistrate and made himself king. His realm, which the Chinese called Linyi, grew into the Cham kingdom known in its own inscriptions as Champa, a seafaring Hindu state that held the coast of central Vietnam for more than twelve centuries.
    • Nagarjuna and the Middle Wayc. A.D. 200, Amaravati, Andhra Pradesh, India. Nagarjuna, a monk of the Andhra country in the days of the later Satavahanas, taught that all things are empty of fixed being, arising only in dependence upon one another. His Madhyamaka, the philosophy of the Middle Way, became a foundation of Mahayana thought in Tibet, China, and Japan. In the same land the great stupa of Amaravati was sheathed in carved limestone slabs.
    • The Pyramid of the Sunc. A.D. 200, Teotihuacan, Mexico. In the northeast of the Valley of Mexico, the builders of Teotihuacan completed the Pyramid of the Sun beside their Avenue of the Dead, in a city laid out on a grid that would house perhaps a hundred thousand people, the greatest of the Americas. Its merchants and soldiers carried the cult of the Feathered Serpent and the storm god as far as the Maya lowlands; its true name and tongue are lost.
