    The chronicle of these years

    • Elissa Founds Carthagec. 814 B.C., Carthage, North Africa. By the tradition the Greeks and Romans told, Elissa, whom Virgil called Dido, fled Tyre after her brother King Pygmalion murdered her husband, and founded Qart-hadasht, the New City, on a peninsula of the Gulf of Tunis. Her colony, placed where the sea lanes of the western Mediterranean narrow, grew into the queen of the Phoenician west.
    • The First Olympiad776 B.C., Olympia, Greece. At the sanctuary of Zeus at Olympia, by the reckoning of later Greek scholars, Koroibos of Elis won the footrace in 776 B.C., the first victor on the lists of the Olympic Games. Every four years the Greeks gathered there under a sacred truce, and they came to count the years of their history in Olympiads.
    • The Fall of Haojing771 B.C., Mount Li, Shaanxi, China. King You of Zhou put aside his queen and heir for the concubine Bao Si, and the queen's father, the Marquis of Shen, called in the Quanrong. They sacked Haojing and slew the king at the foot of Mount Li; no lords came, the tale says, for he had lit the beacons too often in jest. The next year King Ping moved the court east to Luoyi.
    • Romulus Founds Rome753 B.C., The Palatine, Rome. By tradition, on the twenty-first of April, Romulus plows the sacred furrow around the Palatine and kills his brother Remus for leaping the new wall: "So perish whoever else shall leap over my walls" (Livy 1.7). The Romans will count their years from this day, ab urbe condita.
