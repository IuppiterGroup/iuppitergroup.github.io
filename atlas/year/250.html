    The chronicle of these years

    • Ardashir Crowned at CtesiphonA.D. 226, Ctesiphon, Mesopotamia. Ardashir, a prince of Persis, overthrew and killed the last Parthian king, Artabanus IV, on the plain of Hormozgan and was crowned King of Kings of Iran at Ctesiphon. His Sasanian dynasty made Zoroastrianism the faith of the realm, laid claim to the old Achaemenid lands, and fought Rome for four centuries.
    • The Queen of Wa Sends EnvoysA.D. 239, Luoyang, Henan, China. Himiko, a priestess queen who ruled the Wa from her palace at Yamatai, unseen by her people and served by a thousand women, sent envoys by way of Daifang to the Wei court. Wei named her Queen of Wa, Friend of Wei, and sent her a gold seal, a hundred bronze mirrors, and silks. Whether Yamatai lay in Kyushu or in the Yamato plain is still disputed.
    • Persia Takes the Kushan Westc. A.D. 240, Bactra (Balkh), Afghanistan. The Sasanian kings Ardashir I and Shapur I carried Persian arms over the Kushan lands of Bactria and the Kabul valley; Shapur's inscription at Naqsh-e Rustam names Kushanshahr as far as Peshawar among his provinces. Sasanian princes ruled there as Kushanshahs, striking coins in the Kushan manner, while the old Kushan line lingered in the Punjab and at Mathura.
    • Kang Tai and Zhu Ying in Funanc. A.D. 245, Angkor Borei, Cambodia. The Wu kingdom sent the envoys Kang Tai and Zhu Ying to Funan, whose king Fan Xun ruled from a walled city on the lower Mekong. Kang Tai wrote of a people who kept books and archives in a script like that of India, and of the founding legend: the stranger Kaundinya, who came by sea, shot an arrow into the boat of the queen Liuye and took her to wife.
    • Decius Dies at AbritusA.D. 251, Abritus, Moesia. The emperor Decius, who had ordered every citizen to sacrifice to the gods and so begun the first empire-wide persecution of Christians, pursued the Gothic king Cniva into the marshes near Abritus. The Goths cut down the legions in the mud, and Decius and his son Herennius became the first Roman emperors to die in battle against a foreign foe.
    • Valerian Taken by ShapurA.D. 260, Edessa, Mesopotamia. Near Edessa the emperor Valerian, his army weakened by plague, was taken prisoner by Shapur I, the first Roman emperor ever taken captive by a foreign enemy. Shapur carved his triumph in the rock at Naqsh-e Rostam, with one emperor kneeling before his horse and Valerian held captive by the wrist, and Roman prisoners built his dams and cities.
    • Aurelian Takes PalmyraA.D. 272, Palmyra, Syria. Zenobia, widow of Odaenathus, had made Palmyra the seat of an eastern empire reaching from Egypt to Ancyra, proclaiming her son Vaballathus Augustus. Aurelian defeated her armies at Immae and Emesa and besieged Palmyra; the queen fled on a camel toward the Euphrates and was taken. She walked in golden chains in his triumph.
