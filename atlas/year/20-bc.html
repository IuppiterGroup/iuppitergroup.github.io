    The chronicle of these years

    • The Ides of March44 B.C., Curia of Pompey, Rome. Caesar, named dictator for life, was stabbed twenty-three times on the Ides of March at a meeting of the Senate in the hall beside the Theater of Pompey by some sixty conspirators led by Marcus Brutus and Gaius Cassius. He fell at the foot of Pompey's statue. The conspirators cried liberty, but the people mourned Caesar, and Antony turned them against the assassins.
    • The Battles of Philippi42 B.C., Philippi, Macedonia. In two battles on the plain of Philippi in October, Antony and Octavian defeated the armies of the Liberators. Cassius, believing all lost, killed himself after the first; Brutus after the second, saying that he had found no man who was not true to him. With them died the last army of the Republic.
    • Actium31 B.C., Gulf of Ambracia, Greece. Off Actium, Agrippa’s fleet breaks the ships of Antony and Cleopatra. Within a year both lovers are dead by their own hands, Egypt belongs to Rome, and a century of civil war is over. In 27 B.C. the Senate hails Octavian as Augustus.
    • The Death of Cleopatra30 B.C., Alexandria, Egypt. A year after Actium, Octavian entered Alexandria. Antony fell on his sword and died in Cleopatra's arms, and the queen, the last of the Ptolemies, took her own life rather than be led in triumph, by the bite of an asp as the story goes. Caesarion, her son by Caesar, was put to death, and Egypt became the private province of the conqueror.
    • Augustus, First Citizen27 B.C., Rome, Italy. In January Octavian laid down his extraordinary powers before the Senate, which returned to him command of the frontier provinces and their legions and voted him the name Augustus, the revered. Calling himself only princeps, first citizen, he governed for forty years, ended the civil wars, and founded a monarchy that would rule the Mediterranean for five centuries.
    • The Nativity at Bethlehemc. 4 B.C., Bethlehem, Judea. In the last years of King Herod, Jesus was born at Bethlehem, the city of David, to Mary, betrothed to Joseph the carpenter of Nazareth, who had come there for a Roman census. The Gospels tell of the shepherds, the star, and the wise men from the East who came to worship the newborn King of the Jews, and of the flight into Egypt from Herod's wrath.
