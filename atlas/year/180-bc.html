    The chronicle of these years

    • Scipio Defeats Hannibal at Zama202 B.C., Zama, Africa. Publius Cornelius Scipio, who had conquered Spain, carried the war to Africa and allied himself with the Numidian prince Masinissa. At Zama he let Hannibal's elephants pass through lanes in his lines, and the Numidian and Roman horse, returning from the pursuit, fell on the Carthaginian rear. Carthage gave up its fleet and its empire, and Scipio was named Africanus.
    • The Battle of Magnesia190 B.C., Magnesia ad Sipylum, Lydia. Antiochus III the Great, with Hannibal among his counselors, faced the consul Lucius Cornelius Scipio at Magnesia, while his brother Africanus lay sick at Elaea. His scythed chariots panicked, his elephants broke his own phalanx, and the cavalry of Eumenes of Pergamon finished the work. By the Peace of Apamea Antiochus gave up all of Asia Minor beyond the Taurus.
    • Pushyamitra Slays the Last Mauryac. 185 B.C., Pataliputra, Bihar, India. Pushyamitra Shunga, commander of the Mauryan army, struck down King Brihadratha as he reviewed the troops, and took the throne. The empire had already shrunk to the Ganges heartland. Pushyamitra revived the Vedic horse sacrifice, fought off the Greeks from the northwest, and founded the Shunga line, which ruled from Pataliputra and Vidisha for more than a century.
    • Demetrius Crosses into Indiac. 180 B.C., Taxila, Punjab, Pakistan. Demetrius, son of Euthydemus, led the Bactrian Greeks over the Hindu Kush into Arachosia, Gandhara, and the Punjab, opening two centuries of Greek rule in India. His coins show him wearing an elephant scalp in the manner of Alexander. The kingdom he founded passed through many hands, its Greek kings striking coins in Greek and Kharoshthi for their Indian subjects.
    • The Xiongnu Rout the Yuezhi176 B.C., Hexi Corridor, Gansu, China. In a letter to the Han court Modu boasted that his Wise King of the Right had crushed the Yuezhi, masters of the grasslands of Gansu. Some years later the chanyu Laoshang killed their king and made his skull into a drinking cup. The Yuezhi fled west to the Ili and then to Bactria, setting off a chain of migrations felt as far as India.
    • The Battle of Pydna168 B.C., Pydna, Macedonia. Lucius Aemilius Paullus met Perseus, last king of Macedon, at Pydna after an eclipse of the moon the night before had dismayed the Macedonians. On broken ground the phalanx lost its order, and the legionaries cut into its gaps; twenty thousand Macedonians fell. Perseus walked in Paullus's triumph, and the kingdom of Alexander was divided into four republics.
    • The Maccabees Rise167 B.C., Modein, Judea. When Antiochus IV forbade the Jewish law and set up an altar to Olympian Zeus in the Temple, the priest Mattathias killed a royal officer at Modein and fled to the hills with his five sons. His son Judas Maccabeus beat the Seleucid generals and in 164 cleansed and rededicated the Temple, a deliverance still kept at Hanukkah.
    • Dutugemunu Defeats Elarac. 161 B.C., Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka. Dutugemunu, prince of Ruhuna in the south of Lanka, marched north against Elara, a Chola from the mainland who had ruled at Anuradhapura for forty-four years. The chronicle tells that the two kings met in single combat on their elephants beneath the city's walls and Elara fell. Dutugemunu honoured his foe with a monument and raised the great Ruwanwelisaya stupa.
