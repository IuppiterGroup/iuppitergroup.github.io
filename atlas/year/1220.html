    The chronicle of these years

    • Genghis KhanA.D. 1206, The Onon River. At a great assembly by the Onon River, the tribes of the steppe raise up Temujin as Genghis Khan. He has united the Mongols by sword and by oath. Within a lifetime his sons and grandsons will build the largest contiguous land empire the world has ever seen, from Korea to the plains of Russia.
    • Philip Augustus at BouvinesA.D. 1214, Bouvines, Flanders. On 27 July 1214 Philip Augustus met the coalition of the emperor Otto IV, the Count of Flanders, and the English, paid for by King John. The French knights and the militias of the towns broke them; Otto fled, and Count Ferrand was led captive to Paris. The victory secured Philip's conquests, ruined Otto, and sent John home to face his barons.
    • Magna CartaA.D. 1215, Runnymede, England. In the meadow at Runnymede beside the Thames, King John, beaten in France and confronted by rebel barons who held London, set his seal to the Great Charter in June 1215. Annulled within weeks by the pope, it was reissued under his son, and its promise that no free man be imprisoned or dispossessed save by the lawful judgment of his peers or the law of the land became a cornerstone of English liberty.
    • The Mongols Take ZhongduA.D. 1215, Zhongdu, Hebei, China. Genghis Khan had broken the Jin field armies at Badger's Mouth in 1211 and besieged their capital. When Emperor Xuanzong fled south to Kaifeng, the Mongols stormed Zhongdu, sacked it, and left it burning for a month. Among those who passed into Mongol service was the Khitan scholar Yelü Chucai, whose counsel later persuaded the khans to tax the farmers of China rather than slaughter them.
    • The Massacre at OtrarA.D. 1218, Otrar, Transoxiana. A caravan of some four hundred and fifty Muslim merchants sent by Genghis Khan reached Otrar on the Syr Darya in 1218. The governor Inalchuq, kinsman of the Khwarazm-shah Muhammad II, accused them of spying and put them to death, and the shah then slew one of the khan's envoys sent to demand justice. Genghis turned the whole might of the Mongols against Khwarazm.
    • The Hill of Mapungubwec. A.D. 1220, Mapungubwe, South Africa. Where the Limpopo meets the Shashe, a cattle-keeping people grown rich on ivory and gold traded to the coast set their ruler apart on a sandstone hill above his subjects, the first kingdom of southern Africa. In the royal graves lay a little rhinoceros of gold foil over a wooden core. By about 1290 the hill was abandoned, and power passed north to Great Zimbabwe.
    • The Destruction of MervA.D. 1221, Merv, Khorasan. Tolui, youngest son of Genghis Khan, took Merv by false promises in February 1221 and had its people driven onto the plain and divided among his soldiers for slaughter, the artisans alone excepted. The chroniclers counted the dead at seven hundred thousand or more, a number beyond belief, yet the queen of Khorasan's cities was left a desolation. Nishapur suffered the like in April.
    • The Mongols at the KalkaA.D. 1223, Kalka River, Pontic Steppe. A Mongol force under Jebe and Subutai, pursuing the Cumans onto the Pontic steppe, met the princes of Rus' and the Cuman khan Köten. On 31 May 1223 by the river Kalka the allies, divided and quarreling, were destroyed piecemeal. The captured princes were crushed beneath the boards on which the victors feasted. Rus' would hear from the Mongols again.
