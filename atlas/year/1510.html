    The chronicle of these years

    • Michelangelo's Sistine CeilingA.D. 1508, Sistine Chapel, Rome. Two years after laying the foundation stone of a new St. Peter's, Julius II set Michelangelo to paint the vault of the Sistine Chapel. Working through four years, almost alone on his scaffold, the sculptor covered it with the Creation, the Fall, and the Flood, with prophets and sibyls. Unveiled in October 1512, it set a standard for the human figure that no painter could ignore.
    • The Battle of DiuA.D. 1509, Diu, India. Off Diu the viceroy Francisco de Almeida, avenging his son slain by the Mamluk fleet at Chaul the year before, destroyed the combined navies of Mamluk Egypt, the sultan of Gujarat, and the Zamorin of Calicut. Portuguese broadsides decided the day. The victory gave Portugal mastery of the trade of the Indian Ocean for a century.
    • Albuquerque Takes GoaA.D. 1510, Old Goa, India. Afonso de Albuquerque seized Goa from the sultan of Bijapur, lost it, and in November 1510 stormed it again, putting its Muslim defenders to the sword. He made it the capital of the Portuguese Estado da India, encouraged his men to marry local women, and from Goa reached out to seize Malacca and Hormuz. Goa remained Portuguese until 1961.
    • Albuquerque Takes MalaccaA.D. 1511, Malacca, Malaya. Afonso de Albuquerque came with eighteen ships and some twelve hundred men to avenge the Portuguese seized at Malacca two years before. After two assaults on the bridge over the river he took the city from Sultan Mahmud Shah in August 1511 and built the fortress of A Famosa. Portugal now held the gate between the Indian Ocean and the China Sea.
    • The Portuguese Reach the Spice IslandsA.D. 1512, Ternate, Moluccas. Francisco Serrao, shipwrecked on the first Portuguese voyage to the Banda Islands, was welcomed at Ternate by Sultan Bayan Sirrullah, who sought allies against Tidore. The Moluccas grew all the world's cloves, and Serrao's letters to his friend Ferdinand Magellan, telling of islands richer than the Indies, set Magellan dreaming of a westward road to them.
    • Balboa Sights the PacificA.D. 1513, Darién, Panama. Vasco Núñez de Balboa, with some 190 Spaniards and hundreds of native guides and porters, hacked his way over the mountains and through the jungles of the Isthmus of Darién, and on 25 September 1513, from a peak, became the first European to see the Pacific from the shores of the Americas. Four days later he waded into its waters at the Gulf of San Miguel and took possession of it all for Castile.
    • Luther's Ninety-Five ThesesA.D. 1517, Wittenberg, Saxony. On 31 October 1517 Martin Luther, Augustinian friar and professor of theology, sent his ninety-five theses against the sale of indulgences to the Archbishop of Mainz, and by tradition nailed them to the door of the castle church at Wittenberg. Printed and carried across Germany within weeks, they began the Reformation, which divided Western Christendom forever.
    • Charles I Licenses the African Slave TradeA.D. 1518, Santo Domingo, Hispaniola. With the Taíno of the Antilles dying of overwork and disease, Charles I licensed the shipment of four thousand African slaves to the Indies, the first great grant of what became the asiento; soon ships sailed direct from Guinea and São Tomé to the islands. In three and a half centuries some twelve and a half million Africans were carried in chains across the Atlantic, and nearly two million died on the way.
