    The chronicle of these years

    • The Qing Enter LhasaA.D. 1720, Lhasa, Tibet. In 1717 a Dzungar army had stormed Lhasa, killed the Khoshut king Lha-bzang Khan, and plundered the monasteries. Three years later two Qing armies drove them out and escorted the young Seventh Dalai Lama, Kelzang Gyatso, to the Potala. The Kangxi Emperor left a garrison in Lhasa, and from 1727 two imperial residents, the ambans, watched over Tibet for the Qing.
    • The Afghans Take IsfahanA.D. 1722, Isfahan, Persia. Mahmud Hotak with some twenty thousand Afghans routed a far larger Persian army at Gulnabad on 8 March 1722 and laid siege to Isfahan. Through the summer the capital starved; its people ate dogs, rats, and at last the dead. On 23 October Shah Sultan Husayn came out and set the royal plume on Mahmud's turban. The Safavid order was destroyed, and Russia and the Ottomans fell upon Persia's provinces.
    • Dahomey Seizes OuidahA.D. 1727, Ouidah, Benin. King Agaja of Dahomey, having conquered Allada in 1724, fell upon Savi and the port of Ouidah and drove King Huffon of Hueda into the lagoons. Dahomey now held a coast on the Bight of Benin, and its kings, with their famed regiments of women soldiers, became for a century and more great sellers of captives to the Atlantic slave ships.
