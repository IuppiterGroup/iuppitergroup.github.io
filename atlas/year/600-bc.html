    The chronicle of these years

    • Duke Wen of Jin at Chengpu632 B.C., Chengpu, Shandong, China. Duke Wen of Jin, who had wandered nineteen years in exile before winning his throne, met the army of Chu at Chengpu. Keeping an old promise to the King of Chu, he drew back three days' march before giving battle, then broke both Chu wings. The victory made him hegemon of the central states and checked Chu's advance into the north for a generation.
    • Lydia Strikes the First Coinsc. 630 B.C., Sardis, Lydia. The kings of Lydia, rich in the electrum washed down by the river Pactolus, began to stamp lumps of the metal with the head of a lion as a warrant of weight and value. Croesus later struck the first coins of pure gold and silver, and the invention passed quickly to the Greek cities and around the world.
    • The Fall of Nineveh612 B.C., Nineveh, Assyria. The Medes under Cyaxares and the Babylonians under Nabopolassar besieged Nineveh, the greatest city in the world, and stormed it after three months; King Sinsharishkun perished in the sack. The palaces of Sennacherib and Ashurbanipal burned, and the Hebrew prophet Nahum exulted over the bloody city. A remnant of Assyria fled to Harran, which fell in 610.
    • The Battle of Carchemish605 B.C., Carchemish, Syria. The crown prince Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon destroyed the Egyptian army of Necho II and the last of the Assyrians at Carchemish on the Euphrates, pursuing the survivors to Hamath. The victory gave Babylon all of Syria and Palestine, and within weeks Nebuchadnezzar hurried home across the desert to take the throne of his dead father.
    • The Sixteen Great Realmsc. 600 B.C., Kashi (Varanasi), India. Buddhist scripture names sixteen mahajanapadas, great realms, spread across northern India from Gandhara to Anga: kingdoms such as Kosala, Magadha, Avanti, and Vatsa, and republics of clans such as the Vajji league and the Mallas. Walled and moated cities rose along the Ganges, and silver punch-marked coins began to pass from hand to hand between them.
    • Solon Reforms Athens594 B.C., Athens, Greece. Chosen archon to end the strife between rich and poor, Solon cancelled debts, forbade enslavement for debt, and freed Athenians sold abroad. He divided the citizens into classes by wealth, opened the assembly and the courts to all, and replaced the harsh laws of Draco, then left Athens for ten years so that no one could make him change them.
    • Nebuchadnezzar Destroys Jerusalemc. 586 B.C., Jerusalem, Judah. After King Zedekiah rebelled, Nebuchadnezzar II besieged Jerusalem for eighteen months, broke through its walls, and burned the Temple of Solomon. Zedekiah's sons were killed before his eyes, he was blinded and taken to Babylon in fetters, and much of the people of Judah followed him into the Babylonian captivity mourned in the Psalms.
