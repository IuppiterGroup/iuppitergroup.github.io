    The chronicle of these years

    • The Sacred City of Caralc. 2600 B.C., Caral, Peru. In the dry valley of the Supe, some fourteen miles from the Pacific, a people without pottery or writing raised platform mounds and sunken circular plazas, the oldest known city of the Americas, in the age of the Egyptian pyramids. Fed by irrigated fields of squash and beans and by anchovies traded from the coast, they grew cotton for fishing nets and kept their accounts, it seems, in knotted cords.
    • The Great Pyramid Risesc. 2560 B.C., Giza, Egypt. For Pharaoh Khufu, a nation of laborers raises more than two million blocks of stone into a single mountain at Giza. It will stand as the tallest structure made by human hands for nearly four thousand years.
    • The Great Sphinxc. 2540 B.C., Giza, Egypt. Beside the causeway to Khafre's pyramid, a ridge of limestone was carved into a lion with the head of a king, some seventy meters long. Half buried in sand for most of its history, the Great Sphinx was the guardian of the Giza plateau and became the emblem of Egypt itself.
    • The Cities of the Indusc. 2500 B.C., Mohenjo-daro. On the Indus, Mohenjo-daro and Harappa are laid out on grids of baked brick, with covered drains, granaries, and a great public bath. Their merchants trade as far as Mesopotamia, and their writing has never been deciphered.
    • The Sarsens of Stonehengec. 2500 B.C., Stonehenge, Britain. On Salisbury Plain the builders of Stonehenge raised a ring of sarsen stones, each weighing some twenty-five tons, capped with lintels joined like timber, around a horseshoe of great trilithons. Smaller bluestones were dragged from the Preseli hills of Wales, and the axis is set on the midsummer sunrise and midwinter sunset.
    • The Stele of the Vulturesc. 2450 B.C., Girsu, Sumer. Eannatum of Lagash crushed the men of Umma in the war over the Gu-edina, made their ruler swear by the great gods to respect the boundary, and raised a stele of victory. On it the god Ningirsu holds the enemy in a great net while vultures carry off the heads of the slain. Its fragments are in the Louvre.
