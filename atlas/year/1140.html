    The chronicle of these years

    • The Jingkang IncidentA.D. 1127, Kaifeng, Henan, China. The Song had allied with the Jurchen against the Liao, then broken faith with them. The Jin armies besieged Kaifeng twice, and in the spring of 1127 carried off the retired emperor Huizong, painter and collector, his son Emperor Qinzong, and some three thousand of the imperial clan and court to the forests of Manchuria. A surviving prince, Zhao Gou, restored the Song in the south.
    • Roger II Crowned King of SicilyA.D. 1130, Palermo, Sicily. On Christmas Day 1130 Roger II, son of the Norman conqueror of Sicily, was crowned in Palermo king of Sicily, Calabria, and Apulia. From his palace he ruled Greeks, Arabs, Lombards, and Normans with an even hand, and his court, where the geographer al-Idrisi drew his great map of the world, was among the most brilliant of the age.
    • The Battle of QatwanA.D. 1141, Qatwan Steppe, near Samarkand. On the Qatwan steppe north of Samarkand, the heathen Kara-Khitai, Khitan exiles from northern China under their gurkhan Yelü Dashi, destroyed the army of the Seljuk sultan Sanjar in September 1141. Transoxiana passed under infidel overlords. Garbled news of a great king of the East who had smitten the Muslims reached Europe and fed the legend of Prester John.
    • The Death of Yue FeiA.D. 1142, Lin'an, Zhejiang, China. Yue Fei, the general whose back by legend bore the tattooed words Serve the Country with Utmost Loyalty, had driven the Jin from the middle Yangtze and beaten their cavalry at Yancheng. But Emperor Gaozong and his chancellor Qin Hui wanted peace; they recalled him with twelve gold tablets and had him put to death in prison. The Song accepted the Huai River frontier and paid the Jin a yearly tribute.
    • Suger's Choir at Saint-DenisA.D. 1144, Saint-Denis, France. On 11 June 1144 Abbot Suger consecrated the new choir of the royal abbey of Saint-Denis before Louis VII and a crowd of bishops. Its pointed arches and ribbed vaults, and walls opened into windows of colored glass that flooded the sanctuary with light, announced the Gothic style in which the cathedrals of Europe would rise.
    • Zengi Takes EdessaA.D. 1144, Edessa, Mesopotamia. Imad al-Din Zengi, atabeg of Mosul and Aleppo, struck while Count Joscelin II was away and took Edessa, the first of the Crusader states, on Christmas Eve 1144 after a siege of four weeks. The Franks of the town were slain, though the native Christians were for the most part spared. The news shocked Christendom and called forth the Second Crusade.
    • The Almohads Take MarrakeshA.D. 1147, Marrakesh, Morocco. Ibn Tumart, a Masmuda Berber scholar who preached the absolute unity of God and proclaimed himself the Mahdi in the High Atlas, had died about 1130, leaving his disciple Abd al-Mu'min to lead the Almohads. In 1147 Abd al-Mu'min stormed Marrakesh, slaughtered the last of the Almoravid house, and made the city his capital. By 1160 he ruled from the Atlantic to Tripoli.
    • Henry II and the Angevin EmpireA.D. 1154, Westminster, England. On 19 December 1154 Henry of Anjou was crowned at Westminster, ending the long anarchy of Stephen's reign. Through his father he held Anjou and Normandy, and through his wife Eleanor, lately divorced from Louis VII, the vast duchy of Aquitaine. He ruled from the Scottish border to the Pyrenees, a greater domain in France than the French king's own.
