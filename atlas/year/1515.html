    The chronicle of these years

    • Albuquerque Takes MalaccaA.D. 1511, Malacca, Malaya. Afonso de Albuquerque came with eighteen ships and some twelve hundred men to avenge the Portuguese seized at Malacca two years before. After two assaults on the bridge over the river he took the city from Sultan Mahmud Shah in August 1511 and built the fortress of A Famosa. Portugal now held the gate between the Indian Ocean and the China Sea.
    • The Battle of ChaldiranA.D. 1514, Chaldiran, Azerbaijan, Persia. Selim I, having put to death tens of thousands of suspected Qizilbash sympathizers in Anatolia, marched east against Shah Ismail. On 23 August 1514 at Chaldiran his artillery and Janissary muskets broke the charges of the Persian horse, and Selim briefly occupied Tabriz. Ismail, his aura of invincibility gone, never again led an army in person, and eastern Anatolia was won for the Ottomans.
    • The Battle of Marj DabiqA.D. 1516, Marj Dabiq, Syria. North of Aleppo on 24 August 1516 Selim I met the aged Mamluk sultan Qansuh al-Ghawri. The Mamluk horsemen, brave beyond measure but scornful of firearms, were shattered by Ottoman cannon and musketry; al-Ghawri fell from his horse and died on the field. Aleppo, Damascus, and all Syria opened their gates to Selim.
    • The Barbarossa Brothers at AlgiersA.D. 1516, Algiers, Algeria. Oruç and Hızır, sons of a Turkish soldier of Lesbos, had made themselves the scourge of Christian shipping from Tunisian ports. Called to Algiers in 1516 against the Spaniards, Oruç seized the town and killed its emir. After Oruç fell fighting the Spanish in 1518, Hızır, called Hayreddin Barbarossa, placed Algiers under the Ottoman sultan, and for three centuries it was the chief den of the Barbary corsairs.
    • Luther's Ninety-Five ThesesA.D. 1517, Wittenberg, Saxony. On 31 October 1517 Martin Luther, Augustinian friar and professor of theology, sent his ninety-five theses against the sale of indulgences to the Archbishop of Mainz, and by tradition nailed them to the door of the castle church at Wittenberg. Printed and carried across Germany within weeks, they began the Reformation, which divided Western Christendom forever.
    • Charles I Licenses the African Slave TradeA.D. 1518, Santo Domingo, Hispaniola. With the Taíno of the Antilles dying of overwork and disease, Charles I licensed the shipment of four thousand African slaves to the Indies, the first great grant of what became the asiento; soon ships sailed direct from Guinea and São Tomé to the islands. In three and a half centuries some twelve and a half million Africans were carried in chains across the Atlantic, and nearly two million died on the way.
    • Tenochtitlan FallsA.D. 1521, Valley of Mexico. Hernán Cortés, with a few hundred Spaniards and tens of thousands of native allies, takes the Aztec capital of Tenochtitlan after a siege of nearly three months. The Aztec Empire falls, and on its ruins the Spaniards build Mexico City, capital of New Spain.
    • Magellan Falls at MactanA.D. 1521, Mactan, Philippines. Pressing the chiefs of the islands to submit to his ally Humabon, Ferdinand Magellan waded ashore at Mactan on 27 April 1521 with some fifty men against the warriors of Lapulapu, and was cut down in the shallows. Of his five ships only the Victoria, under Juan Sebastian Elcano, came home to Spain in 1522, completing the first voyage around the world.
