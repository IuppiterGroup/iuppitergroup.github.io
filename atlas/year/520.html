    The chronicle of these years

    • The Battle of VouilléA.D. 507, Vouillé, Gaul. Clovis, saying it grieved him that Arians held the best part of Gaul, led the Franks against the Visigoths and killed their king Alaric II with his own hand at Vouillé near Poitiers. The Visigoths lost Aquitaine and Toulouse and kept only Septimania north of the Pyrenees, and the emperor Anastasius sent Clovis the insignia of a consul.
    • Axum Conquers HimyarA.D. 525, Zafar, Arabia. Kaleb, king of Axum, with ships furnished by the emperor Justin, crossed the Red Sea to avenge the martyrs of Najran, defeated and killed Dhu Nuwas, and set a Christian king over Himyar. A few years later the Aksumite general Abraha seized power in Yemen and built a great cathedral at Sana'a to rival the shrine of Mecca.
    • Yashodharman Humbles Mihirakulac. A.D. 528, Mandsaur, Madhya Pradesh, India. The Alchon Huns under Toramana had torn Malwa from the Guptas, and his son Mihirakula, ruling the Punjab from Sagala, was remembered by Buddhists as a destroyer of monasteries. Yashodharman, a king of Malwa, boasted on victory pillars at Mandasor that he had made Mihirakula bow at his feet, a lord whose head had never bent except to Shiva.
    • Justinian's CodeA.D. 529, Constantinople, Thrace. Justinian charged Tribonian and a commission of jurists with gathering the whole of Roman law: the Code of imperial statutes, issued in 529 and revised in 534, the Digest of the writings of the classical jurists, and the Institutes for students. The Corpus Juris Civilis, rediscovered at Bologna in the eleventh century, became the foundation of the law of continental Europe.
    • Benedict Founds Monte Cassinoc. A.D. 529, Monte Cassino, Italy. Benedict of Nursia, who had fled the vices of Rome to live as a hermit at Subiaco, led his monks to the mountain above Cassino, threw down the altar of Apollo there, and built a monastery. His Rule, ordering a life of prayer and work under an abbot, became the pattern of monastic life in the West, and his monks kept learning alive through the dark centuries.
    • The Nika RiotsA.D. 532, Hippodrome, Constantinople. The Blue and Green factions of the Hippodrome united against Justinian's ministers, crying 'Nika,' conquer, burned much of the city and the church of Hagia Sophia, and proclaimed a new emperor. Justinian was ready to flee, but the empress Theodora declared that royalty is a good burial shroud; Belisarius and Mundus trapped the rioters in the Hippodrome and slew some thirty thousand.
    • Belisarius in AfricaA.D. 533, Carthage, Africa. Belisarius lands in Africa with some fifteen thousand men and destroys the Vandal kingdom in a single campaign. Under Justinian, Roman armies go on to win back Italy and the coast of Spain. For a moment the Mediterranean is a Roman sea again.
