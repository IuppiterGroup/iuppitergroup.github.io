    The chronicle of these years

    • The First Battle of PanipatA.D. 1526, Panipat, Haryana, India. Babur came down from Kabul with some twelve thousand men and met Ibrahim Lodi's army, perhaps eight times larger, at Panipat. Lashing carts together to shelter his matchlockmen and field guns, and sweeping round the flanks with mounted archers, he broke the Afghan host before midday. Ibrahim died on the field, and the Mughal Empire was born.
    • The Sack of RomeA.D. 1527, Rome, Italy. On 6 May 1527 the unpaid army of Charles V, Spanish soldiers and Lutheran landsknechts, stormed Rome after its general, the Constable of Bourbon, was killed on the walls. For weeks they murdered, tortured, and plundered, while Pope Clement VII watched from the Castel Sant'Angelo. The sack ended the High Renaissance in Rome and bound the papacy to the emperor.
    • Babur Defeats Rana Sanga at KhanwaA.D. 1527, Khanwa, Rajasthan, India. Rana Sanga of Mewar led a great Rajput confederacy against the newcomer. Before battle at Khanwa, Babur poured out his wine, broke his gold and silver cups, and swore off drink to rally his frightened troops for a holy war. His guns and flanking horse broke the Rajputs; Sanga, wounded, escaped but died the next year, and the Mughals were masters of Hindustan.
    • Demak Overthrows the Last of Majapahitc. A.D. 1527, Daha, Java. The Muslim port kings of the north coast of Java, led by Sultan Trenggana of Demak, crushed the last Hindu-Buddhist kingdom of the Majapahit line at Daha in the Brantas valley. Priests and nobles who would not accept Islam fled east to Blambangan and across the strait to Bali, which alone kept the old faith of the Javanese courts.
    • Fatahillah Takes Sunda KalapaA.D. 1527, Sunda Kalapa, Java. The Hindu king of Sunda had granted the Portuguese leave in 1522 to build a fort at his pepper port of Sunda Kalapa. Before they could return, the Muslim commander Fatahillah, with forces of Demak and Cirebon, took the port, drove off the Portuguese ships, and renamed it Jayakarta, Complete Victory. Jakarta keeps 22 June 1527 as its birthday.
    • Suleiman Before ViennaA.D. 1529, Vienna, Austria. Suleiman marched up the Danube and laid siege to Vienna in September 1529, but the autumn rains had forced him to leave his heavy guns behind. The garrison under Count Niklas Salm beat back every assault, and on 14 October, with winter coming on, the Ottomans burned their camp and withdrew, slaughtering many of their captives. It was the farthest reach of Ottoman conquest for a century and a half.
    • Our Lady of GuadalupeA.D. 1531, Tepeyac, Mexico. In December 1531, on the hill of Tepeyac north of Mexico City, the Virgin Mary appeared to Juan Diego, a Nahua convert, speaking to him in his own tongue and bidding him ask the bishop for a church upon that place. When he opened his cloak before Bishop Juan de Zumárraga, roses fell from it and her image was found upon the cloth; the shrine became the heart of Mexican Catholicism.
    • Pizarro Seizes Atahualpa at CajamarcaA.D. 1532, Cajamarca, Peru. Fresh from his victory over his brother Huascar in a civil war that had torn the Inca empire, Atahualpa met Francisco Pizarro's 168 Spaniards in the square of Cajamarca on 16 November 1532. At a signal the Spaniards fell upon his unarmed retinue with guns, horses, and steel, killing thousands, and took him captive; he filled a room with gold for his ransom and was garroted the following July.
