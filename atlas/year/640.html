    The chronicle of these years

    • Brahmagupta Defines ZeroA.D. 628, Bhinmal, Rajasthan, India. At Bhillamala in Rajasthan the astronomer Brahmagupta completed the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, the first book to treat zero as a number in its own right, with rules for adding, subtracting, and multiplying by it, and for reckoning with negative numbers as debts. Carried to Baghdad and rendered into Arabic, the Indian numerals with their place value and zero passed to Europe as the Arabic figures.
    • The Battle of the YarmoukA.D. 636, Yarmouk River, Syria. In August 636 the great army Heraclius had gathered to recover Syria met the Arabs under Khalid ibn al-Walid and Abu Ubayda in the gorges of the Yarmouk. After six days of battle the imperial host was broken and driven into the ravines. Heraclius left Syria, by tradition with the words "Farewell, Syria, my fair province." Syria was lost to the empire.
    • The Arabs Enter CtesiphonA.D. 637, Ctesiphon, Iraq. Yazdegerd III fled his capital on the Tigris, and Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas entered the White Palace of the Sasanian kings. The spoil was beyond the reckoning of desert men; the great carpet of Khosrow, worked in gold and jewels as a garden in spring, was cut up and shared among the conquerors. The Persians made their next stand at Jalula and lost again.
    • Umar Receives JerusalemA.D. 637, Jerusalem, Palestine. After a long siege the Patriarch Sophronius agreed to surrender the Holy City, but only to the caliph in person. Umar ibn al-Khattab came from Medina, granted the Christians safety for their persons and churches in return for tribute, and by tradition declined to pray in the Church of the Holy Sepulchre lest his followers make it a mosque. Jerusalem passed under Muslim rule for 462 years.
    • Princess Wencheng Goes to TibetA.D. 641, Lhasa, Tibet. Songtsen Gampo, who had united the Tibetan plateau and alarmed the Tang frontier, won a Tang princess as his bride. Princess Wencheng, a kinswoman of Taizong, made the long journey to Lhasa, and Tibetan tradition credits her and the Nepalese princess Bhrikuti with bringing the images of the Buddha honoured in the Jokhang and Ramoche temples.
    • Alexandria Yields to the ArabsA.D. 642, Alexandria, Egypt. By a treaty made with the Patriarch Cyrus, the Roman garrison sailed away from Alexandria in September 642, and the second city of the empire, founded by Alexander nearly a thousand years before, passed to the Arabs. Egypt, the granary of Constantinople, now fed Medina. The Coptic Christians, long at odds with the imperial church, paid the poll tax to new masters.
    • The Victory of VictoriesA.D. 642, Nahavand, Persia. In the Zagros mountains the last great army of the Sasanian kings met the Arabs under al-Nu'man ibn Muqarrin, who fell in the battle as the Persians were routed. The Muslims named it the Victory of Victories, for after Nahavand no Persian host could again take the field, and the provinces of the Iranian plateau fell to the invaders one by one.
    • Narasimhavarman Sacks VatapiA.D. 642, Badami, Karnataka, India. The Pallava king Narasimhavarman I, avenging his father's defeats, sent his army deep into the Deccan with his ally, the Sinhalese prince Manavamma. They stormed the Chalukya capital of Vatapi, and Pulakeshin II perished. Narasimhavarman took the title Vatapikonda, conqueror of Vatapi, and had the victory recorded on a rock at Badami itself.
