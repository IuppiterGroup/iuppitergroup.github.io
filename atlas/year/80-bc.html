    The chronicle of these years

    • Marius Destroys the Teutones102 B.C., Aquae Sextiae, Gaul. The Cimbri and Teutones, Germanic wanderers from the shores of the North Sea, had destroyed Roman armies at Arausio and elsewhere. Gaius Marius, consul again and again, remade the legions, enrolled the landless poor, and made each soldier carry his own pack; at Aquae Sextiae he annihilated the Teutones, and the next year the Cimbri at Vercellae. He was hailed the third founder of Rome.
    • The Social War91 B.C., Corfinium, Italy. When the tribune Marcus Livius Drusus, who proposed to give the Italians citizenship, was murdered, the Marsi, the Samnites, and other allies rose in arms and set up a state of their own, Italia, with its capital at Corfinium. Rome won the war, but only by granting all Italians south of the Po the citizenship it had refused them.
    • The Records of the Grand Historianc. 91 B.C., Chang'an, Shaanxi, China. Castrated for defending the general Li Ling, who had surrendered to the Xiongnu, the court astrologer Sima Qian chose disgrace over suicide so that he might finish the history his father had begun. His Records of the Grand Historian, in 130 chapters, run from the Yellow Emperor to his own day and set the model for every later dynastic history.
    • Sulla Marches on Rome88 B.C., Rome, Italy. When the tribune Sulpicius, at the bidding of Marius, took the command against Mithridates from the consul Lucius Cornelius Sulla, Sulla led his six legions on the city, the first Roman to take Rome by force. In 82, back from the East, he won the battle of the Colline Gate, posted lists of the proscribed, and ruled as dictator.
    • The Pali Canon Written Downc. 80 B.C., Aluvihara, Matale, Sri Lanka. In a time of famine and war, when it was feared that the teaching might perish with the monks who carried it in memory, the monks of Lanka assembled in the rock caves of Aluvihara in the reign of Vattagamani Abhaya and wrote the Buddha's words on palm leaves. The Tipitaka in Pali, the scripture of Theravada Buddhism, was thus first set in writing.
    • Spartacus Breaks Out at Capua73 B.C., Capua, Campania. Seventy-odd gladiators led by Spartacus, a Thracian, broke out of their training school at Capua with kitchen knives and spits, took refuge on Vesuvius, and raised an army of runaway slaves that beat several Roman armies. Marcus Licinius Crassus crushed them in 71, and six thousand captives were crucified along the Appian Way.
    • Pompey Enters the Temple63 B.C., Jerusalem, Judea. Called in to settle the war between the Hasmonean brothers Hyrcanus II and Aristobulus II, Pompey besieged the Temple Mount for three months and took it, entering the Holy of Holies, which he found empty of any image. He left the treasury untouched but made Judea tributary to Rome and set Hyrcanus over it as high priest.
