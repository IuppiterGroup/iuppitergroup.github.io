    The chronicle of these years

    • The Cuban Missile CrisisA.D. 1962, San Cristóbal, Cuba. After the failure of the American-backed invasion at the Bay of Pigs in 1961, Khrushchev secretly sent nuclear missiles to Cuba, and on 14 October 1962 a U-2 photographed their launch sites near San Cristóbal. President Kennedy proclaimed a naval quarantine of the island, and for thirteen days the world stood nearer to nuclear war than ever before or since, until Khrushchev agreed to remove the missiles.
    • The Federation of MalaysiaA.D. 1963, Kuala Lumpur, Malaysia. On 16 September 1963 Malaya joined with Singapore, Sarawak, and North Borneo, now called Sabah, to form Malaysia; Brunei, wary of sharing its oil, stayed out. Sukarno denounced the federation as a British plot and launched the Confrontation, a war of raids across the Borneo frontier fought against British, Gurkha, and Malaysian troops until his fall.
    • The Gulf of TonkinA.D. 1964, Gulf of Tonkin, off Thanh Hoa, Vietnam. On 2 August 1964 North Vietnamese torpedo boats attacked the destroyer USS Maddox in the Gulf of Tonkin; a second attack reported two nights later most likely never took place. Congress nonetheless passed the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution, giving President Johnson power to wage war in Vietnam, and by 1968 more than half a million American troops were in the country.
    • The Secret War in LaosA.D. 1964, Plain of Jars, Laos. From 1964 to 1973, to cut the Ho Chi Minh Trail and support General Vang Pao's Hmong army against the Pathet Lao, American aircraft flew some 580,000 bombing missions over Laos, dropping more than two million tons of bombs. Laos became, for the number of its people, the most heavily bombed country in history, and millions of unexploded bomblets still lie in its fields.
    • The Thirtieth of September MovementA.D. 1965, Lubang Buaya, Jakarta. In the night of 30 September 1965 a band of officers kidnapped and killed six generals and threw their bodies into a well at Lubang Buaya. General Suharto crushed the movement and blamed the Communist Party, and in the months that followed army units and civilian militias slaughtered at least half a million suspected Communists. Suharto took power from Sukarno in March 1966.
    • Singapore Leaves MalaysiaA.D. 1965, Singapore. Race riots and quarrels between Lee Kuan Yew's People's Action Party and the Malay leaders of Kuala Lumpur ended Singapore's place in Malaysia. On 9 August 1965 Lee, near tears on television, announced the island's separation. Without hinterland or water of its own, the city-state made itself under his long rule one of the richest nations on earth.
    • The Cultural RevolutionA.D. 1966, Tiananmen, Peking, China. To destroy his rivals and renew the revolution, Mao Zedong called on the young to rise against the Four Olds and against officials taking the capitalist road. Millions of Red Guards rallied before him in Tiananmen Square, then turned on teachers, writers, and temples; the head of state Liu Shaoqi died in prison. Ten years of terror cost, by most reckonings, more than a million lives.
    • Men on the MoonA.D. 1969, Cape Kennedy, Florida. On 16 July 1969 a Saturn V rocket lifted Apollo 11 from Cape Kennedy, and on 20 July Neil Armstrong and Edwin Aldrin set the lunar module Eagle down on the Sea of Tranquillity while Michael Collins circled above. Armstrong stepped onto the surface with the words 'That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind,' fulfilling the pledge Kennedy had made in 1961.
