    The chronicle of these years

    • The Treaty of ChanyuanA.D. 1005, Chanyuan, Henan, China. When the Liao empress dowager Xiao Chuo and Emperor Shengzong drove deep into Song territory, Emperor Zhenzong was persuaded by his minister Kou Zhun to go to the front at Chanyuan. The armies stood deadlocked and made peace: Song would pay 100,000 taels of silver and 200,000 bolts of silk each year, and the two emperors would call each other brothers. The peace held for over a century.
    • The Tale of Genjic. A.D. 1008, Heian-kyō, Kyoto, Japan. Murasaki Shikibu, a lady-in-waiting to the empress Shōshi, daughter of the all-powerful regent Fujiwara no Michinaga, was at work on a long tale of the loves of Prince Genji, which her diary mentions in 1008. Its fifty-four chapters, rich in poetry and the sadness of passing things, are often called the first novel, and they are the crown of Heian court culture.
    • Al-Hakim Destroys the Holy SepulchreA.D. 1009, Jerusalem, Palestine. The Fatimid caliph al-Hakim, whose edicts against Christians and Jews grew wilder with the years, ordered the Church of the Holy Sepulchre at Jerusalem torn down to its foundations in 1009. The rock-cut tomb of Christ was hacked away nearly to the bedrock. The church was rebuilt by agreement with Constantinople some decades later, but the outrage was long remembered in the West.
    • Ferdowsi Completes the ShahnamehA.D. 1010, Tus, Khorasan. After some thirty years of labor, the poet Abu'l-Qasim Ferdowsi of Tus completed in 1010 the Book of Kings, some fifty thousand couplets of Persian verse telling the history of Iran from the creation of the world to the Arab conquest. He presented it to Mahmud of Ghazni, who paid him poorly. The Shahnameh preserved the Persian tongue and the memory of its heroes for all the centuries after.
    • The Great Temple of ThanjavurA.D. 1010, Thanjavur, Tamil Nadu, India. Rajaraja I dedicated the Brihadishvara temple at Thanjavur in 1010, its granite tower rising sixty-six metres above the shrine, the tallest in India in its day. Its walls carry his inscriptions listing lands, gifts of gold and jewels, and the four hundred temple women assigned to its service. It stands as the noblest monument of the Chola age.
    • The Rising Dragon: Thang LongA.D. 1010, Thang Long, Vietnam. Ly Cong Uan, a palace commander raised in a Buddhist pagoda, founded the Ly dynasty in 1009 and the next year moved the court from cramped Hoa Lu to the old Chinese citadel of Dai La on the Red River. Legend says a golden dragon rose there before his boat, and he named the place Thang Long, the city that is now Hanoi.
    • Basil II Blinds the BulgariansA.D. 1014, Kleidion, Macedonia. On 29 July 1014 Basil II turned the Bulgarian stockade in the pass of Kleidion and captured the army of Tsar Samuel. He had the prisoners blinded, by the chroniclers' count fifteen thousand, leaving one man in each hundred a single eye to lead the rest home. Samuel, the story goes, died of grief at the sight, and by 1018 Bulgaria was an Eastern Roman province.
    • Brian Boru at ClontarfA.D. 1014, Clontarf, Ireland. On Good Friday 1014 Brian Boru, High King of Ireland, met the men of Leinster and the Norse of Dublin with their allies from Orkney and Man. The Norse were driven into the sea, but Brian, past seventy, was cut down in his tent by the fleeing Viking Brodir. The victory broke Norse power in Ireland, but Brian's high kingship died with him.
