    The chronicle of these years

    • Hugh Capet Made KingA.D. 987, Noyon, France. When Louis V, last Carolingian king of the West Franks, died in a hunting fall, the magnates passed over his uncle Charles of Lorraine and chose Hugh Capet, Duke of the Franks. Crowned on 3 July 987, Hugh had his son Robert crowned within the year to secure the succession. His direct heirs ruled France until 1328, and his house until 1848.
    • The Baptism of Rus'A.D. 988, Kiev, Rus'. Vladimir of Kiev, having sent envoys to weigh the faiths of his neighbors, chose the Christianity of Constantinople, whose liturgy in Hagia Sophia had seemed to his men like heaven upon earth. Baptized, he married Anna, sister of Basil II, cast the idol of Perun into the Dnieper, and had the people of Kiev baptized in its waters. The Orthodox civilization of Russia begins here.
    • Rajaraja Takes AnuradhapuraA.D. 993, Anuradhapura, Sri Lanka. Rajaraja Chola sent his fleet across the strait and conquered the north of Lanka, sacking Anuradhapura, which had been the royal city for more than twelve centuries. The Cholas made Polonnaruwa the seat of their new province. In 1017 his son Rajendra completed the conquest and carried King Mahinda V captive to India, where he died.
    • Almanzor Sacks SantiagoA.D. 997, Santiago de Compostela, Galicia. Almanzor, the all-powerful chamberlain of the Córdoban caliph, led one of his fifty-odd raids against the Christian north to the shrine of St. James at Compostela in August 997. The town was razed, though the apostle's tomb was spared. The bells of the church were carried to Córdoba on the shoulders of Christian captives to hang as lamps in the Great Mosque.
    • The Karakhanids Take BukharaA.D. 999, Bukhara, Transoxiana. The Turkish Karakhanids rode down from the steppe and entered Bukhara in 999 unopposed, its clergy having told the people that the quarrel was only between princes. The Samanid house that had ruled Transoxiana for over a century fell, and the land beyond the Oxus passed for good under Turkish lords. South of the river, Mahmud of Ghazni took Khorasan.
    • Stephen Crowned King of HungaryA.D. 1000, Esztergom, Hungary. Vajk, son of the Árpád prince Géza, baptized Stephen, crushed his pagan kinsman Koppány and was crowned at Esztergom at Christmas 1000 with a crown sent, by tradition, by Pope Sylvester II. He founded bishoprics, counties, and royal castles, and the nomad confederation of the Magyars became a Christian kingdom of the Latin West.
    • Leif Erikson in Vinlandc. A.D. 1000, L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland. Leif, son of Erik the Red, sailed west from Greenland and wintered on a coast he called Vinland for the wild grapes his men found there. The sagas tell of later voyages and of fights with the natives, and the Norse turf halls found at L'Anse aux Meadows in Newfoundland bear them out: Europeans had reached America five centuries before Columbus.
    • Voyagers Settle Eastern Polynesiac. A.D. 1000, Raiatea, Society Islands. After two thousand years in Tonga and Samoa, Polynesian navigators sailed east in double-hulled canoes laden with taro, breadfruit, pigs, dogs, and fowls, and found the Society Islands and the Marquesas. Steering by stars, swells, and the flight of birds, their children reached within three centuries the farthest corners of the Polynesian triangle: Hawaii, Rapa Nui, and Aotearoa.
