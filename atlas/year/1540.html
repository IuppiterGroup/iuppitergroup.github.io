    The chronicle of these years

    • Pizarro Seizes Atahualpa at CajamarcaA.D. 1532, Cajamarca, Peru. Fresh from his victory over his brother Huascar in a civil war that had torn the Inca empire, Atahualpa met Francisco Pizarro's 168 Spaniards in the square of Cajamarca on 16 November 1532. At a signal the Spaniards fell upon his unarmed retinue with guns, horses, and steel, killing thousands, and took him captive; he filled a room with gold for his ransom and was garroted the following July.
    • The City of the KingsA.D. 1535, Lima, Peru. Francisco Pizarro founded Lima on the banks of the Rímac on 18 January 1535, naming it the City of the Kings for the Magi of the Epiphany. Seat of the viceroyalty of Peru from 1544, with the university of San Marcos founded in 1551, its cathedral, and its Inquisition, it was for two centuries the capital of all Spanish South America.
    • Tabinshwehti Takes PeguA.D. 1539, Pegu, Burma. Tabinshwehti, the young king of Toungoo, and his general and brother-in-law Bayinnaung overran the Mon kingdom of Hanthawaddy and made Pegu their capital. Tabinshwehti wore his hair in the Mon fashion and courted Mon loyalty, took Martaban with Portuguese gunners in 1541, and by 1544 ruled the Irrawaddy from the sea to Pagan.
    • Sher Shah Drives Out HumayunA.D. 1540, Kannauj, Uttar Pradesh, India. Sher Khan Sur, an Afghan of Bihar, beat Humayun at Chausa in 1539 and again near Kannauj in 1540, driving him into exile in Persia. As Sher Shah he ruled only five years, but he rebuilt the great road from Bengal to the Indus with inns and wells, measured the land for revenue, and struck the silver rupiya that gave India its rupee.
    • Calvin Returns to GenevaA.D. 1541, Geneva, Switzerland. Recalled from exile in Strasbourg in September 1541, the French reformer John Calvin, author of the Institutes of the Christian Religion, made Geneva a city governed by the discipline of its pastors and elders. Its academy trained preachers for France, Scotland, the Netherlands, and Hungary, and Calvinism became the fighting faith of the Reformation.
    • Copernicus Moves the EarthA.D. 1543, Frombork, Royal Prussia. Nicolaus Copernicus, canon of Frauenburg in Royal Prussia, had long worked out a system with the sun at rest near the center and the earth turning about it. His On the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres was printed at Nuremberg in 1543, and by tradition the first copy reached him on his deathbed. The same year Vesalius published his great anatomy of the human body.
    • Gragn Falls at Wayna DagaA.D. 1543, Wayna Daga, Ethiopia. For fourteen years Imam Ahmad ibn Ibrahim al-Ghazi, called Gragn, 'the left-handed,' had led the armies of Adal with Ottoman matchlocks across the Christian highlands, burning churches and monasteries, and forcing Islam upon the conquered. Four hundred Portuguese musketeers under Cristóvão da Gama, Vasco's son, came to Emperor Gelawdewos's aid; Cristóvão was taken and beheaded, but at Wayna Daga in February 1543 Gragn was shot dead and his host scattered.
    • The Silver Mountain of PotosíA.D. 1545, Potosí, Bolivia. An Indian herder named Diego Huallpa found silver on the Cerro Rico, the Rich Mountain, more than four thousand metres up in the Andes, and within a few decades Potosí was one of the largest cities in the world. Worked by Andean laborers drafted under the mita, its mines poured out silver that paid for Spain's wars, flowed through Manila to China, and bound the commerce of the world together.
