    The chronicle of these years

    • Ivan the Terrible Takes KazanA.D. 1552, Kazan, Russia. Ivan IV, crowned in 1547 as the first Tsar of all Russia, laid siege to Kazan with a great army and sappers who mined its walls. On 2 October 1552 the mines blew breaches and the Russians stormed the city, slaughtering its defenders and freeing the Russian captives held there as slaves. Astrakhan fell four years later, the Volga became a Russian river, and St. Basil's rose on Red Square.
    • The Peace of AugsburgA.D. 1555, Augsburg, Germany. Exhausted by war, the princes of the Empire and King Ferdinand, acting for his brother Charles V, agreed at Augsburg on 25 September 1555 that each ruler should choose between the Catholic and the Lutheran faith for his own land, a rule later summed up as cuius regio, eius religio. Calvinists were left outside the peace, and Charles abdicated within the year.
    • The Second Battle of PanipatA.D. 1556, Panipat, Haryana, India. Hemu, a Hindu merchant who had risen to command the armies of the Sur Afghans, took Delhi and crowned himself Raja Vikramaditya. At Panipat he was winning against the Mughals of the fourteen-year-old Akbar when an arrow struck him in the eye. His leaderless army fled; Hemu was brought before Akbar and beheaded, and the Mughal Empire was restored for good.
    • Setthathirath Moves to VientianeA.D. 1560, Vientiane, Laos. Threatened by the armies of Bayinnaung, King Setthathirath moved the capital of Lan Xang down the Mekong to Vientiane, leaving the Phra Bang at the old capital, which took its name as Luang Prabang. At Vientiane he housed the Emerald Buddha, brought from Chiang Mai, and in 1566 raised the great golden stupa of That Luang.
    • The Great Siege of MaltaA.D. 1565, Birgu, Malta. From May to September 1565 Grand Master Jean de Valette, with some six hundred knights and a few thousand Maltese and Spanish soldiers, held the harbor forts of Malta against Suleiman's armada. Fort St. Elmo fell after a month; Birgu and Senglea held through repeated assaults until a Spanish relief force landed and the Turks sailed away. Valletta, the new capital, bears the Grand Master's name.
    • The Battle of TalikotaA.D. 1565, Talikota, Karnataka, India. The sultans of Bijapur, Ahmadnagar, Golconda, and Bidar buried their quarrels and met the army of Vijayanagara under the aged regent Aliya Rama Raya near Talikota. Rama Raya was captured and beheaded, and his host broke. The victors spent months sacking the City of Victory, burning, levering apart, and hacking at its temples and palaces until it was a ruin.
    • Legazpi at CebuA.D. 1565, Cebu, Philippines. Miguel Lopez de Legazpi, sent from New Spain by Philip II, after whom the islands had been named, landed at Cebu and founded the first Spanish town in Asia. A soldier found in a burned hut the image of the Holy Child left by Magellan's men, and the Augustinian Andres de Urdaneta sailed north to find the westerly winds that would carry the galleons home to Acapulco.
