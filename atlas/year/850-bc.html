    The chronicle of these years

    • Lapita Voyagers Reach Tongac. 880 B.C., Nukuleka, Tongatapu, Tonga. Sailing east from Fiji, Lapita canoes reached Tongatapu and founded a village at Nukuleka on the lagoon shore, held to be the first settlement in Polynesia. In Tonga and Samoa the newcomers, cut off from their kin to the west, slowly became the Polynesians, and there they lingered for nearly two thousand years before the great voyages to the east.
    • The Battle of Qarqar853 B.C., Qarqar, Syria. Shalmaneser III met a coalition of twelve kings at Qarqar on the Orontes, led by Hadadezer of Damascus and Irhuleni of Hamath, with Ahab of Israel bringing 2,000 chariots and an Arab chieftain named Gindibu 1,000 camels, the first Arabs named in history. Shalmaneser boasted of a great victory, but the allies held him back from Syria for years.
    • The People Drive Out King Li841 B.C., Haojing, Shaanxi, China. King Li of Zhou, greedy and quick to silence his critics, was driven from Haojing by a rising of the people of the capital and fled to Zhi. For fourteen years the realm was governed under the Gonghe regency, and from this year the Chinese record runs in an unbroken count of years, the oldest certain date in Chinese history.
    • Elissa Founds Carthagec. 814 B.C., Carthage, North Africa. By the tradition the Greeks and Romans told, Elissa, whom Virgil called Dido, fled Tyre after her brother King Pygmalion murdered her husband, and founded Qart-hadasht, the New City, on a peninsula of the Gulf of Tunis. Her colony, placed where the sea lanes of the western Mediterranean narrow, grew into the queen of the Phoenician west.
