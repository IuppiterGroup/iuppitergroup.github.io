    The chronicle of these years

    • The Young Turk RevolutionA.D. 1908, Salonica, Macedonia. Officers of the Committee of Union and Progress in the Third Army in Macedonia, among them Enver Bey, took to the hills in July 1908 and threatened to march on the capital. Abdul Hamid II restored the constitution of 1876 on 24 July, and the empire rejoiced in a brief brotherhood of creeds. When a counter-revolution failed in 1909, the sultan was deposed and sent to Salonica.
    • Oil at Masjed SoleymanA.D. 1908, Masjed Soleyman, Persia. After seven years of fruitless drilling under William Knox D'Arcy's concession, a British crew struck oil at Masjed Soleyman in the Zagros foothills on 26 May 1908, the first great oil field of the Middle East. The Anglo-Persian Oil Company was formed the next year, and in 1914 the British government, turning its navy from coal to oil, bought a controlling share.
    • Belgium Takes the CongoA.D. 1908, Boma, Congo. King Leopold II had ruled the Congo Free State as his private domain since 1885, and his agents and concession companies drove its people to gather wild rubber by terror, with the taking of hostages and the severing of hands. After the reports of Roger Casement and the agitation of E. D. Morel, Belgium annexed the territory in 1908 as the Belgian Congo; the toll in lives had been immense.
    • The Annexation of KoreaA.D. 1910, Seoul, Korea. Having made Korea a protectorate in 1905 and forced Emperor Gojong to abdicate, Japan annexed the country outright, and the last emperor, Sunjong, yielded his throne. For thirty-five years Korea was ruled by Japanese governors-general from Keijō, its land surveyed and taken, its schools taught in Japanese, and its patriots hunted.
    • The Wuchang UprisingA.D. 1911, Wuchang, Hubei, China. A bomb that went off by accident in a revolutionary hideout in Hankou forced the hand of the plotters in the New Army at Wuchang, who rose on the night of 10 October and seized the city. Province after province declared for the revolution, and on 12 February 1912 the six-year-old Xuantong Emperor, Puyi, abdicated. More than two thousand years of imperial rule had ended.
    • The Bogd Khan EnthronedA.D. 1911, Urga, Mongolia. As the Qing fell, the princes of Outer Mongolia declared their independence and enthroned the Jebtsundamba Khutughtu, the highest lama of the Mongols, as Bogd Khan, the Holy Emperor, at Urga. The new state turned to Russia for protection, and China would grant it only autonomy, but Mongolia was never again ruled from Beijing for long.
    • The Fall of Porfirio DíazA.D. 1911, Ciudad Juárez, Mexico. Francisco Madero, a wealthy landowner jailed for daring to run against the aged dictator Porfirio Díaz, called the nation to arms in November 1910. In May 1911 the irregulars of Pascual Orozco and Pancho Villa took Ciudad Juárez, and Díaz, after some thirty-five years in power, sailed for exile; a decade of revolution followed in which perhaps a million Mexicans died.
    • SarajevoA.D. 1914, Sarajevo, Bosnia. On the twenty-eighth of June, Gavrilo Princip shoots Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, in the streets of Sarajevo. Within six weeks the great powers of Europe are at war, and four empires will not survive it.
