    The chronicle of these years

    • Luther's Ninety-Five ThesesA.D. 1517, Wittenberg, Saxony. On 31 October 1517 Martin Luther, Augustinian friar and professor of theology, sent his ninety-five theses against the sale of indulgences to the Archbishop of Mainz, and by tradition nailed them to the door of the castle church at Wittenberg. Printed and carried across Germany within weeks, they began the Reformation, which divided Western Christendom forever.
    • Charles I Licenses the African Slave TradeA.D. 1518, Santo Domingo, Hispaniola. With the Taíno of the Antilles dying of overwork and disease, Charles I licensed the shipment of four thousand African slaves to the Indies, the first great grant of what became the asiento; soon ships sailed direct from Guinea and São Tomé to the islands. The slaves were made in Africa: taken in the wars and raids of African kings and merchants and sold by them at the coast, as captives had been sold north across the Sahara to the Muslim world since the seventh century. In three and a half centuries some twelve and a half million were carried in chains across the Atlantic, and nearly two million died on the way; the older Muslim trade, which outlasted it, took more than seven million across the Sahara alone, and millions more by the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean.
    • Cortés Meets MoctezumaA.D. 1519, Tenochtitlan, Mexico. Hernán Cortés, who had landed on the Gulf coast with some five hundred soldiers, scuttled his ships, and won the Tlaxcalans to his side, came over the southern causeway into Tenochtitlan on 8 November 1519. Moctezuma II received him with gifts and lodged him in the palace of his father Axayacatl; within a week Cortés had made the emperor his prisoner.
    • Magellan's StraitA.D. 1520, Strait of Magellan, Chile. Ferdinand Magellan, a Portuguese sailing for Spain, wintered on the Patagonian coast, crushed a mutiny there, and on 21 October 1520 entered the strait that bears his name. After thirty-eight days among its fjords and squalls three of his ships came out into an ocean so calm he called it the Pacific; one of them, the Victoria, completed the first voyage around the world in 1522.
    • Tenochtitlan FallsA.D. 1521, Valley of Mexico. Hernán Cortés, with a few hundred Spaniards and tens of thousands of native allies, takes the Aztec capital of Tenochtitlan after a siege of nearly three months. The Aztec Empire falls, and on its ruins the Spaniards build Mexico City, capital of New Spain.
    • Magellan Falls at MactanA.D. 1521, Mactan, Philippines. Pressing the chiefs of the islands to submit to his ally Humabon, Ferdinand Magellan waded ashore at Mactan on 27 April 1521 with some fifty men against the warriors of Lapulapu, and was cut down in the shallows. Of his five ships only the Victoria, under Juan Sebastian Elcano, came home to Spain in 1522, completing the first voyage around the world.
    • Luther Before the Diet of WormsA.D. 1521, Worms, Germany. Summoned before the young emperor Charles V and the princes of the Empire, Luther refused on 18 April 1521 to recant his writings unless convinced by Scripture and plain reason, and is said to have ended, 'Here I stand; I can do no other.' Outlawed by the Edict of Worms, he was spirited away by his prince to the Wartburg, where he turned the New Testament into German.
    • The First Battle of PanipatA.D. 1526, Panipat, Haryana, India. Babur came down from Kabul with some twelve thousand men and met Ibrahim Lodi's army, perhaps eight times larger, at Panipat. Lashing carts together to shelter his matchlockmen and field guns, and sweeping round the flanks with mounted archers, he broke the Afghan host before midday. Ibrahim died on the field, and the Mughal Empire was born.
