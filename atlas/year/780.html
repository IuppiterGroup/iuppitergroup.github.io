    The chronicle of these years

    • The Rearguard at RoncevauxA.D. 778, Roncevaux Pass, Navarre. Returning from a fruitless campaign before Zaragoza, Charlemagne razed the walls of Pamplona. On 15 August 778 Basques fell upon his rearguard in the Pyrenean passes and slew it to a man, among them Roland, prefect of the Breton March. Three centuries later the disaster was sung, with Saracens in place of Basques, as the Song of Roland.
    • The Founding of Samyec. A.D. 779, Samye, Tibet. King Trisong Detsen, with the Indian masters Śāntarakṣita and Padmasambhava, completed Samye, the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet, laid out as a mandala of the universe, and saw the first Tibetans ordained as monks. A great debate held there between Indian and Chinese teachers ended, by Tibetan tradition, in favour of the Indian school.
    • The Second Council of NicaeaA.D. 787, Nicaea, Bithynia. Summoned by the Empress Irene in the name of her young son Constantine VI, some three hundred bishops met in the church of Hagia Sophia at Nicaea and restored the veneration of icons, distinguishing the honor paid to holy images from the worship due to God alone. It was the seventh and last council that the Orthodox and Catholic Churches both hold ecumenical.
    • The Northmen Sack LindisfarneA.D. 793, Lindisfarne, England. On 8 June 793 heathen raiders from the sea fell on Lindisfarne, the holy island of Aidan and Cuthbert, slew monks, drowned others, and carried off the treasures of the church. 'Never before has such terror appeared in Britain,' wrote Alcuin from the court of Charlemagne. The Viking Age had begun.
    • The Capital of PeaceA.D. 794, Heian-kyō, Kyoto, Japan. Emperor Kanmu, leaving behind the overbearing temples of Nara and an ill-omened capital at Nagaoka, founded Heian-kyō, the Capital of Peace and Tranquillity, on a grid of streets copied from Chang'an. It remained the seat of the emperors for more than a thousand years, until the court moved to Tokyo in 1869.
