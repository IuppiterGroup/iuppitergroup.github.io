    The chronicle of these years

    • Tahrir SquareA.D. 2011, Cairo, Egypt. Inspired by Tunisia, crowds filled Cairo's Tahrir Square on 25 January 2011 and held it for eighteen days against police, hired thugs on camels, and the regime's propaganda. On 11 February Hosni Mubarak resigned after thirty years. The Muslim Brotherhood's Mohamed Morsi won the presidency in 2012, but in July 2013 the army under Abdel Fattah el-Sisi removed him and killed hundreds of his supporters at the Rabaa sit-in.
    • The Revolution of DignityA.D. 2014, Independence Square, Kyiv. When President Yanukovych, under pressure from Moscow, refused in November 2013 to sign an association agreement with the European Union, protesters occupied Independence Square in Kyiv for three winter months. Between 18 and 20 February 2014 snipers and riot police killed about a hundred of them, remembered as the Heavenly Hundred; Yanukovych fled the capital on the night of the 21st, and on 22 February parliament removed him.
    • Russia Seizes the CrimeaA.D. 2014, Simferopol, Crimea. Days after Yanukovych's fall, Russian soldiers without insignia seized the Crimean parliament and the airports of the peninsula. A referendum held under occupation on 16 March 2014 was followed on the 18th by Russia's annexation of the Crimea, which the United Nations General Assembly declared invalid. In April Russian-led militias seized towns in the Donbas, and war began in eastern Ukraine.
    • The Islamic State Takes MosulA.D. 2014, Mosul, Iraq. In June 2014 a few thousand fighters of the Islamic State of Iraq and Syria routed the Iraqi army and seized Mosul. From the pulpit of its Great Mosque Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi proclaimed himself caliph. Mosul's Christians, their houses marked with the Arabic letter nun, were given the choice of conversion, tribute, or the sword and fled, leaving the city without a Christian community for the first time in many centuries.
    • War in YemenA.D. 2015, Sana'a, Yemen. The Houthis, a Zaydi revivalist movement from the mountains of Sa'da, seized Sana'a in September 2014 and drove President Abd Rabbuh Mansur Hadi south to Aden. On 26 March 2015 Saudi Arabia and its allies began a campaign of air strikes and blockade on his behalf. The war that followed brought famine and cholera to the poorest country in Arabia.
    • Britain Votes to LeaveA.D. 2016, Westminster, London. In a referendum called by David Cameron on 23 June 2016, the British people voted by 51.9 percent to 48.1 percent to leave the European Union they had joined in 1973. Cameron resigned; after three years of parliamentary deadlock under Theresa May, Boris Johnson won an election on the promise to get Brexit done, and the United Kingdom left on 31 January 2020.
    • The Rohingya ExodusA.D. 2017, Maungdaw, Rakhine. After Rohingya militants attacked police posts in northern Rakhine on 25 August 2017, the Burmese army burned hundreds of villages of the Muslim Rohingya, whom the state had long denied citizenship. More than seven hundred thousand fled across the Naf River into Bangladesh, and investigators of the United Nations accused the generals of genocidal intent.
    • Contagion at WuhanA.D. 2019, Wuhan, Hubei, China. In December doctors in Wuhan began to see a cluster of pneumonia cases caused by a new coronavirus, and the authorities silenced those who raised the alarm. In January 2020 the city of eleven million was sealed off, but the disease had already spread. The COVID-19 pandemic killed some seven million people by official counts across the world and halted much of its economy.
