    The chronicle of these years

    • Thera Eruptsc. 1600 B.C., Akrotiri, Thera. The volcano of Thera blew apart in one of the greatest eruptions of human history, burying the Minoan town of Akrotiri under ash and pumice and casting tephra across the Aegean and beyond. Its people had fled in time; their houses, with frescoes of boxing boys, swallows, and fleets, lay beneath the ash until Spyridon Marinatos began to excavate them in 1967.
    • Mursili I Sacks Babylonc. 1595 B.C., Babylon, Mesopotamia. The Hittite king Mursili I marched down the Euphrates, took Babylon, and ended the dynasty of Hammurabi under its last king, Samsu-ditana. He carried off the statue of Marduk and went home with his plunder, leaving the land to the Kassites, who would rule Babylonia for more than four centuries.
    • Ahmose Drives Out the Hyksosc. 1540 B.C., Avaris, Egypt. Seqenenre Tao of Thebes died in battle with the Hyksos, his skull split by their axes, and Kamose carried the war north to the walls of Avaris. It was Ahmose, his successor, who took the city, pursued the enemy into Canaan, and besieged Sharuhen for three years, founding the Eighteenth Dynasty and the New Kingdom.
    • The Hymns of the Rigvedac. 1500 B.C., The Punjab, Pakistan. Herding tribes speaking an Indo-Aryan tongue came over the passes into the land of seven rivers and there composed the Rigveda: 1,028 hymns to Indra, Agni, Varuna, and Soma, in a Sanskrit kin to Greek and Latin. Learned by heart and handed down by priestly families with flawless exactness for three thousand years, the hymns are the oldest scripture still in use.
    • Hatshepsut Takes the Double Crown1473 B.C., Deir el-Bahari, Egypt. Hatshepsut, widow of Thutmose II and regent for the young Thutmose III, had herself crowned king and ruled some fifteen years. She built the terraced temple of Deir el-Bahari beneath the cliffs of Thebes and sent a fleet to Punt for incense trees, carved on its walls.
    • Thutmose III at Megiddoc. 1457 B.C., Megiddo, Canaan. Against the advice of his officers, Thutmose III led his army in single file through the narrow Aruna pass and fell on the coalition of the king of Kadesh before Megiddo. The enemy fled into the city, hauled up over the walls by their clothing, and surrendered after a siege of seven months. It is the first battle recorded in detail.
    • The Mycenaeans Take Knossosc. 1450 B.C., Knossos, Crete. The palaces of Crete burned, and Greek-speaking lords from the mainland took Knossos. Their scribes adapted the Minoan script into Linear B to write early Greek, a script Michael Ventris deciphered in 1952. From this time the Aegean belonged to the Mycenaeans.
    • The Exodus and the Law at Sinaic. 1446 B.C., Mount Sinai. Four hundred and eighty years before Solomon began the Temple, by the reckoning of the First Book of Kings, Moses led the children of Israel out of Egypt after the plagues and the first Passover. They crossed the sea on dry ground, and at Mount Sinai God gave them the Law and the Ten Commandments.
