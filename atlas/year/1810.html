    The chronicle of these years

    • Two Emperors on a Raft at TilsitA.D. 1807, Tilsit, East Prussia. After beating Bennigsen's Russians at Friedland on 14 June 1807, Napoleon met Alexander I on 25 June on a raft moored in the Niemen at Tilsit. The treaties that followed bound Russia to join his blockade of British trade and stripped Prussia of half its lands, from which he carved the Kingdom of Westphalia for his brother Jérôme and the Duchy of Warsaw.
    • Parliament Abolishes the Slave TradeA.D. 1807, Westminster, London. On 25 March 1807, after twenty years of agitation by William Wilberforce, Thomas Clarkson, and the evangelicals of Clapham, the Act for the Abolition of the Slave Trade received the royal assent, forbidding British subjects to carry slaves. The Royal Navy's West Africa Squadron, formed the next year, spent half a century hunting slavers and freed about 150,000 Africans at sea, while the kings of Asante and Dahomey, great sellers of captives, pressed to keep the trade open.
    • The Second of May in MadridA.D. 1808, Madrid, Spain. When Murat's troops moved to carry the last of the Spanish royal family off to Bayonne, the people of Madrid rose on 2 May 1808 and fought the Mamelukes and grenadiers in the Puerta del Sol. Murat had prisoners shot through the night, as Goya later painted. Napoleon made his brother Joseph King of Spain, and the Peninsular War began.
    • The Portuguese Court Comes to RioA.D. 1808, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. As Junot's French army closed on Lisbon in November 1807, the Prince Regent John, his mad mother Queen Maria, and some ten thousand courtiers sailed for Brazil under British escort, reaching Rio de Janeiro in March 1808. Rio became the capital of the Portuguese monarchy, the ports of Brazil were opened to the world, and in 1815 Brazil was raised to a kingdom equal with Portugal.
    • The Cry of DoloresA.D. 1810, Dolores Hidalgo, Mexico. At dawn on 16 September 1810 the parish priest Miguel Hidalgo y Costilla rang the bell of his church at Dolores and called his Indian and mestizo parishioners to rise against the Spanish-born rulers of New Spain, beneath the banner of Our Lady of Guadalupe. His host of tens of thousands sacked Guanajuato but was routed at the Bridge of Calderón; Hidalgo was captured and shot in 1811.
    • The British Conquer JavaA.D. 1811, Meester Cornelis, Java. With Holland annexed to Napoleon's empire, an army from British India under Sir Samuel Auchmuty landed near Batavia and stormed the Franco-Dutch lines at Meester Cornelis in August 1811. Thomas Stamford Raffles ruled Java as lieutenant-governor for five years, sacked the court of Yogyakarta, and began a land tax, before the island was handed back to the Netherlands in 1816.
    • BorodinoA.D. 1812, Borodino, Russia. Some seventy-five miles west of Moscow on 7 September 1812, Field Marshal Kutuzov stood to fight, and the Grande Armée stormed the Raevsky redoubt and the Bagration flèches in the bloodiest single day of the age, some seventy thousand men falling. Bagration was mortally wounded. Napoleon would not commit his Guard; Kutuzov withdrew in good order, and the French entered an abandoned Moscow a week later.
    • The Battle of the NationsA.D. 1813, Leipzig, Saxony. For four days in October 1813 the armies of Russia, Prussia, Austria, and Sweden closed upon Napoleon around Leipzig in the greatest battle Europe had yet seen, more than half a million men in all. The Saxons went over to the allies in the field; on 19 October a sapper blew the Elster bridge too soon, drowning Marshal Poniatowski and trapping the rearguard. Napoleon's German empire collapsed.
