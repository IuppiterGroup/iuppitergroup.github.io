    The chronicle of these years

    • Ibn Saud Takes RiyadhA.D. 1902, Riyadh, Najd. Abd al-Aziz ibn Saud, a young exile living with his family in Kuwait, rode into Najd with some forty companions. Before dawn on 15 January 1902 his men surprised the Rashidi governor Ajlan outside the Masmak fort in Riyadh and killed him. The town rallied to the house of Saud, and from it Ibn Saud built the kingdom that would bear his family's name.
    • The First Flight at Kitty HawkA.D. 1903, Kitty Hawk, North Carolina. On the windswept dunes of Kill Devil Hills near Kitty Hawk, on 17 December 1903, Orville Wright lifted the brothers' gasoline-engined Flyer into the air for twelve seconds and 120 feet, the first controlled and sustained flight of a powered machine heavier than air. Wilbur's fourth flight that day lasted fifty-nine seconds; within forty years the airplane had transformed war, commerce, and travel.
    • The Entente CordialeA.D. 1904, Foreign Office, London. Six years after their confrontation at Fashoda, Britain and France signed on 8 April 1904 a set of agreements settling their colonial quarrels: France recognized British control of Egypt, and Britain gave France a free hand in Morocco, with lesser bargains over Newfoundland, Siam, and the New Hebrides. Lansdowne and Paul Cambon had ended centuries of rivalry, and the entente hardened against Germany.
    • The Herero WarA.D. 1904, Waterberg, Namibia. The Herero of German South West Africa, stripped of land and cattle by settlers, rose under Samuel Maharero in January 1904. After the battle at the Waterberg in August, General Lothar von Trotha drove the survivors into the waterless Omaheke and decreed that every Herero found within German borders be shot; with the Nama rising that followed, perhaps four in five of the Herero perished.
    • The Battle of TsushimaA.D. 1905, Tsushima Strait, Japan. After a voyage of eighteen thousand miles from the Baltic, the Russian fleet of Admiral Rozhestvensky met Admiral Tōgō Heihachirō in the Tsushima Strait. In two days the Japanese sank or captured nearly all of it, losing only three torpedo boats. With Port Arthur fallen and Mukden lost, Russia made peace, and for the first time in modern history an Asian power had beaten a European one.
    • Bloody SundayA.D. 1905, Winter Palace, St. Petersburg. On Sunday, 22 January 1905, the priest Georgy Gapon led tens of thousands of workers carrying icons and portraits of the Tsar toward the Winter Palace with a petition for an eight-hour day and a constituent assembly. Troops fired on the columns at several points, and well over a hundred were killed. The revolution of 1905 followed, ended only by the October Manifesto and a Duma.
    • Einstein's Wonderful YearA.D. 1905, Bern, Switzerland. In 1905 a twenty-six-year-old technical examiner at the Swiss patent office in Bern, Albert Einstein, published four papers in Annalen der Physik: on light as quanta, on the motion of particles suspended in liquid, on the electrodynamics of moving bodies, which set out special relativity, and on the equivalence of mass and energy. Physics was not the same again.
    • The Persian Constitutional RevolutionA.D. 1906, Tehran, Persia. In the summer of 1906 some fourteen thousand merchants, clerics, and craftsmen took sanctuary in the gardens of the British legation at Tehran, demanding a house of justice. The ailing Mozaffar al-Din Shah yielded; the first Majlis met in October and drew up a constitution, which he signed just before his death. His son Mohammad Ali Shah shelled the Majlis in 1908 but was deposed the next year.
