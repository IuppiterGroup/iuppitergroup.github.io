    The chronicle of these years

    • Abram Goes Out to Canaanc. 1900 B.C., Shechem, Canaan. At the word of the Lord, Abram left Haran with Sarai his wife and Lot his nephew and came into the land of Canaan, to the oak of Moreh at Shechem, where God promised the land to his offspring and Abram built an altar. Jews, Christians, and Muslims alike call him father in faith.
    • Sumu-abum Founds the Dynasty of Babylon1894 B.C., Babylon. Sumu-abum, an Amorite chieftain, made the small town of Babylon on the Euphrates the seat of his house. His line would hold it for three centuries, and under his descendant Hammurabi the obscure town became the capital of Mesopotamia and the holy city of Marduk.
    • Senusret III Sets the Border at Semnac. 1862 B.C., Semna, Nubia. Senusret III fixed Egypt's southern frontier at Semna, above the second cataract, with fortresses on both banks and a boundary stele forbidding any Nubian to pass north except to trade. A later stele mocks the Nubians as cowards and warns his sons to hold the line.
    • The First Alphabetc. 1850 B.C., Serabit el-Khadim, Sinai. Canaanite workers at the Egyptian turquoise mines of Serabit el-Khadim wrote their own tongue with a few dozen signs borrowed from hieroglyphs, each standing for the first sound of the word it pictured. From this alphabet came the Phoenician, Hebrew, Greek, Latin, and Arabic letters.
    • Shamshi-Adad, King of the Universe1808 B.C., Shubat-Enlil, Upper Mesopotamia. Shamshi-Adad, an Amorite adventurer, seized Ashur and built a kingdom across upper Mesopotamia, ruling from Shubat-Enlil while his sons held Ekallatum and Mari. He took the title king of the universe, and his letters scold the idle younger son at Mari like any exasperated father.
    • Hammurabi Conquers Larsa1763 B.C., Larsa, Sumer. After a long siege Hammurabi of Babylon took Larsa and the aged Rim-Sin, and with it all of Sumer. Within two years Eshnunna and Mari had fallen too, and the Amorite king of a small city ruled from the Gulf to the middle Euphrates.
    • The Laws of Hammurabic. 1754 B.C., Babylon. Hammurabi of Babylon, now master of Mesopotamia, has nearly three hundred laws cut into a stele of black stone for all to see: an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth. At its top the king stands before Shamash, the sun god who gave him the right to judge.
    • The Palaces of Erlitouc. 1750 B.C., Yanshi, Henan, China. On the Luo River near Luoyang rose the first palace city of China, with halls of rammed earth inside a walled enclosure, a bronze foundry, and a turquoise workshop. Here were cast the earliest ritual bronze vessels, and here a dragon of some two thousand turquoise pieces lay upon a buried lord. Many scholars take it for a capital of the Xia.
