    The chronicle of these years

    • Hammurabi Conquers Larsa1763 B.C., Larsa, Sumer. After a long siege Hammurabi of Babylon took Larsa and the aged Rim-Sin, and with it all of Sumer. Within two years Eshnunna and Mari had fallen too, and the Amorite king of a small city ruled from the Gulf to the middle Euphrates.
    • The Laws of Hammurabic. 1754 B.C., Babylon. Hammurabi of Babylon, now master of Mesopotamia, has nearly three hundred laws cut into a stele of black stone for all to see: an eye for an eye, a tooth for a tooth. At its top the king stands before Shamash, the sun god who gave him the right to judge.
    • The Palaces of Erlitouc. 1750 B.C., Yanshi, Henan, China. On the Luo River near Luoyang rose the first palace city of China, with halls of rammed earth inside a walled enclosure, a bronze foundry, and a turquoise workshop. Here were cast the earliest ritual bronze vessels, and here a dragon of some two thousand turquoise pieces lay upon a buried lord. Many scholars take it for a capital of the Xia.
    • The Hyksos Rule from Avarisc. 1650 B.C., Avaris, Egypt. West Semitic rulers from Canaan, whom the Egyptians called heqau khasut, rulers of foreign lands, took power in the eastern Delta and made Avaris their capital. Their Fifteenth Dynasty held Lower Egypt for a century and took tribute from Thebes, and in their time the horse-drawn chariot and the composite bow came to the Nile.
    • Hattusili Makes Hattusa His Capitalc. 1650 B.C., Hattusa, Anatolia. Labarna, who took the name Hattusili, rebuilt Hattusa in defiance of Anitta's curse and made it the seat of the Hittite kingdom. He led his chariots over the Taurus against Yamhad and the cities of Syria, and his grandson Mursili I would carry the war to Babylon.
    • Thera Eruptsc. 1600 B.C., Akrotiri, Thera. The volcano of Thera blew apart in one of the greatest eruptions of human history, burying the Minoan town of Akrotiri under ash and pumice and casting tephra across the Aegean and beyond. Its people had fled in time; their houses, with frescoes of boxing boys, swallows, and fleets, lay beneath the ash until Spyridon Marinatos began to excavate them in 1967.
    • Tang Overthrows the Xiac. 1600 B.C., Yanshi, Henan, China. Tradition holds that Tang, lord of Shang, rose against Jie, the last and most wicked king of Xia, routed him at Mingtiao, and founded a house that ruled the Yellow River plain for more than five centuries. A walled Shang city built soon afterward stands only a few kilometres from the old palaces of Erlitou.
    • The Shaft Graves of Mycenaec. 1600 B.C., Mycenae, Greece. Within a ring of upright slabs on the hill of Mycenae, warrior lords were buried with gold death masks, inlaid daggers, and cups of gold. Heinrich Schliemann opened them in 1876 and believed he had found Agamemnon; they were three centuries older, the first great lords of Mycenaean Greece.
