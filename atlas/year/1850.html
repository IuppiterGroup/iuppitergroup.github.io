    The chronicle of these years

    • Liberia Declares Its IndependenceA.D. 1847, Monrovia, Liberia. Founded in 1822 by the American Colonization Society as a home for freed slaves from the United States, the settlement at Cape Mesurado had grown into a string of colonies along the Pepper Coast. On 26 July 1847 its settlers proclaimed the Republic of Liberia, with a constitution modeled on the American, and chose the Virginia-born Joseph Jenkins Roberts as first president.
    • The Springtime of the PeoplesA.D. 1848, Boulevard des Capucines, Paris. When the government banned a reform banquet, Paris rose on 22 February 1848; troops fired on a crowd on the Boulevard des Capucines, and within two days Louis Philippe had abdicated and a provisional government, with the poet Lamartine as its leading voice, proclaimed the Second Republic. The news ran across Europe like fire: within a month Vienna, Berlin, Milan, Venice, and Pest were in revolution.
    • The Frankfurt ParliamentA.D. 1848, St. Paul's Church, Frankfurt. Elected across the German Confederation, more than five hundred deputies, most of them professors, lawyers, and officials, filed into St. Paul's Church in Frankfurt on 18 May 1848 to make a united Germany. They wrote fundamental rights and a constitution, and in March 1849 elected Frederick William IV of Prussia emperor; he refused a crown from the gutter, and the rump of the Parliament was dispersed by soldiers at Stuttgart in June.
    • The Communist ManifestoA.D. 1848, Bishopsgate, London. Commissioned by the Communist League at its congress in London, the Manifesto of the Communist Party by Karl Marx and Friedrich Engels was printed in German in Bishopsgate in February 1848, days before the Paris rising. Its twenty-three pages declared all history the history of class struggles and ended in a call to the workers of all countries to unite.
    • Gold at Sutter's MillA.D. 1848, Coloma, California. On 24 January 1848 James Marshall, building a sawmill for John Sutter on the American River, found flakes of gold in the tailrace. By the end of 1849 some ninety thousand forty-niners had poured into California by sea and overland, San Francisco had grown from a village to a city, and California entered the Union as a free state in 1850.
    • The Surrender at VilágosA.D. 1849, Világos (Șiria), Romania. Hungary declared its independence at Debrecen in April 1849, and Kossuth's honvéd armies drove the Austrians from most of the kingdom, until Nicholas I sent some 200,000 Russians over the Carpathians to aid Francis Joseph. On 13 August General Görgei laid down his arms to the Russians at Világos; in October thirteen Hungarian commanders, twelve of them generals, were hanged or shot at Arad, and Kossuth fled to Turkey.
    • The Punjab AnnexedA.D. 1849, Gujrat, Punjab, Pakistan. Ten years after Ranjit Singh's death his kingdom was torn by palace murders and two wars with the British. After the bloody drawn battle of Chillianwala, Lord Gough crushed the Sikh army at Gujrat in February 1849, and Lord Dalhousie annexed the Punjab. The boy Maharaja Duleep Singh gave up the Koh-i-Noor, which was sent to Queen Victoria.
    • The Coup of the Second of DecemberA.D. 1851, Paris, France. Barred by the constitution from a second term, President Louis-Napoléon Bonaparte struck on 2 December 1851, the anniversary of Austerlitz: troops occupied Paris, the leading deputies were arrested, and resistance on the boulevards was shot down; Victor Hugo, who had tried to raise the barricades, fled into exile. A plebiscite approved the coup, and a year later to the day he was proclaimed Napoleon III.
