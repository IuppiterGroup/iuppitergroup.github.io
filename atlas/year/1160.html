    The chronicle of these years

    • The Almohads Take MarrakeshA.D. 1147, Marrakesh, Morocco. Ibn Tumart, a Masmuda Berber scholar who preached the absolute unity of God and proclaimed himself the Mahdi in the High Atlas, had died about 1130, leaving his disciple Abd al-Mu'min to lead the Almohads. In 1147 Abd al-Mu'min stormed Marrakesh, slaughtered the last of the Almoravid house, and made the city his capital. By 1160 he ruled from the Atlantic to Tripoli.
    • Henry II and the Angevin EmpireA.D. 1154, Westminster, England. On 19 December 1154 Henry of Anjou was crowned at Westminster, ending the long anarchy of Stephen's reign. Through his father he held Anjou and Normandy, and through his wife Eleanor, lately divorced from Louis VII, the vast duchy of Aquitaine. He ruled from the Scottish border to the Pyrenees, a greater domain in France than the French king's own.
    • Henry II Lands in IrelandA.D. 1171, Waterford, Ireland. Cambro-Norman knights called in by Diarmait Mac Murchada, the exiled king of Leinster, had taken Wexford, Waterford, and Dublin, and their leader Strongbow had married Diarmait's daughter. Lest they found a rival kingdom, Henry II landed at Waterford in October 1171 with a great army and received the submission of most of the Irish kings: the beginning of English lordship in Ireland.
    • Saladin Ends the Fatimid CaliphateA.D. 1171, Cairo, Egypt. Saladin, the Kurdish nephew of Shirkuh, had become vizier to the last Fatimid caliph, al-Adid, in 1169 as officer of the Sunni Nur al-Din. In September 1171 he had the Friday prayer said in Cairo for the Abbasid caliph of Baghdad; days later al-Adid died. Two centuries of Shi'ite rule over Egypt ended without a blow, and Saladin became master of the Nile.
