    The chronicle of these years

    • The Relief of ViennaA.D. 1683, The Kahlenberg, Vienna. With the Ottoman army of Kara Mustafa at the walls of Vienna, King Jan III Sobieski of Poland leads his winged hussars down the Kahlenberg in one of the greatest cavalry charges in history. The siege is broken, and the Turk will never come so far into Europe again.
    • Newton's PrincipiaA.D. 1687, Cambridge, England. In July 1687 the Royal Society published, at Edmond Halley's expense, Isaac Newton's Mathematical Principles of Natural Philosophy. From three laws of motion and a single law of universal gravitation the Cambridge professor deduced the orbits of the planets, the paths of comets, and the tides of the sea. The heavens and the earth obeyed one law, and the Age of Reason took the book as its model.
    • Aurangzeb Takes GolcondaA.D. 1687, Golconda, Telangana, India. Having taken Bijapur in 1686, Aurangzeb laid siege to Golconda, the last of the Deccan sultanates. Its walls held for eight months against mines and assaults, until a bribed officer opened a postern gate by night. Abul Hasan Qutb Shah was carried off to captivity at Daulatabad, and the Mughal Empire reached its widest bounds.
    • William of Orange Lands at TorbayA.D. 1688, Brixham, England. Invited by seven leading Englishmen who dreaded a Catholic dynasty after the birth of a son to James II, William of Orange, the king's nephew and son-in-law, landed at Torbay on 5 November 1688 with a Dutch army. James's army melted away and he fled to France. Parliament gave the crown to William and Mary with a Bill of Rights, and the Glorious Revolution made Parliament supreme.
    • The Siamese Revolution of 1688A.D. 1688, Lopburi, Thailand. King Narai of Ayutthaya had welcomed French embassies and Jesuits and raised the Greek adventurer Constantine Phaulkon to be his chief minister, while French troops held Bangkok and Mergui. As Narai lay dying at Lopburi, the Master of the Elephants, Phetracha, seized power, had Phaulkon beheaded, and besieged the French until they sailed away.
    • The Treaty of NerchinskA.D. 1689, Nerchinsk, Russia. After Qing armies twice besieged the Russian fort of Albazin on the Amur, the envoys of Kangxi and of the Tsars met at Nerchinsk, the Jesuits Thomas Pereira and Jean-François Gerbillon interpreting in Latin. Russia gave up the Amur valley, and the frontier was fixed along the Argun and the Stanovoy mountains: the first treaty China made with a European power.
    • Job Charnock Founds CalcuttaA.D. 1690, Kolkata, West Bengal, India. On 24 August 1690 Job Charnock, agent of the East India Company, landed at Sutanuti on the Hooghly and set up a factory among three villages, Sutanuti, Kalikata, and Gobindapur. Fortified as Fort William a few years later, Calcutta grew into the capital of British India and the second city of the British Empire.
    • The Gold of Minas Geraisc. A.D. 1693, Ouro Preto, Brazil. Bandeirantes from São Paulo, ranging the interior for slaves and treasure, struck gold in the streams of the highlands that came to be called Minas Gerais, the General Mines. A rush of Portuguese and of African slaves followed; for much of the eighteenth century Brazil was the greatest gold producer on earth, and Vila Rica, now Ouro Preto, rose in baroque splendor with churches carved by the sculptor Aleijadinho.
