    The chronicle of these years

    • The Rebellion of An LushanA.D. 755, Fanyang, Hebei, China. An Lushan, a general of Sogdian and Turkic blood who commanded three frontier armies, rose at Fanyang, took Luoyang, and proclaimed himself emperor of Yan. Emperor Xuanzong fled Chang'an, and his mutinous guards forced him to have his beloved Yang Guifei strangled at Mawei. Eight years of war followed, and the Tang census fell from over fifty million to under seventeen.
    • The Donation of PepinA.D. 756, St. Peter's, Rome. Twice summoned by Pope Stephen II against the Lombards, Pepin crossed the Alps, besieged King Aistulf in Pavia, and gave the cities he surrendered, the old Exarchate of Ravenna, to St. Peter. The abbot Fulrad laid their keys on the apostle's tomb. Thus were born the Papal States, which would endure for eleven centuries.
    • The Umayyad Emirate of CórdobaA.D. 756, Córdoba, Spain. Abd al-Rahman, grandson of the caliph Hisham, escaped the slaughter of his house and wandered five years through Africa before crossing to Spain. In May 756 he defeated the governor Yusuf al-Fihri on the banks of the Guadalquivir and was proclaimed emir at Córdoba. His dynasty ruled al-Andalus for nearly three centuries, owing nothing to Baghdad.
    • The Kailasa Temple of Ellorac. A.D. 760, Ellora, Maharashtra, India. Under the Rashtrakuta king Krishna I, masons cut a whole temple to Shiva downward out of the basalt hillside at Ellora, removing some two hundred thousand tonnes of rock to free a shrine, a hall, and a gateway in a single piece. Carved to stand for Mount Kailasa, the god's Himalayan home, it is the greatest of all rock-cut temples.
    • Al-Mansur Founds BaghdadA.D. 762, Baghdad, Iraq. The caliph al-Mansur chose a site on the Tigris where the canals from the Euphrates came nearest, and in 762 began his City of Peace: a perfect circle of brick walls, four gates facing the quarters of his empire, and at the center his green-domed palace and mosque. Within fifty years Baghdad was the greatest city west of China.
    • The Rearguard at RoncevauxA.D. 778, Roncevaux Pass, Navarre. Returning from a fruitless campaign before Zaragoza, Charlemagne razed the walls of Pamplona. On 15 August 778 Basques fell upon his rearguard in the Pyrenean passes and slew it to a man, among them Roland, prefect of the Breton March. Three centuries later the disaster was sung, with Saracens in place of Basques, as the Song of Roland.
    • The Founding of Samyec. A.D. 779, Samye, Tibet. King Trisong Detsen, with the Indian masters Śāntarakṣita and Padmasambhava, completed Samye, the first Buddhist monastery in Tibet, laid out as a mandala of the universe, and saw the first Tibetans ordained as monks. A great debate held there between Indian and Chinese teachers ended, by Tibetan tradition, in favour of the Indian school.
