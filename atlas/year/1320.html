    The chronicle of these years

    • The Popes at AvignonA.D. 1309, Avignon, Provence. Clement V, a Gascon pope elected under the shadow of the French king, settled in 1309 at Avignon on the Rhône, beside the papal Comtat Venaissin. For nearly seventy years his successors ruled the Church from its great palace there, an age Petrarch called the Babylonian Captivity, until Gregory XI returned to Rome in 1377.
    • Malik Kafur Reaches MaduraiA.D. 1311, Madurai, Tamil Nadu, India. Malik Kafur, a slave bought for a thousand dinars and raised to command Alauddin's armies, swept through the south: he humbled the Kakatiyas of Warangal, sacked the Hoysala capital of Dwarasamudra, and pressed on into the Pandya country during a war between royal brothers, plundering Madurai and the temples of the Kaveri. He returned to Delhi with elephants and treasure beyond any earlier hoard.
    • Özbeg Khan Brings Islam to the HordeA.D. 1313, Sarai, Volga. Özbeg, khan of the Golden Horde from 1313, embraced Islam and made it the faith of the house of Jochi. In his reign of nearly thirty years Sarai on the lower Volga was one of the great cities of the world, the clearing house of the trade between the Black Sea, Persia, and the Russian forests, and the princes of Rus came to it to buy their patents of rule.
    • Robert the Bruce at BannockburnA.D. 1314, Bannockburn, Scotland. On 23 and 24 June 1314 Robert the Bruce met the far larger army of Edward II marching to relieve Stirling Castle. Bruce slew Henry de Bohun with his axe in single combat before the hosts, and on the second day the Scottish spearmen drove the English knights into the Bannock burn. Scotland's freedom was won in fact, and in 1328 by treaty.
    • Mansa Musa Comes to CairoA.D. 1324, Cairo, Egypt. On his pilgrimage to Mecca, Mansa Musa of Mali reached Cairo with a train of thousands and spent and gave away gold so freely that its value in the city was depressed for years, as al-Umari recorded a dozen years later. Returning, he built mosques at Gao and Timbuktu; the Catalan Atlas of 1375 shows him enthroned, holding a nugget of gold.
    • The Mexica Found Tenochtitlanc. A.D. 1325, Tenochtitlan, Mexico. The Mexica, latecomers to the Valley of Mexico who had served its lords as mercenaries, settled on a marshy island in Lake Texcoco where, their priests said, they saw the sign promised by their god Huitzilopochtli: an eagle perched upon a cactus growing from a rock. There they built Tenochtitlan, which within two centuries was a city of perhaps two hundred thousand.
    • Orhan Takes BursaA.D. 1326, Bursa, Bithynia. Prusa, at the foot of Mount Olympus in Bithynia, had been blockaded by Ottoman forts for years when it surrendered in April 1326 to Orhan, son of Osman, who died about that time. As Bursa it became the first Ottoman capital, where Osman and Orhan were buried. Nicaea fell in 1331 and Nicomedia in 1337, and the Eastern Roman hold on Bithynia was ended.
    • Muhammad bin Tughlaq Moves to DaulatabadA.D. 1327, Daulatabad, Maharashtra, India. Muhammad bin Tughlaq, a sultan of vast learning and wild schemes, ordered the people of Delhi to remove to Devagiri, which he renamed Daulatabad, some seven hundred miles to the south, to rule the Deccan from its centre. Many died on the road. Within a few years he let them return, and Ibn Battuta found Delhi, the greatest city of the Muslim east, half empty.
