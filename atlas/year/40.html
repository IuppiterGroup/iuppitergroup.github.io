    The chronicle of these years

    • Jesus Begins His Ministryc. A.D. 28, Capernaum, Galilee. In the fifteenth year of Tiberius, John the Baptist preached repentance by the Jordan, and Jesus of Nazareth came to be baptized by him. Jesus then went about Galilee, making Capernaum on its lake his home, calling the fishermen Simon Peter, Andrew, James, and John, preaching the Kingdom of God, and healing the sick, as the four Gospels record.
    • The Crucifixion and Resurrectionc. A.D. 30, Golgotha, Jerusalem. At the Passover Jesus entered Jerusalem, shared a last supper with his disciples, and was arrested in the garden of Gethsemane. Jesus was handed over to Pontius Pilate by the high priest Caiaphas and the Temple authorities, and Pilate ordered him crucified. He died on the cross at Golgotha outside the walls; on the third day the tomb was found empty, and the risen Christ appeared to his disciples.
    • Pentecost in Jerusalemc. A.D. 30, Jerusalem, Judea. Fifty days after the Passover, as the Acts of the Apostles records, the Holy Spirit came upon the disciples gathered in Jerusalem like tongues of fire, and they spoke in the languages of pilgrims from every nation. Peter preached the risen Christ to the crowd, about three thousand were baptized that day, and the Church began.
    • Kujula Kadphises Unites the Yuezhic. A.D. 40, Bactria, Afghanistan. Kujula Kadphises, chief of the Kushan clan, one of five Yuezhi tribes holding Bactria, overthrew the other four and made himself king. The Chinese annals tell that he went on to take the Kabul valley and Gandhara from the Parthians, founding the Kushan Empire. He lived past eighty, and his early coins copied the head of the Greek king Hermaeus.
    • The Revolt of the Trung SistersA.D. 40, Me Linh, Vietnam. When the Han governor Su Ding put to death the husband of Trung Trac, she and her sister Trung Nhi raised the Lac lords against Chinese rule and took sixty-five citadels. Trung Trac reigned as queen at Me Linh until the general Ma Yuan marched south; defeated in A.D. 43, the sisters perished, and Vietnam honors them still as its first heroines.
    • Claudius Invades BritainA.D. 43, Camulodunum, Britain. Aulus Plautius landed in Kent with four legions and forced the crossing of the Medway against Caratacus and the Catuvellauni. The emperor Claudius came over with elephants for the final advance and entered Camulodunum, the British capital, staying sixteen days; eleven kings submitted, and Britain became a Roman province for nearly four centuries.
    • Paul Sets Out from Antiochc. A.D. 46, Antioch, Syria. At Antioch, where the disciples were first called Christians, the church laid hands on Barnabas and Paul and sent them to Cyprus and Asia Minor. On this and later journeys Paul founded churches at Philippi, Thessalonica, Corinth, and Ephesus and wrote them the letters that stand in the New Testament, carrying the Gospel from the synagogues to the Gentiles.
    • Roman Ships at Muzirisc. A.D. 50, Pattanam, Kerala, India. Greek pilots had learned to ride the southwest monsoon straight across the Arabian Sea, and each summer ships from Roman Egypt reached Muziris on the Malabar coast for pepper, pearls, and fine cloth. Pliny grumbled that India drained the empire of fifty million sesterces a year, and hoards of Roman gold and silver coins are still turned up in southern India.
