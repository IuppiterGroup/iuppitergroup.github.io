    The chronicle of these years

    • The End of the Prague SpringA.D. 1968, Wenceslas Square, Prague. Under Alexander Dubček, first secretary from January 1968, Czechoslovakia lifted censorship and promised socialism with a human face. On the night of 20 August some quarter of a million troops of the Soviet Union, Poland, Hungary, and Bulgaria invaded. Dubček was taken to Moscow and forced to submit; in January 1969 the student Jan Palach burned himself to death in Wenceslas Square in protest.
    • The Tet OffensiveA.D. 1968, Hue, Vietnam. In the lunar New Year truce of January 1968 some eighty thousand Communist troops struck more than a hundred towns of South Vietnam, and commandos broke into the American embassy compound in Saigon. At Hue the fighting lasted a month, and the occupiers massacred some three thousand civilians. A military defeat for Hanoi, the offensive broke the will of Washington.
    • Men on the MoonA.D. 1969, Cape Kennedy, Florida. On 16 July 1969 a Saturn V rocket lifted Apollo 11 from Cape Kennedy, and on 20 July Neil Armstrong and Edwin Aldrin set the lunar module Eagle down on the Sea of Tranquillity while Michael Collins circled above. Armstrong stepped onto the surface with the words 'That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind,' fulfilling the pledge Kennedy had made in 1961.
    • Gaddafi Seizes LibyaA.D. 1969, Tripoli, Libya. On 1 September 1969, while King Idris was taking the waters in Turkey, junior officers led by the twenty-seven-year-old Captain Muammar Gaddafi seized power without resistance. Gaddafi expelled the American and British bases and the Italian settlers, nationalized the oil, and in 1977 proclaimed the Jamahiriya, a state of the masses, which he ruled as Guide of the Revolution for forty-two years.
    • The Aswan High DamA.D. 1970, Aswan, Egypt. Built with Soviet loans and engineers from 1960, the High Dam at Aswan was completed in July 1970, holding back the Nile in Lake Nasser, more than three hundred miles long. It ended the yearly flood that had governed Egyptian life since the Pharaohs and gave the country electric power, but it drowned Nubia, whose people were resettled; the temples of Abu Simbel were cut apart and raised to higher ground.
    • The Bhola CycloneA.D. 1970, Bhola, Bangladesh. On the night of 12 November 1970 a cyclone drove a storm surge over the low islands of the Bay of Bengal in East Pakistan, drowning between three hundred thousand and half a million people, the deadliest tropical storm on record. The government's slow relief inflamed Bengali anger, and a month later the Awami League swept the East in the national elections.
    • The Birth of BangladeshA.D. 1971, Dhaka, Bangladesh. When the army of West Pakistan launched Operation Searchlight against the Bengalis on 25 March 1971, killing students, intellectuals, and Hindus by the thousand, Sheikh Mujibur Rahman's followers proclaimed independence. Ten million refugees fled to India. In December India intervened, and on 16 December General Niazi surrendered with some ninety thousand men at Dacca. Bangladesh was free.
    • The Arab Oil EmbargoA.D. 1973, Kuwait City, Kuwait. Meeting in Kuwait on 17 October 1973, the Arab oil ministers agreed to cut production month by month and soon embargoed shipments to the United States and the Netherlands for their support of Israel. The price of oil quadrupled within months. The age of cheap energy ended, the West fell into recession, and the Gulf states gained wealth and power beyond all precedent.
