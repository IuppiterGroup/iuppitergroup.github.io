    The chronicle of these years

    • The Hijra to MedinaA.D. 622, Medina, Arabia. Warned of a plot against his life, Muhammad slipped out of Mecca with Abu Bakr and reached the oasis of Yathrib, thereafter Medina, the City of the Prophet, in September 622. There he became lawgiver, judge, and war leader of a community bound by faith rather than blood. The Muslim calendar counts its years from this emigration.
    • The Avars and Persians Before ConstantinopleA.D. 626, Constantinople, Thrace. In the summer of 626, while Heraclius campaigned far off in the Caucasus, the Avar khagan and his Slav subjects invested the Theodosian Walls, and the Persian general Shahrbaraz watched from Chalcedon across the Bosporus. Patriarch Sergius carried the icon of the Virgin along the ramparts; the imperial fleet sank the Slav canoes in the Golden Horn, and the khagan burned his engines and withdrew.
    • Heraclius Victorious at NinevehA.D. 627, Nineveh, Mesopotamia. Allied with the Western Turks, Heraclius struck south from the Caucasus into the heart of the Persian empire. On 12 December 627 he broke the army of Rhahzadh among the ruins of Nineveh, by tradition slaying the general with his own hand. Khosrow II was deposed and put to death by his own nobles, and Persia gave back the lost provinces of the East and the True Cross.
    • Brahmagupta Defines ZeroA.D. 628, Bhinmal, Rajasthan, India. At Bhillamala in Rajasthan the astronomer Brahmagupta completed the Brahmasphutasiddhanta, the first book to treat zero as a number in its own right, with rules for adding, subtracting, and multiplying by it, and for reckoning with negative numbers as debts. Carried to Baghdad and rendered into Arabic, the Indian numerals with their place value and zero passed to Europe as the Arabic figures.
    • Mecca Opens Its GatesA.D. 630, Mecca, Arabia. After the Quraysh broke the truce of Hudaybiyya, Muhammad marched on Mecca with ten thousand men. The city yielded with little bloodshed, and Abu Sufyan made his submission. The idols about the Kaaba, some 360 by tradition, were cast down, and the shrine that Muslims hold was raised by Abraham was given over to the worship of the one God.
    • Taizong Becomes Heavenly QaghanA.D. 630, Yin Mountains, Inner Mongolia, China. In a winter campaign the general Li Jing fell upon the camp of the Eastern Turkic Illig Qaghan near the Yin Mountains, and the qaghan was soon taken and brought to Chang'an. The chiefs of the steppe hailed Emperor Taizong as Tengri Qaghan, Heavenly Khan, and for half a century the Tang emperor reigned as sovereign both of China and of the nomads.
    • Abu Bakr Chosen CaliphA.D. 632, Medina, Arabia. Muhammad died at Medina in June 632, leaving no undisputed successor. In the hall of the Banu Sa'ida the leading companions acclaimed his oldest friend and father-in-law, Abu Bakr, as khalifa, the successor. The partisans of Ali, the Prophet's cousin and son-in-law, held that the office was his by right, and the seed of the great schism of Islam was sown.
    • The Battle of the YarmoukA.D. 636, Yarmouk River, Syria. In August 636 the great army Heraclius had gathered to recover Syria met the Arabs under Khalid ibn al-Walid and Abu Ubayda in the gorges of the Yarmouk. After six days of battle the imperial host was broken and driven into the ravines. Heraclius left Syria, by tradition with the words "Farewell, Syria, my fair province." Syria was lost to the empire.
