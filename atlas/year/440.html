    The chronicle of these years

    • The Founding of Nalandac. A.D. 430, Nalanda, Bihar, India. Under Kumaragupta I a Buddhist monastery rose at Nalanda, near Rajagriha, and grew under later kings into the greatest seat of learning in Asia. Its brick halls and temples housed thousands of monks studying logic, grammar, medicine, and the Mahayana scriptures, and Xuanzang studied there for five years. Its libraries perished in the Turkish conquest about 1200.
    • Patrick Returns to Irelandc. A.D. 432, Armagh, Ireland. Patrick, a Briton seized by Irish raiders as a boy and enslaved for six years as a herdsman, escaped home and later returned to Ireland as a bishop. He baptized thousands, ordained clergy, and set up his church, by later tradition, at Armagh. His Confession is the oldest document of Irish history, and the island became a land of saints and scholars.
    • Genseric Takes CarthageA.D. 439, Carthage, Africa. Genseric, the lame and ruthless king who had led the Vandals and Alans across the Strait of Gibraltar ten years before, broke his treaty with Rome, seized Carthage by surprise in October, and made it his capital. Catholic bishops were exiled and their churches given to the Arians, and from Carthage the Vandal ships raided Sicily, Italy, and Greece.
    • The Battle of the Catalaunian PlainsA.D. 451, Catalaunian Plains, Gaul. Attila invaded Gaul with Huns, Ostrogoths, Gepids, and other subject peoples. The Roman general Aetius, allied with the Visigoths of Theodoric I, the Franks, and the Burgundians, met him on the plains between Troyes and Châlons in one of the bloodiest battles of antiquity. Theodoric fell, but Attila withdrew behind his wagons and then out of Gaul.
    • The Council of ChalcedonA.D. 451, Chalcedon, Bithynia. Some five hundred bishops, the largest council of antiquity, met at Chalcedon across the Bosporus and, guided by the Tome of Pope Leo, defined that Christ is one person in two natures, truly God and truly man. The definition became the faith of Rome and Constantinople, but Egypt, much of Syria, and Armenia in time rejected it.
    • Pope Leo Meets AttilaA.D. 452, Mincio River, Italy. Attila crossed the Julian Alps, razed Aquileia after a long siege, and sacked Milan and the cities of the Po. Near the Mincio he was met by an embassy from Valentinian III led by Pope Leo I, and he turned back from Italy, whether from awe, from plague in his army, or from fear of an Eastern army behind him. He died the next year.
    • The Vandals Sack RomeA.D. 455, Rome, Italy. After the murder of Valentinian III, Genseric sailed from Carthage and took Rome without resistance. At the plea of Pope Leo he spared the people from slaughter and the city from fire, but for fourteen days the Vandals stripped it of its treasures, among them the spoils of the Temple of Jerusalem, and carried off the empress Eudoxia and her daughters.
    • Skandagupta Repels the Hunsc. A.D. 455, Bhitari, Uttar Pradesh, India. Skandagupta, coming to the throne in a season of peril, drove back the Hunas who had poured into India from the northwest. His pillar at Bhitari tells that the earth shook when he closed with them in battle, and that he passed a night sleeping on the bare ground to restore his family's shaken fortune. The empire held, but its treasury was drained.
