    The chronicle of these years

    • The Bolsheviks Seize PetrogradA.D. 1917, Winter Palace, Petrograd. On the night of 7 November 1917, the October Revolution by the Russian calendar, the Red Guards and soldiers of the Military Revolutionary Committee directed by Trotsky occupied the bridges, stations, and telegraph of Petrograd and took the Winter Palace, arresting Kerensky's ministers. Lenin proclaimed Soviet power, peace, and land to the peasants, and founded the first communist state.
    • The Treaty of VersaillesA.D. 1919, Hall of Mirrors, Versailles. In the Hall of Mirrors where the German Empire had been proclaimed, the German delegates signed on 28 June 1919, five years to the day after Sarajevo, the treaty dictated by Clemenceau, Lloyd George, and Wilson. Germany lost Alsace-Lorraine, its colonies, and much of its eastern provinces to Poland, accepted responsibility for the war, and was disarmed and burdened with reparations; the Covenant of the League of Nations stood at its head.
    • The March First MovementA.D. 1919, Seoul, Korea. Thirty-three religious leaders, Christians, Cheondogyo believers, and Buddhists, signed a declaration of Korean independence, which was read aloud in Tapgol Park in Seoul. Peaceful demonstrations spread to every province, and the Japanese police and army answered with rifles and fire, killing thousands. A Provisional Government of Korea was founded in Shanghai.
    • The May Fourth MovementA.D. 1919, Tiananmen, Peking, China. When the Paris Peace Conference gave Germany's rights in Shandong to Japan instead of returning them to China, three thousand students of Peking University and other colleges marched to Tiananmen and burned the house of a pro-Japanese minister. Strikes and boycotts spread to Shanghai, and the movement grew into a revolt against Confucian tradition in the name of science and democracy.
    • The Massacre at AmritsarA.D. 1919, Jallianwala Bagh, Amritsar, India. On 13 April 1919, the festival of Baisakhi, General Reginald Dyer marched troops into the Jallianwala Bagh, a walled garden at Amritsar where thousands had gathered in defiance of a ban on meetings, and without warning fired until the ammunition was spent. The official count was 379 dead, Indian estimates far higher. Gandhi, Tagore, and millions of moderate Indians turned against the Raj.
    • The Miracle on the VistulaA.D. 1920, Radzymin, Poland. In August 1920 Tukhachevsky's Red Army stood before Warsaw, meaning to carry revolution over Poland's corpse into Germany. While the line held at Radzymin, Piłsudski struck on 16 August from the Wieprz river into the Soviet flank and rear; the Red armies broke and fled, leaving tens of thousands of prisoners. The Peace of Riga in 1921 fixed Poland's eastern frontier.
    • The Treaty of TrianonA.D. 1920, Grand Trianon, Versailles. Signed on 4 June 1920 in the Grand Trianon palace at Versailles, the peace with Hungary stripped the old kingdom of St. Stephen of over two thirds of its territory and a third of its Magyar people, who found themselves under Romanian, Czechoslovak, and Yugoslav rule. Flags in Budapest flew at half-mast, and the revision of Trianon became the creed of Hungarian politics.
    • The Ottoman Sultanate AbolishedA.D. 1922, Ankara, Turkey. On 1 November 1922 the Grand National Assembly at Ankara, led by Mustafa Kemal, abolished the Ottoman sultanate, and on 17 November Mehmed VI, last of the thirty-six sultans of the house of Osman, left Constantinople on a British warship. His cousin Abdulmejid was left as caliph alone, until the caliphate itself was abolished in March 1924. Six centuries of Ottoman rule were at an end.
