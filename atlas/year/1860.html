    The chronicle of these years

    • The Rising at MeerutA.D. 1857, Meerut, Uttar Pradesh, India. On 10 May 1857, after eighty-five troopers had been fettered for refusing cartridges rumoured to be greased with cow and pig fat, the sepoys at Meerut rose, shot their officers, and rode for Delhi. There they proclaimed the aged Bahadur Shah II emperor of Hindustan. Rebellion spread across the north, and the British needed more than a year to put it down.
    • The Slaughter at SolferinoA.D. 1859, Solferino, Lombardy. On 24 June 1859 the armies of Napoleon III and Victor Emmanuel II met Francis Joseph's Austrians south of Lake Garda in a battle of some 300,000 men, the last in Europe in which three monarchs commanded in person. Austria lost Lombardy. The Genevan Henri Dunant, who tended the wounded at Castiglione, wrote a book on the carnage that led to the founding of the Red Cross in 1863.
    • On the Origin of SpeciesA.D. 1859, Down House, Kent. More than twenty years after the voyage of the Beagle, and spurred by a letter from Alfred Russel Wallace in the Moluccas setting out the same theory, Charles Darwin published On the Origin of Species on 24 November 1859. Its argument that species arise by natural selection sold out the first printing of 1,250 copies to the booksellers at once and began a controversy that outlived him.
    • The French Take SaigonA.D. 1859, Saigon, Vietnam. Rigault de Genouilly sailed south from Tourane and on 17 February 1859 stormed the citadel of Saigon. Defeated again at Ky Hoa in 1861, the court of Tu Duc yielded three eastern provinces of the Mekong delta by the Treaty of Saigon in 1862, and Admiral de la Grandiere seized the western three in 1867, forming the French colony of Cochinchina.
    • Garibaldi's ThousandA.D. 1860, Marsala, Sicily. Sailing from Quarto near Genoa in two steamers, Giuseppe Garibaldi landed at Marsala on 11 May 1860 with about a thousand red-shirted volunteers to aid the Sicilian rising. He beat the Bourbon troops at Calatafimi, took Palermo, crossed to the mainland, and entered Naples in September. At Teano on 26 October he hailed Victor Emmanuel II as King of Italy and handed him half a kingdom.
    • The Damascus MassacreA.D. 1860, Damascus, Syria. Civil war between Druze and Maronites in Mount Lebanon in the spring of 1860 left thousands of Christians dead, and in July the mob of Damascus fell upon the Christian quarter, burning churches and killing thousands while Ottoman troops looked on or joined in. The exiled Algerian emir Abd al-Qadir and his men sheltered many in his house. France landed an army, and Mount Lebanon received a Christian governor.
    • The Burning of the Summer PalaceA.D. 1860, Yuanmingyuan, Beijing, China. In the Second Opium War an Anglo-French army marched on Beijing, and the Xianfeng Emperor fled to Jehol. When envoys seized under a flag of truce died under torture, Lord Elgin ordered the burning of the Yuanmingyuan, the vast garden palace of the Qing emperors, with its European pavilions built by the Jesuits. The fires burned for three days.
    • The Kingdom of Italy ProclaimedA.D. 1861, Turin, Piedmont. On 17 March 1861 the first Italian parliament, meeting in Turin, proclaimed Victor Emmanuel II King of Italy, uniting Piedmont, Lombardy, the central duchies, the Romagna, and the Two Sicilies. Cavour, the minister who had made it, died in June. Venice was won in 1866 and Rome in 1870, and the capital moved to Florence in 1865 and to Rome in 1871.
