    The chronicle of these years

    • Cyclone NargisA.D. 2008, Irrawaddy Delta, Burma. On 2 May 2008 Cyclone Nargis drove a wall of seawater across the low rice lands of the Irrawaddy delta, and some 138,000 people died. The ruling generals, who had crushed the monk-led Saffron Revolution the year before, at first barred foreign relief workers and pressed on a week later with a referendum on their new constitution.
    • The Defeat of the Tamil TigersA.D. 2009, Mullaitivu, Sri Lanka. After twenty-six years of civil war the Sri Lankan army drove the Liberation Tigers of Tamil Eelam into a strip of coast near Mullaitivu, where tens of thousands of civilians were trapped with them. On 18 May 2009 the Tiger leader Velupillai Prabhakaran was killed near the Nanthi Kadal lagoon, and the war was over.
    • The Spark at Sidi BouzidA.D. 2010, Sidi Bouzid, Tunisia. On 17 December 2010 Mohamed Bouazizi, a young fruit seller in the Tunisian town of Sidi Bouzid whose cart had been confiscated by officials, set himself on fire before the governor's office. His death in January set off protests across Tunisia, and on 14 January 2011 President Zine El Abidine Ben Ali fled to Saudi Arabia after twenty-three years in power. The Arab Spring had begun.
    • The Haiti EarthquakeA.D. 2010, Port-au-Prince, Haiti. On 12 January 2010 an earthquake of magnitude 7.0 struck near Léogâne, some sixteen miles from Port-au-Prince, and in less than a minute leveled much of the capital, among its ruins the National Palace and the cathedral. Estimates of the dead ranged from a hundred thousand to more than three hundred thousand, making it one of the deadliest earthquakes ever recorded; cholera followed.
    • Tahrir SquareA.D. 2011, Cairo, Egypt. Inspired by Tunisia, crowds filled Cairo's Tahrir Square on 25 January 2011 and held it for eighteen days against police, hired thugs on camels, and the regime's propaganda. On 11 February Hosni Mubarak resigned after thirty years. The Muslim Brotherhood's Mohamed Morsi won the presidency in 2012, but in July 2013 the army under Abdel Fattah el-Sisi removed him and killed hundreds of his supporters at the Rabaa sit-in.
    • The Syrian Civil WarA.D. 2011, Daraa, Syria. In March 2011 security men in Daraa arrested and tortured schoolboys who had written slogans against the regime on a wall, and the protests that followed were met with gunfire. The uprising spread across Syria and became a civil war of many sides, in which Russia and Iran upheld Bashar al-Assad, and which by 2024 had killed perhaps half a million and driven half the nation from their homes.
    • The Tōhoku Earthquake and TsunamiA.D. 2011, Fukushima, Japan. On 11 March the strongest earthquake ever measured in Japan struck off the coast of Tōhoku, and a tsunami up to forty metres high swept over the shore, killing some eighteen thousand people. At the Fukushima Daiichi plant the waves cut the power to the cooling systems, three reactors melted down, and over a hundred thousand people were evacuated.
    • South Sudan IndependentA.D. 2011, Juba, South Sudan. Two civil wars between the Arab and Muslim north of Sudan and the African, largely Christian and animist south, the second of them costing some two million lives between 1983 and 2005, ended in a peace that promised the south a vote. In January 2011 nearly ninety-nine per cent voted to separate, and on 9 July South Sudan became the newest nation on earth under Salva Kiir.
