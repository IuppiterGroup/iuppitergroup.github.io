    The chronicle of these years

    • The Edict of NantesA.D. 1598, Nantes, Brittany. Henry IV, the Huguenot king who had turned Catholic to win Paris, ended thirty-six years of religious war with the edict signed at Nantes in April 1598. The Huguenots received freedom of conscience, public worship in named places, equal right to offices, and some hundred fortified towns as surety. The settlement held for nearly ninety years.
    • Shah Abbas Makes Isfahan His CapitalA.D. 1598, Isfahan, Persia. Abbas I, who had won back Khorasan from the Uzbeks and remade his army around a corps of slave soldiers taken from Georgia and Armenia, moved his capital to Isfahan in 1598. Around a vast square, the Naqsh-e Jahan, he raised the royal mosque, the mosque of Shaykh Lutfallah, the Ali Qapu palace, and the royal bazaar. Persians said that Isfahan was half the world.
    • The Battle of SekigaharaA.D. 1600, Sekigahara, Gifu, Japan. Two years after Hideyoshi's death the great lords divided between Tokugawa Ieyasu and the Western Army raised by Ishida Mitsunari in the name of Hideyoshi's son. In the fog of an autumn morning at Sekigahara the treachery of Kobayakawa Hideaki decided the day for Ieyasu. Three years later he was named shogun, and his house ruled Japan in peace for two and a half centuries.
    • James VI Takes the English CrownA.D. 1603, London, England. When Elizabeth I died childless on 24 March 1603, the crown passed to her cousin James VI of Scotland, great-grandson of Henry VII's daughter Margaret. He rode south to London as James I, and the two kingdoms, enemies for three centuries, shared one king; a century later they would share one Parliament.
    • The Duyfken Reaches AustraliaA.D. 1606, Cape York, Australia. Willem Janszoon, sent from Banten in the little ship Duyfken to seek gold in New Guinea, coasted the western shore of Cape York in March 1606, believing it part of New Guinea. His men, the first Europeans known to set foot in Australia, fought with the Aboriginal people, and Janszoon named the farthest point he reached Cape Keerweer, Cape Turnabout.
    • Jamestown FoundedA.D. 1607, Jamestown, Virginia. One hundred and four men and boys of the Virginia Company landed on a marshy peninsula of the James River on 14 May 1607 and built a fort they named for King James. Disease and hunger killed most of them, and the 'starving time' of 1609 and 1610 nearly ended the venture, but John Rolfe's tobacco, first shipped in 1614, gave Virginia its fortune and England its first lasting colony in America.
    • Champlain Founds QuebecA.D. 1608, Quebec, Canada. Samuel de Champlain built a fortified habitation of timber beneath the cliffs where the St. Lawrence narrows, at the place the Algonquins called kebec. Of the twenty-eight men who wintered there only eight survived the scurvy, but Champlain held on, allied himself with the Huron and Algonquin against the Iroquois, and made Quebec the capital of New France.
    • Galileo's Starry MessengerA.D. 1610, Padua, Italy. Turning a telescope of his own making on the heavens from Padua, Galileo Galilei saw the mountains of the moon, the countless stars of the Milky Way, and four moons circling Jupiter, and published them in March 1610 in the Sidereus Nuncius. His later defense of the motion of the earth brought him before the Roman Inquisition in 1633, where he abjured it.
