    The chronicle of these years

    • The Arab Oil EmbargoA.D. 1973, Kuwait City, Kuwait. Meeting in Kuwait on 17 October 1973, the Arab oil ministers agreed to cut production month by month and soon embargoed shipments to the United States and the Netherlands for their support of Israel. The price of oil quadrupled within months. The age of cheap energy ended, the West fell into recession, and the Gulf states gained wealth and power beyond all precedent.
    • The Fall of SaigonA.D. 1975, Saigon, Vietnam. Two years after the last American combat troops left, North Vietnam's spring offensive of 1975 swept down the coast. As helicopters lifted the last Americans and thousands of Vietnamese from the city, a North Vietnamese tank broke through the gates of the Independence Palace on 30 April, and General Duong Van Minh surrendered. Vietnam was united under Communist rule the next year.
    • The Death of FrancoA.D. 1975, Madrid, Spain. Francisco Franco died on 20 November 1975 after thirty-six years in power, and two days later Juan Carlos de Borbón, grandson of Alfonso XIII and the successor Franco had named, was proclaimed king. With Adolfo Suárez as prime minister he dismantled the regime by its own laws; a democratic constitution was approved in 1978, and the King faced down an attempted coup in the Cortes in February 1981.
    • The Lebanese Civil WarA.D. 1975, Beirut, Lebanon. On 13 April 1975, after gunmen fired on a church in the Ain al-Rummaneh quarter of Beirut, Phalangist militiamen ambushed a bus carrying Palestinians and killed some two dozen. Lebanon's delicate balance of Christian and Muslim sects collapsed into a civil war of militias, massacres, and foreign armies that lasted fifteen years and killed some 150,000 people, until the Taif Agreement of 1989.
    • The Khmer Rouge Take Phnom PenhA.D. 1975, Phnom Penh, Cambodia. On 17 April 1975 the black-clad soldiers of the Khmer Rouge entered Phnom Penh and drove its two million people into the countryside. Under Pol Pot, Democratic Kampuchea abolished money, markets, schools, and religion and turned the land into a vast labor camp. Before Vietnamese troops ended the regime in January 1979, some 1.7 million Cambodians had died of execution, starvation, and disease.
    • Papua New Guinea IndependentA.D. 1975, Port Moresby, Papua New Guinea. On 16 September 1975 the Australian flag was lowered at Port Moresby, and Papua New Guinea became an independent state under Prime Minister Michael Somare. Its nearly three million people spoke more than eight hundred languages; many highland valleys had first been entered by outsiders only in the 1930s, when gold prospectors found there a million people unknown to the world.
    • The Iranian RevolutionA.D. 1979, Tehran, Iran. After a year of mass demonstrations, Mohammad Reza Shah left Iran on 16 January 1979, and on 1 February the Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini returned from fifteen years of exile to a welcome of millions. The monarchy fell within days, and by referendum Iran became an Islamic Republic under the guardianship of the jurist. In November student militants seized the American embassy and held fifty-two hostages for 444 days.
    • The Soviet Invasion of AfghanistanA.D. 1979, Kabul, Afghanistan. On 24 December 1979 Soviet airborne troops began landing at Kabul, and on the 27th special forces stormed the Tajbeg Palace, killed the communist ruler Hafizullah Amin, and installed Babrak Karmal. Some hundred thousand Soviet troops fought for nine years against the mujahideen, armed by America, Pakistan, and Saudi Arabia; about a million Afghans died and five million fled abroad.
