    The chronicle of these years

    • Al-Biruni Writes on Indiac. A.D. 1030, Ghazni, Afghanistan. Al-Biruni of Khwarazm, carried to Ghazni in Mahmud's train, learned Sanskrit and wrote his Kitab al-Hind, a sober account of Indian religion, philosophy, astronomy, and custom without equal in medieval literature. He noted coolly that Mahmud's raids had utterly ruined the prosperity of the country and left the Hindus cherishing the most inveterate aversion toward all Muslims.
    • The Caliphate of Córdoba AbolishedA.D. 1031, Córdoba, Spain. After two decades of civil war, in which Berber and Slav soldiers set up and pulled down caliphs and sacked Córdoba and Madinat al-Zahra, the notables of Córdoba deposed Hisham III in 1031 and declared the caliphate at an end. Al-Andalus broke into some two dozen taifa kingdoms, rich in poets and palaces and weak in arms, which soon paid tribute to the Christian kings.
    • Li Yuanhao Proclaims the Great XiaA.D. 1038, Xingqing, Ningxia, China. Li Yuanhao, chief of the Tangut people of the Ordos and Gansu, threw off his Song titles, ordered his subjects to shave their crowns, had a script of his own devised for the Tangut tongue, and proclaimed himself emperor of the Great Xia at Xingqing. His armies beat the Song three times in as many years, and the Western Xia endured for nearly two centuries.
    • The Seljuks at DandanaqanA.D. 1040, Dandanaqan, near Merv. Tughril and Chaghri, grandsons of the Oghuz chief Seljuk, had led their Turkmen herdsmen across the Oxus into Khorasan. Near the fort of Dandanaqan between Merv and Sarakhs, in May 1040, their horsemen wore down the heavier army of the Ghaznavid sultan Mas'ud in the waterless desert and routed it. Khorasan was theirs, and the road to Persia and Baghdad lay open.
    • The First Gunpowder FormulasA.D. 1044, Kaifeng, Henan, China. The Complete Essentials for the Military Classics, compiled for Emperor Renzong by Zeng Gongliang and Ding Du, set down the earliest known recipes for gunpowder, mixing saltpetre, sulphur, and charcoal for fire arrows, smoke balls, and bombs hurled by catapult. Over the next three centuries the invention passed to the Mongols, the Islamic world, and Europe, and it overturned the castles and armies of the earth.
    • Anawrahta Takes the Throne of PaganA.D. 1044, Pagan, Burma. Anawrahta slew his kinsman Sokkate, the chronicles say in single combat, and became king of Pagan. In thirty-three years he made the little city on the Irrawaddy the head of the first empire of Burma, building irrigation works around Kyaukse, beating back his neighbors, and setting a chain of forty-three fortified towns along the eastern hills.
    • The City of Cahokiac. A.D. 1050, Cahokia, Illinois. Across the Mississippi from the site of St. Louis, the Mississippian people of Cahokia, growing maize on the rich bottomlands, built within a few generations a town of perhaps fifteen thousand around Monks Mound, a platform of earth a hundred feet high and broader at its base than the Great Pyramid of Giza. Its lords, buried with sacrificed retainers and thousands of shell beads, ruled the largest town north of Mexico.
    • The Great SchismA.D. 1054, Hagia Sophia, Constantinople. On 16 July 1054 Cardinal Humbert, legate of Pope Leo IX, strode into Hagia Sophia at the hour of the liturgy and laid on the altar a bull excommunicating Patriarch Michael Cerularius. The patriarch answered with anathemas of his own. Quarrels over the authority of Rome, the Filioque, and unleavened bread hardened into a rupture between the Latin and Greek Churches that has never healed.
