    The chronicle of these years

    • A New WorldA.D. 1492, San Salvador, Bahamas. In January, Ferdinand and Isabella take Granada, ending nearly eight centuries of Moorish rule in Spain. In October, sailing under their flag, Christopher Columbus crosses the Atlantic and makes landfall in the Bahamas. The known world is about to double in size.
    • The Fall of GranadaA.D. 1492, Alhambra, Granada. On 2 January 1492, after a war of ten years, Boabdil, last Nasrid emir of Granada, handed the keys of the Alhambra to Ferdinand and Isabella and rode into exile. The silver cross was raised on the Torre de la Vela. Nearly eight centuries after the Muslim conquest, all Spain was Christian, and that same year the queen sent Columbus across the Ocean Sea.
    • The Expulsion of the Jews from SpainA.D. 1492, Granada, Spain. By the edict signed at Granada on 31 March 1492, Ferdinand and Isabella commanded every Jew in their kingdoms to accept baptism or leave by the end of July. Many thousands chose exile, to Portugal, Italy, North Africa, and the Ottoman lands, where Sultan Bayezid II welcomed them; the Sephardim carried their Spanish tongue across the Mediterranean for four centuries.
    • Vasco da Gama at CalicutA.D. 1498, Kozhikode, Kerala, India. Vasco da Gama, having rounded the Cape of Good Hope and found a pilot at Malindi, anchored off Calicut in May 1498, ten months out of Lisbon. Asked what they sought, his men answered: Christians and spices. The Zamorin received him coldly, but the sea road from Europe to India was open, and Portuguese cannon would soon command the Arabian Sea.
    • Vasco da Gama at MalindiA.D. 1498, Malindi, Kenya. After skirmishes at Mozambique and an attempted attack at Mombasa, Vasco da Gama's ships found a friendly sultan at Malindi, the rival of Mombasa, who gave him a pilot skilled in the monsoon. In less than a month the pilot brought the fleet across the Indian Ocean to Calicut, and the sea road from Europe to India lay open.
    • Guru Nanak's Callc. A.D. 1499, Sultanpur Lodhi, Punjab, India. Nanak, a keeper of the stores of the Lodi governor at Sultanpur, vanished into the river Bein for three days and returned to proclaim that there was no Hindu and no Muslim. He travelled far preaching devotion to one God, the equality of all, and honest labour, and at Kartarpur gathered the first community of Sikhs. Nine Gurus followed him.
    • Cabral Lands in BrazilA.D. 1500, Porto Seguro, Brazil. Pedro Álvares Cabral, leading thirteen ships to India in Vasco da Gama's wake, swung far west into the Atlantic and on 22 April 1500 sighted a mountain he named Monte Pascoal. He anchored at the bay of Porto Seguro, where on the Sunday after Easter the Franciscan Henrique de Coimbra said the first Mass in the land, and took possession of it for Portugal before sailing on to India.
    • Shah Ismail Crowned at TabrizA.D. 1501, Tabriz, Persia. Ismail, the fourteen-year-old head of the Safavid Sufi order of Ardabil, led his red-capped Qizilbash Turkmen to victory over the Aq Qoyunlu and entered Tabriz in the summer of 1501. Proclaimed shah, he decreed Twelver Shi'ism the faith of his realm and imposed it by the sword on a largely Sunni people. Iran took the religious identity that has set it apart from its neighbors ever since.
