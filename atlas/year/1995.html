    The chronicle of these years

    • The End of the Soviet UnionA.D. 1991, Kremlin, Moscow. On 8 December 1991, at a hunting lodge in the Belovezha Forest, the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus declared that the USSR had ceased to exist and founded a Commonwealth of Independent States. Gorbachev resigned on Christmas Day; that evening the red flag came down over the Kremlin, and fifteen republics stood as independent states.
    • The Rwandan GenocideA.D. 1994, Kigali, Rwanda. When the aircraft of President Juvénal Habyarimana was shot down over Kigali on 6 April 1994, Hutu extremists of the army and the Interahamwe militia began the systematic slaughter of the Tutsi minority and of the Hutu who opposed them, with machetes and with lists prepared in advance. In about a hundred days some 800,000 were killed, until Paul Kagame's Rwandan Patriotic Front took Kigali in July.
    • South Africa VotesA.D. 1994, Pretoria, South Africa. In April 1994 South Africans of every race voted together for the first time, standing for hours in lines that stretched across the veld. The ANC won nearly two-thirds of the votes, and on 10 May Nelson Mandela was inaugurated as president at the Union Buildings in Pretoria, with Thabo Mbeki and F. W. de Klerk as his deputies.
    • The Massacre at SrebrenicaA.D. 1995, Srebrenica, Bosnia. In July 1995 the Bosnian Serb army of General Ratko Mladić overran Srebrenica, a United Nations safe area guarded by a few hundred Dutch peacekeepers. Over the following days it separated the Muslim men and boys from the women and murdered some eight thousand of them in fields and warehouses, the worst massacre in Europe since the Second World War, judged genocide by the international courts.
    • The Taliban Take KabulA.D. 1996, Kabul, Afghanistan. The Taliban, student militias raised among the refugees and religious schools of the Pashtun south, had taken Kandahar in 1994 with Pakistani backing. In September 1996 they entered Kabul, dragged the former president Najibullah from the United Nations compound, and hanged him. Under Mullah Omar they barred girls from school, banned music and television, and sheltered Osama bin Laden.
    • The Handover of Hong KongA.D. 1997, Hong Kong, China. At midnight on 30 June 1997, in a convention center on Hong Kong's harbor, the Union Jack was lowered and the colony taken by Britain in the Opium War passed to the People's Republic of China under the formula of one country, two systems. The Prince of Wales and the last governor, Chris Patten, sailed away on the royal yacht Britannia; the empire's last great colony was gone.
    • The Asian Financial CrisisA.D. 1997, Bangkok, Thailand. On 2 July 1997 Thailand, its reserves drained in defense against speculators, let the baht float, and it soon lost half its value. Panic ran through the borrowed prosperity of the Asian tigers: the rupiah fell by four-fifths, banks and builders failed from Seoul to Jakarta, and the International Monetary Fund imposed its remedies. In Indonesia the crash brought down Suharto within a year.
    • Mobutu FallsA.D. 1997, Kinshasa, Congo. Rwanda and Uganda, pursuing the Hutu killers who had fled into eastern Zaire, armed a rebellion under the veteran guerrilla Laurent-Désiré Kabila that marched across the country in seven months. Mobutu Sese Seko, who had ruled and plundered Zaire for thirty-two years, fled in May 1997; the Second Congo War that followed drew in nine African nations, and millions died of war, hunger, and disease.
