    The chronicle of these years

    • The Treaty of BongayaA.D. 1667, Makassar, Celebes. The sultanate of Gowa at Makassar, a cosmopolitan Muslim port that defied the Dutch spice monopoly, fell to the Company admiral Cornelis Speelman and his Bugis ally Arung Palakka of Bone. By the treaty signed at Bongaya Sultan Hasanuddin, the Rooster of the East, yielded his forts and his trade; the great brick fortress of Somba Opu fell to storm in 1669.
    • Aurangzeb Pulls Down the TemplesA.D. 1669, Varanasi, Uttar Pradesh, India. In 1669 Aurangzeb ordered his governors to demolish the schools and temples of the infidels. That year the Vishvanath temple of Benares was pulled down and a mosque raised on its plinth, and in 1670 the great Keshava Deva temple of Mathura. In 1679 he reimposed the jizya, the poll tax on non-Muslims that Akbar had abolished.
    • The Revolt of the Three FeudatoriesA.D. 1673, Kunming, Yunnan, China. When the young Kangxi Emperor moved to abolish their princedoms, Wu Sangui, the general who had let the Manchus through the wall, rose in Yunnan, joined by the feudatories of Fujian and Guangdong. For eight years the south was at war. Wu proclaimed himself emperor before he died in 1678, and by 1681 Kangxi had crushed the revolt and was master of all China.
    • Shivaji Crowned at RaigadA.D. 1674, Raigad, Maharashtra, India. On 6 June 1674, at his mountain capital of Raigad, Shivaji was crowned Chhatrapati, lord of the royal umbrella, by the Brahmin Gaga Bhatt of Benares, weighed against gold, and enthroned as a sovereign Hindu king. He went on to conquer the Carnatic as far as Gingee and left at his death in 1680 a kingdom the Mughals could never subdue.
    • The Martyrdom of Guru Tegh BahadurA.D. 1675, Chandni Chowk, Delhi, India. Guru Tegh Bahadur, ninth of the Sikh Gurus, took up the cause of the Kashmiri Brahmins who were being pressed to convert, and was arrested and beheaded at Chandni Chowk in Delhi on Aurangzeb's orders when he refused to embrace Islam. The gurdwara of Sis Ganj marks the spot. His son Gobind was not yet nine years old.
    • The Pueblo RevoltA.D. 1680, Santa Fe, New Mexico. Popé, a Tewa medicine man flogged by the Spaniards for sorcery, sent runners bearing knotted cords to the pueblos, and on 10 August 1680 the Pueblo peoples rose together, killing some four hundred settlers and twenty-one Franciscan friars and burning the churches. They besieged Santa Fe and drove the survivors south to El Paso; the Spaniards did not return until Diego de Vargas's reconquest of 1692.
    • The Court at VersaillesA.D. 1682, Versailles, France. On 6 May 1682 Louis XIV made Versailles, his father's hunting lodge transformed by Le Vau, Le Brun, Le Nôtre, and Hardouin-Mansart into the grandest palace in Europe, the seat of his court and government. Thousands of nobles lived there under the king's eye, bound by an etiquette that turned the warrior aristocracy of France into courtiers.
    • The Relief of ViennaA.D. 1683, The Kahlenberg, Vienna. With the Ottoman army of Kara Mustafa at the walls of Vienna, King Jan III Sobieski of Poland leads his winged hussars down the Kahlenberg in one of the greatest cavalry charges in history. The siege is broken, and the Turk will never come so far into Europe again.
