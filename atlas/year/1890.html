    The chronicle of these years

    • Gold on the WitwatersrandA.D. 1886, Johannesburg, South Africa. George Harrison, an Australian prospector, struck the main gold reef on the farm Langlaagte, and within months a camp named Johannesburg spread across the high veld of Paul Kruger's South African Republic. The Rand proved the richest goldfield on earth, and the quarrel between Kruger and its foreign miners, the uitlanders, and their mining magnates led on to the Boer War.
    • The Golden LawA.D. 1888, Rio de Janeiro, Brazil. Brazil, which had received nearly five million enslaved Africans, more than any other land, was the last nation of the Americas to abolish slavery. On 13 May 1888, with her father Pedro II abroad, the Princess Imperial Isabel signed the Golden Law freeing the last seven hundred thousand slaves without compensation; the embittered planters helped overthrow the monarchy the next year.
    • The Trans-Siberian Railway BegunA.D. 1891, Vladivostok, Russia. On 31 May 1891 the Tsarevich Nicholas, returning from a journey to Japan where a policeman had slashed him with a sword, turned the first earth for the Ussuri line at Vladivostok. Driven by Sergei Witte, the Trans-Siberian Railway reached across nearly six thousand miles of steppe and taiga to the Pacific, bound Siberia to Russia, and drew the empire into Manchuria and war with Japan.
    • The Durand LineA.D. 1893, Kabul, Afghanistan. Sir Mortimer Durand, foreign secretary of British India, agreed with the Amir Abdur Rahman Khan at Kabul on a boundary between Afghanistan and the Raj running some 2,600 kilometres through the Pashtun and Baluch hills. It split the Pashtun tribes between two states, and no Afghan government has since accepted it as the border with Pakistan.
    • French Gunboats at PaknamA.D. 1893, Paknam, Siam. Pressing claims to the lands east of the Mekong, France sent two gunboats past the forts at Paknam at the river mouth on 13 July 1893, and they anchored before the Grand Palace. Facing blockade, King Chulalongkorn yielded all Laos east of the Mekong. Siam survived as a buffer between British Burma and French Indochina, but at the price of its outer provinces.
    • Votes for Women in New ZealandA.D. 1893, Wellington, New Zealand. After petitions bearing some thirty-two thousand signatures, gathered by Kate Sheppard and the Women's Christian Temperance Union, the Electoral Act became law on 19 September 1893. New Zealand thus became the first self-governing country in the world to grant women the vote, and that November nearly two-thirds of its adult women went to the polls.
    • The Overthrow of Queen LiliuokalaniA.D. 1893, Honolulu, Hawaii. When Queen Liliuokalani sought a new constitution to restore royal power, a Committee of Safety of American and European sugar men, backed by marines landed from the USS Boston, deposed her on 17 January 1893. The Republic of Hawaii under Sanford Dole crushed a royalist rising in 1895, and in 1898 the United States annexed the islands.
