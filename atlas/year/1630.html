    The chronicle of these years

    • Nzinga's Embassy to LuandaA.D. 1622, Luanda, Angola. Sent by her brother, the ngola of Ndongo, to treat with the Portuguese governor João Correia de Sousa, Nzinga found no chair set for her and sat upon a kneeling servant to meet him as an equal. She was baptized Ana in Luanda; as queen of Ndongo and then Matamba from 1624 until her death in 1663, she fought the Portuguese for thirty years and allied herself with the Dutch.
    • Sultan Agung Besieges BataviaA.D. 1628, Batavia, Java. Sultan Agung of Mataram, master of nearly all Java, sent his armies against the Dutch at Batavia in 1628 and again in 1629. The Javanese dug siegeworks and dammed the river, but the Dutch burned their rice stores along the coast, and famine and disease wasted the besiegers. Coen died of sickness during the second siege, yet Batavia held.
    • The Sack of MagdeburgA.D. 1631, Magdeburg, Saxony. On 20 May 1631 the imperial army of Tilly and Pappenheim stormed Magdeburg, the Protestant city that had defied the emperor. Fire swept the town and soldiers butchered its people; of perhaps 25,000 inhabitants some 20,000 perished, and little but the cathedral stood. Magdeburg quarter became the cry of Protestant soldiers who would give no mercy.
    • Gustavus Adolphus Falls at LützenA.D. 1632, Lützen, Saxony. Victor at Breitenfeld the year before, Gustavus Adolphus met Wallenstein's army near Lützen on 16 November 1632, in fog and gunsmoke. Leading a cavalry charge, the king was lost in the mist and shot dead. His men fought on and drove Wallenstein from the field, but the Protestant cause had lost its champion, and Sweden passed to his daughter Christina, not yet six.
    • The Taj Mahal BegunA.D. 1632, Agra, Uttar Pradesh, India. When his empress Mumtaz Mahal died bearing their fourteenth child in 1631, Shah Jahan began on the Yamuna at Agra a tomb of white marble inlaid with precious stones. Some twenty thousand workmen laboured on it for two decades, and the domed mausoleum in its garden became the crown of Mughal architecture.
    • Hong Taiji Proclaims the QingA.D. 1636, Mukden, Liaoning, China. Hong Taiji, son of Nurhaci, having won the imperial seal of the Yuan from the heirs of Ligdan Khan of the Chahar, renamed his people Manchu and his dynasty Qing, the Pure, and took the title of emperor at Mukden. That winter his armies overran Joseon, and King Injo knelt to him at Samjeondo. The Ming now faced a rival empire beyond the wall.
    • Fasilides Builds GondarA.D. 1636, Gondar, Ethiopia. Having expelled the Jesuits, whose Latin rites his father Susenyos had imposed at the cost of civil war, the emperor Fasilides ended the wandering of the royal camp and founded a fixed capital at Gondar, north of Lake Tana. There he and his heirs raised castles of stone with battlements and towers and dozens of churches; Gondar remained the capital for more than two centuries.
