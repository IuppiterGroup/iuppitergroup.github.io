    The chronicle of these years

    • The Great Bengal FamineA.D. 1770, Murshidabad, West Bengal, India. After the rains failed in 1769, famine swept Bengal and Bihar. The Company's servants, newly masters of the revenue, kept up their collections, and hoarders of grain made fortunes. Warren Hastings reckoned that a third of the people perished, perhaps ten million souls, and great stretches of the delta fell back into jungle.
    • Cook at Botany BayA.D. 1770, Botany Bay, Australia. Cook, having charted New Zealand, came upon the unknown east coast of Australia and anchored in April 1770 in a bay Joseph Banks found so rich in plants that it was named Botany Bay. Sailing north, Cook nearly lost his ship on the Great Barrier Reef, and on Possession Island in August claimed the whole eastern coast for George III.
    • The Tay Son RebellionA.D. 1771, Tay Son, Vietnam. Three brothers of Tay Son village, Nguyen Nhac, Nguyen Hue, and Nguyen Lu, rose against the corrupt regent of the Nguyen lords, promising to take from the rich and give to the poor. They seized Quy Nhon in 1773, and when the Trinh invaded from the north the Nguyen house was crushed between them; by 1777 only the youth Nguyen Anh survived.
    • The First Partition of PolandA.D. 1772, St. Petersburg, Russia. On 5 August 1772 Russia, Prussia, and Austria signed at St. Petersburg a treaty taking nearly a third of the lands of the Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth, whose liberum veto and foreign intrigues had left it defenseless. Frederick the Great took West Prussia, joining his scattered domains; of Maria Theresa he said that she wept, but she took.
    • The Treaty of Küçük KaynarcaA.D. 1774, Küçük Kaynarca, Bulgaria. After crushing defeats on land and sea, Sultan Abdul Hamid I made peace with Catherine the Great at Küçük Kaynarca on 21 July 1774. The Crimean Khanate, Muslim and an Ottoman vassal for three centuries, was declared independent, Russia gained ports on the Black Sea, and the empress won a right to speak for the Orthodox Christians of the sultan's realm. Russia annexed the Crimea in 1783.
    • The Declaration of IndependenceA.D. 1776, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania. Meeting in the State House at Philadelphia, the Second Continental Congress voted on 2 July 1776 that the thirteen colonies were free and independent states, and on 4 July adopted the Declaration drafted by Thomas Jefferson, which held that all men are created equal and endowed by their Creator with unalienable rights. It founded the first great modern republic, and its words echoed through every revolution that followed.
    • Death of Captain CookA.D. 1779, Kealakekua Bay, Hawaii. Cook, who had come upon the Hawaiian Islands in 1778, returned to Kealakekua Bay during the Makahiki festival and was received with the highest honors. Forced back weeks later by a broken mast, his men quarreled with the islanders over a stolen boat, and on 14 February 1779 Cook was killed on the shore while trying to take the chief Kalaniopuu hostage.
    • The Rebellion of Túpac Amaru IIA.D. 1780, Tinta, Peru. José Gabriel Condorcanqui, a mestizo cacique and muleteer descended from the last Inca, took the name Túpac Amaru II, seized and hanged the corregidor Antonio de Arriaga at Tinta in November 1780, and raised the southern Andes against the forced sale of goods and the mita. Captured in 1781, he was made to watch the execution of his wife Micaela Bastidas and was then torn by horses and beheaded in the square of Cusco.
