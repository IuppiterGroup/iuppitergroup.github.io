    The chronicle of these years

    • The Battle of the NationsA.D. 1813, Leipzig, Saxony. For four days in October 1813 the armies of Russia, Prussia, Austria, and Sweden closed upon Napoleon around Leipzig in the greatest battle Europe had yet seen, more than half a million men in all. The Saxons went over to the allies in the field; on 19 October a sapper blew the Elster bridge too soon, drowning Marshal Poniatowski and trapping the rearguard. Napoleon's German empire collapsed.
    • Abdication at FontainebleauA.D. 1814, Fontainebleau, France. With the allies in Paris and his marshals refusing to march, Napoleon abdicated unconditionally at Fontainebleau on 6 April 1814. On 20 April he bade farewell to the Old Guard in the Court of the White Horse and left for Elba, a sovereign island of some twelve thousand people, while Louis XVIII returned to the throne of his fathers.
    • The Constitution of EidsvollA.D. 1814, Eidsvoll, Norway. Ceded by Denmark to Sweden at Kiel in January 1814, the Norwegians refused the bargain; 112 delegates met at the manor of Eidsvoll and on 17 May signed a liberal constitution, electing the Danish prince Christian Frederick king. A short war with Bernadotte followed, and Norway accepted the Swedish king that autumn, but it kept its constitution, which remains in force today.
    • WaterlooA.D. 1815, Brabant, Belgium. Wellington’s squares hold through a long June afternoon, Blücher’s Prussians fall on the French flank, and Napoleon’s Imperial Guard breaks. The Emperor abdicates and is sent to St. Helena. Europe settles into a century without a general war.
    • The Final Act of the Congress of ViennaA.D. 1815, Vienna, Austria. After nine months of negotiation, balls, and intrigue led by Metternich, Castlereagh, Talleyrand, Hardenberg, and Tsar Alexander I, the powers signed the Final Act on 9 June 1815, nine days before Waterloo. Russia took most of Poland, Prussia three fifths of Saxony's land and the Rhineland, Austria Lombardy and Venetia; a German Confederation replaced the Empire, and the balance struck at Vienna kept Europe from general war for a century.
    • The British Take KandyA.D. 1815, Kandy, Sri Lanka. The chiefs of Kandy, the last independent kingdom of Lanka, turned against their cruel king Sri Vikrama Rajasinha and opened the way to a British force. By the Kandyan Convention of March 1815 they ceded the kingdom to the British crown, which pledged to protect the Buddhist faith. For the first time the whole of Ceylon lay under one government.
    • The Eruption of TamboraA.D. 1815, Mount Tambora, Sumbawa. In April 1815 Mount Tambora on Sumbawa blew itself apart in the greatest eruption of recorded history, its roar heard in Sumatra, more than a thousand miles away. The little kingdoms of Tambora and Sanggar vanished, and tens of thousands died by fire and famine. The veil it cast around the earth brought the Year Without a Summer of 1816, with frost and hunger in Europe and New England.
    • San Martín Crosses the AndesA.D. 1817, Chacabuco, Chile. José de San Martín led the Army of the Andes, some five thousand men with mules and cannon, over passes higher than twelve thousand feet from Mendoza into Chile in January 1817, and with Bernardo O'Higgins routed the royalists at Chacabuco on 12 February. A year later the victory of Maipú made Chile free, and San Martín turned north by sea against the Spanish citadel of Peru.
