    The chronicle of these years

    • Buddhism Reaches YamatoA.D. 538, Asuka, Nara, Japan. King Seong of Baekje, seeking allies against Silla, sent the Yamato court a gilt bronze image of the Buddha and sutras, in 538 by one account and 552 by the Nihon Shoki. The Soga clan took up the new faith while the Mononobe and Nakatomi opposed it, and the quarrel ended in 587, when Soga no Umako destroyed the Mononobe in battle.
    • Belisarius Enters RavennaA.D. 540, Ravenna, Italy. Belisarius, who had landed in Sicily in 535 and held Rome through a year's siege by the Goths, blockaded Ravenna until the starving Ostrogoths offered to make him emperor of the West. He feigned assent, entered the city, and took King Witiges captive to Constantinople. The Gothic war was not over: under Totila it would rage for twelve more years.
    • Khosrow Sacks AntiochA.D. 540, Antioch, Syria. Khosrow I broke the Eternal Peace while Justinian's armies were in Italy, took Antioch, the great city of the East, plundered and burned it, and bathed in the Mediterranean. He carried its people to Mesopotamia and built for them a new city near Ctesiphon, Khosrow's Better Antioch, with baths and a hippodrome.
    • The Plague of JustinianA.D. 541, Pelusium, Egypt. Bubonic plague appeared at Pelusium in Egypt and spread along the sea lanes to Constantinople, where in the spring of 542, Procopius says, it killed up to ten thousand a day, and Justinian himself fell sick. It returned again and again for two centuries, emptying cities and armies from Persia to Ireland.
    • Ly Bi Proclaims Van XuanA.D. 544, Long Bien, Vietnam. Ly Bi, a landowner of mixed Chinese and native descent, drove the Liang governor out of Jiaozhou and in 544 proclaimed himself Emperor Ly Nam De of Van Xuan, the Kingdom of Ten Thousand Springs. Liang armies under Chen Baxian broke him within two years, but his heirs held out in the marshes and hills until the Sui came in 602.
    • Chenla Overruns Funanc. A.D. 550, Ba Phnom, Cambodia. Chenla, a vassal kingdom of Khmer speakers on the middle Mekong, turned on its overlord. Under Bhavavarman and his kinsman Chitrasena its armies pressed south and took the old royal hill of Ba Phnom, and the Funan court withdrew toward the coast. Chinese annals record that Chenla swallowed Funan, and with it the delta plains and the Indian learning of its court.
    • Bumin Rises Against the RouranA.D. 552, Altai Mountains, Mongolia. The Türks, ironworkers in the service of the Rouran, were refused a royal bride by the khagan Anagui, who called their chief Bumin a mere blacksmith slave. Bumin allied with Western Wei, routed the Rouran, drove Anagui to suicide, and took the title Il Qaghan. Within a generation his people ruled from Manchuria to the Black Sea.
    • The Turks and Persians Destroy the Hephthalitesc. A.D. 557, Bukhara, Uzbekistan. Khosrow I of Persia allied with Istami, yabghu of the newly risen Turks, and the two powers closed on the Hephthalites from south and north. Near Bukhara the Turks shattered the Hephthalite host under its king Ghadfar, and the realm of the White Huns was divided between the victors along the Oxus. Sogdia passed under the overlordship of the Turkic khaganate.
