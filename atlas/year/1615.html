    The chronicle of these years

    • Galileo's Starry MessengerA.D. 1610, Padua, Italy. Turning a telescope of his own making on the heavens from Padua, Galileo Galilei saw the mountains of the moon, the countless stars of the Milky Way, and four moons circling Jupiter, and published them in March 1610 in the Sidereus Nuncius. His later defense of the motion of the earth brought him before the Roman Inquisition in 1633, where he abjured it.
    • Minin and Pozharsky Free MoscowA.D. 1612, Kremlin, Moscow. In the Time of Troubles a Polish army had routed the Russians at Klushino in 1610 and occupied the Kremlin. Kuzma Minin, a meat trader of Nizhny Novgorod, raised money and men, and Prince Dmitry Pozharsky led the people's levy to Moscow. In November 1612 the starving Polish garrison surrendered, and the next year an assembly of the land chose Michael Romanov as tsar.
    • The Defenestration of PragueA.D. 1618, Prague Castle, Prague. On 23 May 1618 Protestant nobles of Bohemia, enraged at breaches of the royal charter of religious liberty, threw two Catholic regents of the emperor and their secretary from a window of Prague Castle. All three survived the fall of some seventy feet. The estates chose the Calvinist Frederick of the Palatinate as king, and the Thirty Years' War began.
    • Coen Founds BataviaA.D. 1619, Batavia, Java. Jan Pieterszoon Coen, governor-general of the Dutch East India Company, stormed Jayakarta on 30 May 1619, burned the town, and on its ashes built Batavia, a walled city of canals on the Dutch model. 'Despair not, spare not your enemies,' he wrote; from Batavia the Company would rule the trade of the Indies, and in time the islands themselves, for nearly two hundred years.
    • The Battle of SarhūA.D. 1619, Sarhū, Liaoning, China. Nurhaci, who had united the Jurchen tribes and in 1616 proclaimed the Later Jin, faced four Ming columns marching on his capital. Concentrating his banners against each in turn, he destroyed three of them in five days around Sarhū. Two years later he took Liaoyang and Shenyang, and the Manchu conquest of the northeast had begun.
    • The Battle of the White MountainA.D. 1620, White Mountain, Prague. On 8 November 1620 the Catholic League under Tilly and the imperial army routed the army of the Bohemian estates on the White Mountain outside Prague in less than two hours. Frederick, the Winter King, fled. Twenty-seven leaders of the revolt were executed in the Old Town Square, the Protestant nobility was dispossessed, and Bohemia was brought back to the Catholic faith by force.
    • The Pilgrims at PlymouthA.D. 1620, Plymouth, Massachusetts. The Mayflower, carrying 102 passengers, among them Separatists who had fled England for Leiden, made landfall at Cape Cod in November 1620, and before going ashore the men signed the Mayflower Compact, binding themselves into a 'civil body politic.' At Plymouth half of them died in the first winter, but with the help of Squanto and the Wampanoag sachem Massasoit the colony endured.
    • The Conquest of the Banda IslandsA.D. 1621, Banda Neira, Moluccas. Determined to hold the world's only source of nutmeg and mace, Coen landed on the Banda Islands with some two thousand men and Japanese mercenaries. Of perhaps fifteen thousand Bandanese only about a thousand remained after war, massacre, starvation, and deportation as slaves to Batavia, and the Company parceled the islands among Dutch planters worked by slaves.
