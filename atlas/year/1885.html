    The chronicle of these years

    • The British at Tel el-KebirA.D. 1882, Tel el-Kebir, Egypt. Colonel Ahmed Urabi led a movement of Egyptian officers against the khedive Tewfik and the European controllers of Egypt's debts. After riots in Alexandria, the British bombarded the city, landed an army on the Suez Canal, and on 13 September 1882 stormed Urabi's lines at Tel el-Kebir after a night march. Britain occupied Egypt for seventy-four years.
    • The Eruption of KrakatoaA.D. 1883, Krakatoa, Sunda Strait. On 27 August 1883 the volcano of Krakatoa in the Sunda Strait destroyed itself in four tremendous explosions, the last heard on Rodrigues in the Indian Ocean, three thousand miles away. Its waves, more than thirty meters high, swept away 165 villages on the coasts of Java and Sumatra and some 36,000 people; the telegraph carried the news around the world within hours.
    • The Berlin Conference on AfricaA.D. 1884, Wilhelmstrasse, Berlin. From November 1884 to February 1885 the delegates of fourteen powers met in Bismarck's residence on the Wilhelmstrasse to settle the rules for claiming Africa. The General Act required effective occupation of coasts claimed, declared free trade on the Congo and the Niger, and in effect recognized King Leopold II's International Association as the Congo Free State, the King's personal domain.
    • The Indian National CongressA.D. 1885, Mumbai, Maharashtra, India. Seventy-two delegates, lawyers, journalists, and teachers from every province, gathered at Gokuldas Tejpal College in Bombay in December 1885 at the call of the retired civil servant Allan Octavian Hume, and founded the Indian National Congress. Moderate and loyal at first, it became the vehicle of the movement that won India its independence.
    • Ham Nghi and the Can VuongA.D. 1885, Hue, Vietnam. The treaties of 1883 and 1884 made Annam and Tonkin French protectorates. In July 1885 the regent Ton That Thuyet attacked the French garrison at Hue and fled to the hills with the boy emperor Ham Nghi, who called on the scholars to rise in the Can Vuong, Aid the King. Bands of the movement slaughtered thousands of Vietnamese Catholics before Ham Nghi was betrayed in 1888.
    • Thibaw DeposedA.D. 1885, Mandalay, Burma. King Thibaw's court fined a British timber company and sought friendship with France. In November 1885 a British force steamed up the Irrawaddy and took Mandalay almost without a fight, and Thibaw and Queen Supayalat were carried off to exile in India. On 1 January 1886 Upper Burma was annexed and joined to British India, and a long guerrilla war followed.
    • The Fall of KhartoumA.D. 1885, Khartoum, Sudan. Muhammad Ahmad, a Dongolawi holy man who had proclaimed himself the Mahdi, the redeemer awaited by Muslims, raised the Sudan against its Egyptian masters and destroyed every army sent against him. After a siege of ten months his ansar stormed Khartoum on 26 January 1885 and slew General Charles Gordon at the governor's palace, two days before British relief steamers came in sight.
    • Gold on the WitwatersrandA.D. 1886, Johannesburg, South Africa. George Harrison, an Australian prospector, struck the main gold reef on the farm Langlaagte, and within months a camp named Johannesburg spread across the high veld of Paul Kruger's South African Republic. The Rand proved the richest goldfield on earth, and the quarrel between Kruger and its foreign miners, the uitlanders, and their mining magnates led on to the Boer War.
