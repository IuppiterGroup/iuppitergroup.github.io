    The chronicle of these years

    • Peter Founds St. PetersburgA.D. 1703, Hare Island, St. Petersburg. On 27 May 1703, on land newly taken from Sweden at the mouth of the Neva, Peter the Great laid the foundations of the fortress of St. Peter and St. Paul on Hare Island. Built in the marshes by conscripted laborers, thousands of whom died, the new city became Russia's capital in 1712, its window on Europe.
    • Marlborough at BlenheimA.D. 1704, Blindheim, Bavaria. On 13 August 1704 the Duke of Marlborough and Prince Eugene, having marched from the Netherlands to the Danube, fell on the French and Bavarian army at Blenheim. Marshal Tallard was taken, and some 30,000 of his men were killed, wounded, or captured. Vienna was saved, Bavaria knocked out of the war, and the legend of Louis XIV's invincible armies broken.
    • The Union of England and ScotlandA.D. 1707, Edinburgh, Scotland. On 16 January 1707 the Scottish Parliament ratified the Treaty of Union, amid the riots of the Edinburgh mob, many members swayed by compensation for the losses of the Darien scheme. On 1 May the kingdom of Great Britain came into being, with one Parliament at Westminster, while Scotland kept its own Church and its own law.
    • The Death of AurangzebA.D. 1707, Ahmednagar, Maharashtra, India. Aurangzeb died at Ahmadnagar in his eighty-ninth year, after a quarter century of war in the Deccan against the Marathas, leaving the treasury drained and the empire seething with revolt. His sons fought for the throne, and within a generation the governors of Bengal, Awadh, and the Deccan were masters in all but name. He was buried in a plain grave near Ellora.
    • Peter the Great at PoltavaA.D. 1709, Poltava, Ukraine. Charles XII, marching on Moscow, had turned south into Ukraine to join the Cossack hetman Ivan Mazepa. On 8 July 1709, wounded and carried in a litter, he flung his winter-wasted and hungry army against Peter's redoubts at Poltava. The Swedes were destroyed, the survivors surrendered at Perevolochna, and Charles fled to the Turks. Russia had replaced Sweden as the power of the North.
    • The Peace of UtrechtA.D. 1713, Utrecht, Netherlands. The treaties signed at Utrecht in April 1713 ended the War of the Spanish Succession. Louis XIV's grandson kept Spain and the Indies as Philip V, on condition that the French and Spanish crowns never unite; Austria would take the Spanish Netherlands, Milan, and Naples; Britain won Gibraltar, Minorca, and the slave-trading contract to Spanish America; and Savoy received Sicily with a royal crown.
