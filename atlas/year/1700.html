    The chronicle of these years

    • Zumbi of PalmaresA.D. 1695, Serra da Barriga, Brazil. For most of the seventeenth century runaway slaves held Palmares, a league of fortified villages in the hills of Alagoas that numbered many thousands and beat off expedition after expedition. In 1694 bandeirantes under Domingos Jorge Velho stormed its citadel on the Serra da Barriga; its last war leader, Zumbi, was betrayed and killed on 20 November 1695, and his head was displayed in Recife.
    • The Last Maya Kingdom FallsA.D. 1697, Flores, Guatemala. For more than a century and a half after the conquest of Yucatán, the Itza Maya held their island capital of Nojpetén in Lake Petén Itzá, keeping their books and gods and turning away the friars. On 13 March 1697 Martín de Urzúa y Arizmendi attacked by water with a galley built on the lake and took the city; the king Kan Ek' was captured, and the last independent Maya kingdom was no more.
    • The Omanis Take Fort JesusA.D. 1698, Mombasa, Kenya. After a siege of thirty-three months, in which plague and hunger reduced the Portuguese garrison to a handful, the Arabs of Oman under the imam Saif ibn Sultan stormed Fort Jesus, the great bastion the Portuguese had built at Mombasa in 1593. Portuguese power north of Mozambique was broken, and the Swahili coast passed under the lordship of Oman.
    • The Treaty of KarlowitzA.D. 1699, Karlowitz, Syrmia. After the defeat before Vienna and sixteen years of war with the Holy League, ending in Prince Eugene's victory at Zenta, the Ottomans signed peace at Karlowitz on 26 January 1699. Hungary and Transylvania went to Austria, the Morea to Venice, and Podolia to Poland. For the first time the empire of the sultans yielded great provinces by treaty, and its long retreat from Europe began.
    • The Founding of the KhalsaA.D. 1699, Anandpur Sahib, Punjab, India. At the spring festival of Baisakhi, Guru Gobind Singh asked before a great assembly at Anandpur who would give his head for the faith. Five men stepped forward, and with them he founded the Khalsa, the order of the pure, baptized with sweetened water stirred by a double-edged sword, bound to keep the five Ks and to bear arms.
    • The Golden StoolA.D. 1701, Kumasi, Ghana. Osei Tutu, king of Kumasi, united the Akan chiefdoms of the forest and broke their overlords of Denkyira at the battle of Feyiase. Tradition tells that his priest Okomfo Anokye called down from the sky a stool of gold that held the soul of the Asante nation; the union grew into an empire that sold gold and captives to the Europeans on the coast for two centuries.
    • Peter Founds St. PetersburgA.D. 1703, Hare Island, St. Petersburg. On 27 May 1703, on land newly taken from Sweden at the mouth of the Neva, Peter the Great laid the foundations of the fortress of St. Peter and St. Paul on Hare Island. Built in the marshes by conscripted laborers, thousands of whom died, the new city became Russia's capital in 1712, its window on Europe.
    • Marlborough at BlenheimA.D. 1704, Blindheim, Bavaria. On 13 August 1704 the Duke of Marlborough and Prince Eugene, having marched from the Netherlands to the Danube, fell on the French and Bavarian army at Blenheim. Marshal Tallard was taken, and some 30,000 of his men were killed, wounded, or captured. Vienna was saved, Bavaria knocked out of the war, and the legend of Louis XIV's invincible armies broken.
