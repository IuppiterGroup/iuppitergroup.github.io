    The chronicle of these years

    • SarajevoA.D. 1914, Sarajevo, Bosnia. On the twenty-eighth of June, Gavrilo Princip shoots Archduke Franz Ferdinand, heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, in the streets of Sarajevo. Within six weeks the great powers of Europe are at war, and four empires will not survive it.
    • The Germans Invade BelgiumA.D. 1914, Liège, Belgium. Following the Schlieffen plan to defeat France by a wheel through the Low Countries, German armies crossed the Belgian frontier on 4 August 1914, and Britain, guarantor of Belgian neutrality, declared war that night. The forts of Liège held for twelve days until crushed by Krupp and Škoda siege howitzers, and at Louvain on 25 August the Germans burned the university library.
    • TannenbergA.D. 1914, Tannenberg, East Prussia. Russia invaded East Prussia sooner than Germany expected. Recalled from retirement, General Paul von Hindenburg, with Erich Ludendorff as chief of staff, fell on Samsonov's Second Army in the forests and lakes of Masuria from 26 to 30 August 1914, encircling it and taking some 92,000 prisoners. Samsonov shot himself; the victory was named for the field where the Teutonic Knights had fallen in 1410.
    • The Miracle of the MarneA.D. 1914, Meaux, France. With the German right wing wheeling east of Paris, General Joffre turned his retreating armies on 6 September 1914 and struck its exposed flank along the Marne, while General Gallieni sent reinforcements from Paris in requisitioned taxicabs. A gap opened between the German First and Second Armies; on 9 September Moltke's staff ordered retreat to the Aisne, and the war of movement in the west ended in trenches.
    • The Landings at GallipoliA.D. 1915, Anzac Cove, Gallipoli. To force the Dardanelles and knock Turkey out of the war, as Winston Churchill urged, British, French, Australian, and New Zealand troops landed on the Gallipoli peninsula on 25 April 1915. The Turks, with Mustafa Kemal commanding above Anzac Cove, held the heights there and at Cape Helles; after eight months and over a quarter of a million casualties on each side, the allies withdrew in January 1916.
    • The Armenian GenocideA.D. 1915, Deir ez-Zor, Syria. On the night of 24 April 1915 the Young Turk government arrested the Armenian leaders of Constantinople, and in the months that followed Armenian men across Anatolia were massacred and the women, children, and old driven on death marches into the Syrian desert toward Deir ez-Zor. Assyrian and Greek Christians suffered a like fate. Most historians reckon the Armenian dead at a million or more.
    • The Bolsheviks Seize PetrogradA.D. 1917, Winter Palace, Petrograd. On the night of 7 November 1917, the October Revolution by the Russian calendar, the Red Guards and soldiers of the Military Revolutionary Committee directed by Trotsky occupied the bridges, stations, and telegraph of Petrograd and took the Winter Palace, arresting Kerensky's ministers. Lenin proclaimed Soviet power, peace, and land to the peasants, and founded the first communist state.
    • The Treaty of VersaillesA.D. 1919, Hall of Mirrors, Versailles. In the Hall of Mirrors where the German Empire had been proclaimed, the German delegates signed on 28 June 1919, five years to the day after Sarajevo, the treaty dictated by Clemenceau, Lloyd George, and Wilson. Germany lost Alsace-Lorraine, its colonies, and much of its eastern provinces to Poland, accepted responsibility for the war, and was disarmed and burdened with reparations; the Covenant of the League of Nations stood at its head.
