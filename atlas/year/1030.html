    The chronicle of these years

    • The Norse in VinlandA.D. 1021, L'Anse aux Meadows, Newfoundland. Some twenty years after Leif Eriksson, son of Erik the Red, sailed from Greenland to a land of grapes and timber he called Vinland, Norsemen at L'Anse aux Meadows on the northern tip of Newfoundland felled trees with iron axes; the tree rings date their cutting to 1021. Their turf halls, found in 1960, are the only proven Norse settlement in the Americas outside Greenland, nearly five centuries before Columbus.
    • Rajendra Brings the Ganges Southc. A.D. 1023, Gangaikonda Cholapuram, Tamil Nadu, India. Rajendra I sent his armies north through Kalinga to the Ganges, where they defeated the Pala king Mahipala. Waters of the holy river were carried south in vessels to fill a great tank at his new capital, Gangaikonda Cholapuram, the city of the Chola who took the Ganges, crowned with a temple rivalling his father's at Thanjavur.
    • Rajendra Chola Strikes SrivijayaA.D. 1025, Kadaram, Kedah. Rajendra I, the Chola emperor of south India, sent a fleet across the Bay of Bengal that fell on the ports of Srivijaya, Palembang, Malaiyur, Tamralinga, and Kadaram among them, as his inscriptions boast. King Sangrama Vijayottungavarman was taken with his treasures and the jeweled gate of his city. Srivijaya never wholly recovered its grip on the Straits.
    • Mahmud Sacks SomnathA.D. 1026, Somnath, Gujarat, India. In midwinter Mahmud of Ghazni crossed the Thar desert with thirty thousand horse to Somnath on the coast of Gujarat, the most celebrated temple of Shiva in India. Its defenders fought on the walls for two days; tens of thousands, the chroniclers say, were slain. Mahmud had the lingam broken and its pieces laid at the steps of the mosque of Ghazni to be trodden underfoot.
    • Al-Biruni Writes on Indiac. A.D. 1030, Ghazni, Afghanistan. Al-Biruni of Khwarazm, carried to Ghazni in Mahmud's train, learned Sanskrit and wrote his Kitab al-Hind, a sober account of Indian religion, philosophy, astronomy, and custom without equal in medieval literature. He noted coolly that Mahmud's raids had utterly ruined the prosperity of the country and left the Hindus cherishing the most inveterate aversion toward all Muslims.
    • The Caliphate of Córdoba AbolishedA.D. 1031, Córdoba, Spain. After two decades of civil war, in which Berber and Slav soldiers set up and pulled down caliphs and sacked Córdoba and Madinat al-Zahra, the notables of Córdoba deposed Hisham III in 1031 and declared the caliphate at an end. Al-Andalus broke into some two dozen taifa kingdoms, rich in poets and palaces and weak in arms, which soon paid tribute to the Christian kings.
    • Li Yuanhao Proclaims the Great XiaA.D. 1038, Xingqing, Ningxia, China. Li Yuanhao, chief of the Tangut people of the Ordos and Gansu, threw off his Song titles, ordered his subjects to shave their crowns, had a script of his own devised for the Tangut tongue, and proclaimed himself emperor of the Great Xia at Xingqing. His armies beat the Song three times in as many years, and the Western Xia endured for nearly two centuries.
    • The First Gunpowder FormulasA.D. 1044, Kaifeng, Henan, China. The Complete Essentials for the Military Classics, compiled for Emperor Renzong by Zeng Gongliang and Ding Du, set down the earliest known recipes for gunpowder, mixing saltpetre, sulphur, and charcoal for fire arrows, smoke balls, and bombs hurled by catapult. Over the next three centuries the invention passed to the Mongols, the Islamic world, and Europe, and it overturned the castles and armies of the earth.
