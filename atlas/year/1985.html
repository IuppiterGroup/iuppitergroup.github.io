    The chronicle of these years

    • Black JulyA.D. 1983, Colombo, Sri Lanka. After the Tamil Tigers ambushed and killed thirteen soldiers near Jaffna, Sinhalese mobs in Colombo and across the island hunted Tamils through July 1983, burning their homes and shops and murdering hundreds, while some police and soldiers looked on. Thousands of young Tamils joined the separatist militants, and the civil war began that would last twenty-six years.
    • Operation Blue StarA.D. 1984, Amritsar, Punjab, India. In June 1984 the Indian army stormed the Golden Temple at Amritsar to dislodge the armed Sikh militant Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale and his followers, who had fortified the shrine. The militants and hundreds of pilgrims died, and the Akal Takht was wrecked. On 31 October two of Indira Gandhi's Sikh bodyguards shot her dead, and mobs in Delhi slaughtered thousands of Sikhs in reprisal.
    • The Bhopal DisasterA.D. 1984, Bhopal, Madhya Pradesh, India. On the night of 2 to 3 December 1984 some forty tonnes of methyl isocyanate gas escaped from the Union Carbide pesticide plant at Bhopal and drifted over the sleeping slums around it. Thousands died within days, and many thousands more in the years after, in the worst industrial disaster in history.
    • Gorbachev Comes to PowerA.D. 1985, Kremlin, Moscow. On 11 March 1985, after the deaths of three ailing leaders in three years, the Politburo chose Mikhail Gorbachev, at fifty-four its youngest member, as general secretary. He preached glasnost and perestroika, openness and restructuring, to save the Soviet system, withdrew from Afghanistan, and at summits with Ronald Reagan agreed to scrap intermediate-range nuclear missiles.
    • The Chernobyl DisasterA.D. 1986, Chernobyl, Ukraine. In the early hours of 26 April 1986, during a botched safety test, reactor number four of the Chernobyl power station north of Kiev exploded and burned, throwing radioactive dust across Ukraine, Belorussia, and Europe. The town of Pripyat was evacuated only after thirty-six hours, and a zone of some thirty kilometers around the plant was emptied of its people.
    • People Power on EDSAA.D. 1986, EDSA, Manila. The murder of the opposition leader Benigno Aquino on the airport tarmac in 1983 turned the Philippines against Ferdinand Marcos, who had ruled under martial law from 1972. When Marcos proclaimed himself the victor of the snap election of February 1986 over Aquino's widow Corazon, army rebels mutinied, Cardinal Sin called the faithful into the streets, and a million unarmed people shielded them; Marcos fled to Hawaii.
    • The Renovation of VietnamA.D. 1986, Hanoi, Vietnam. With inflation running at hundreds of percent and its people hungry, the Communist Party of Vietnam at its Sixth Congress in December 1986 chose Nguyen Van Linh to lead and proclaimed Doi Moi, Renovation. Collective farming was abandoned and private trade and foreign investment welcomed, and within a few years Vietnam turned from importing rice to being one of the world's great rice exporters.
    • The Fall of the Berlin WallA.D. 1989, Bornholmer Strasse, Berlin. After a summer of flight through Hungary and an autumn of mass demonstrations in Leipzig, the East German party spokesman Günter Schabowski announced on the evening of 9 November 1989, by mistake, that citizens might cross the border at once. Crowds massed at the checkpoints; at Bornholmer Strasse the guards opened the gate before midnight, and Berliners danced on the Wall.
