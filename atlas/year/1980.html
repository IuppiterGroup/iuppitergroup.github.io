    The chronicle of these years

    • A Polish PopeA.D. 1978, St. Peter's Square, Vatican City. On 16 October 1978 the cardinals elected Karol Wojtyła, Archbishop of Kraków, the first non-Italian pope in more than four and a half centuries, who took the name John Paul II. When he returned to Poland in June 1979, millions came to his Masses, and in Warsaw's Victory Square he prayed that the Spirit would renew the face of the earth, the face of this land. A year later Solidarity was born.
    • The Pact of XiaogangA.D. 1978, Xiaogang, Anhui, China. In the winter of 1978 eighteen farmers of the hungry village of Xiaogang secretly divided the collective fields into family plots, pressing their thumbprints on the pact. A few weeks later the party's Third Plenum confirmed Deng Xiaoping's ascendancy and turned the state toward reform and opening. The communes were dissolved within five years, and a generation of growth followed.
    • The Iranian RevolutionA.D. 1979, Tehran, Iran. After a year of mass demonstrations, Mohammad Reza Shah left Iran on 16 January 1979, and on 1 February the Ayatollah Ruhollah Khomeini returned from fifteen years of exile to a welcome of millions. The monarchy fell within days, and by referendum Iran became an Islamic Republic under the guardianship of the jurist. In November student militants seized the American embassy and held fifty-two hostages for 444 days.
    • The Soviet Invasion of AfghanistanA.D. 1979, Kabul, Afghanistan. On 24 December 1979 Soviet airborne troops began landing at Kabul, and on the 27th special forces stormed the Tajbeg Palace, killed the communist ruler Hafizullah Amin, and installed Babrak Karmal. Some hundred thousand Soviet troops fought for nine years against the mujahideen, armed by America, Pakistan, and Saudi Arabia; about a million Afghans died and five million fled abroad.
    • Vietnam Overthrows the Khmer RougeA.D. 1979, Phnom Penh, Cambodia. Provoked by Khmer Rouge massacres in its border provinces, Vietnam invaded Cambodia at Christmas 1978 and took Phnom Penh on 7 January 1979, setting up a government under Heng Samrin. The world now saw the mass graves and the prison of Tuol Sleng, where some fourteen thousand had been tortured and killed. Pol Pot's army withdrew to the Thai border to fight on.
    • Solidarity at the Gdańsk ShipyardA.D. 1980, Lenin Shipyard, Gdańsk. Striking over the dismissal of a crane operator, Anna Walentynowicz, the workers of the Lenin Shipyard at Gdańsk were led from August 1980 by the electrician Lech Wałęsa; on 31 August the government signed an agreement allowing free trade unions. Solidarity grew to some ten million members before General Jaruzelski declared martial law on 13 December 1981 and interned its leaders.
    • The Iran-Iraq WarA.D. 1980, Khorramshahr, Iran. Saddam Hussein, seeing revolutionary Iran in disarray, invaded on 22 September 1980 and took Khorramshahr after weeks of street fighting. Iran recovered the city in 1982 and carried the war into Iraq with human-wave assaults of young volunteers, and Iraq answered with poison gas. When a ceasefire came in August 1988 the frontier was unchanged, and perhaps half a million had died.
    • Black JulyA.D. 1983, Colombo, Sri Lanka. After the Tamil Tigers ambushed and killed thirteen soldiers near Jaffna, Sinhalese mobs in Colombo and across the island hunted Tamils through July 1983, burning their homes and shops and murdering hundreds, while some police and soldiers looked on. Thousands of young Tamils joined the separatist militants, and the civil war began that would last twenty-six years.
