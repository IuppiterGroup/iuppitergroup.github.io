    The chronicle of these years

    • The Last Emperor of the WestA.D. 476, Ravenna, Italy. The German general Odoacer deposes the boy emperor Romulus Augustulus at Ravenna and sends the imperial regalia to Constantinople. The Western Empire is ended. In the East, Rome endures for nearly a thousand years more.
    • Kashyapa's Rock at Sigiriyac. A.D. 477, Sigiriya, Sri Lanka. Kashyapa, who had seized the throne of Lanka and walled his father Dhatusena up alive, feared his brother's vengeance and built a palace on the summit of a sheer rock nearly two hundred metres high, entered through the paws of a colossal lion. Painted maidens still float on its face. When his brother Moggallana returned with an army from India in 495, Kashyapa took his own life on the field.
    • The Huns Slay Shah PerozA.D. 484, Herat, Afghanistan. Peroz, king of kings of Persia, had once been taken prisoner by the Hephthalites and ransomed. Seeking revenge, he marched against their king Akhshunwar in 484; the Hephthalites lured his army into a concealed trench, and Peroz perished with much of the Persian nobility. For a generation Persia paid tribute to the White Huns, whose power stretched from the Oxus into India.
    • Clovis Defeats SyagriusA.D. 486, Soissons, Gaul. Clovis, the twenty-year-old king of the Salian Franks of Tournai, defeated Syagrius, the last Roman ruler in northern Gaul, at Soissons. Syagrius fled to the Visigoths, who handed him over to be killed. Clovis took his realm between the Somme and the Loire, the foundation of the Frankish kingdom of France.
    • Theodoric Takes RavennaA.D. 493, Ravenna, Italy. Sent by the emperor Zeno to wrest Italy from Odoacer, Theodoric led the Ostrogoths over the Julian Alps in 489, won three battles, and besieged Ravenna for nearly three years. He agreed to share power with Odoacer, then killed him with his own hand at a banquet, and ruled Italy for thirty-three years as king of the Goths and viceroy for the emperor.
    • Emperor Xiaowen Moves to LuoyangA.D. 494, Luoyang, Henan, China. Emperor Xiaowen of Northern Wei led his court from the frontier capital Pingcheng to Luoyang, pretending at first to march against the south. He then forbade Xianbei dress and speech at court, changed the royal surname from Tuoba to Yuan, and married his princes into Chinese families. In the same years the first Buddhas were carved at Longmen.
    • The Baptism of Clovisc. A.D. 496, Reims, Gaul. Clovis, hard pressed by the Alamanni in battle, vowed to believe in the God of his Catholic wife Clotilde if he won. Victorious, he was baptized at Reims by Bishop Remigius with three thousand of his warriors: 'Bow your head, proud Sicambrian; adore what you have burned, burn what you have adored.' Unlike the Arian kings of the Goths, Vandals, and Burgundians, he took the Catholic faith.
    • The Astronomy of AryabhataA.D. 499, Kusumapura (Patna), Bihar, India. At Kusumapura the astronomer Aryabhata completed the Aryabhatiya at the age of twenty-three. In 121 verses he gave the value of pi as 3.1416, taught that the earth turns daily on its axis, explained eclipses by the shadows of the earth and moon, and set out a table of sines. Indian and Islamic astronomers studied his rules for a thousand years.
