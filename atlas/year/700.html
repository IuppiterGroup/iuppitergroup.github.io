    The chronicle of these years

    • Wu Zetian Takes the ThroneA.D. 690, Luoyang, Henan, China. Wu Zhao, a concubine of Taizong who became the empress of Gaozong and then ruled through her sons, set aside the last of them and proclaimed herself emperor of a new Zhou dynasty, the only woman ever to rule China in her own name. She favoured Buddhism, made Luoyang her Divine Capital, and widened the examinations, ruling by informers until she was forced from the throne in 705.
    • The Dome of the RockA.D. 691, Temple Mount, Jerusalem. On the Temple Mount the caliph Abd al-Malik raised an octagonal shrine over the rock from which Muslims hold that Muhammad ascended to heaven, completing it in 691 or 692. Its golden dome and mosaics, the work of craftsmen trained in the manner of Constantinople, rivaled the churches of Christendom, and its inscriptions rebuked the Christian doctrine of the Trinity. It is the oldest surviving great monument of Islam.
    • Carthage Falls to the ArabsA.D. 698, Carthage, Africa. Hassan ibn al-Nu'man first stormed Carthage, the last great Roman city of Africa, in 695; a Roman fleet retook it, but in 698 it fell again and for good. The Arabs razed it and built their port at Tunis instead. The Berber resistance under the prophetess the Arabs called al-Kahina was broken soon after, and her sons went over to the conquerors.
    • The Capital at NaraA.D. 710, Heijō-kyō, Nara, Japan. Empress Genmei moved the court to Heijō-kyō, a new capital laid out on the grid of Chang'an, with a great avenue running north to the palace. Through the Nara age the city held perhaps a hundred thousand people, and its temples grew so mighty that the court later moved away to escape them.
    • Tariq Crosses into SpainA.D. 711, Gibraltar, Spain. Tariq ibn Ziyad, a Berber freedman in the service of the governor Musa ibn Nusayr, crossed the strait with some seven thousand men in April 711 and landed beneath the rock that bears his name, Jabal Tariq. In July he destroyed the army of the Visigothic king Roderic by the Guadalete, and Roderic was never seen again. Toledo fell within months, and nearly all Spain within seven years.
    • Qutayba Takes SamarkandA.D. 712, Samarkand, Sogdia. Qutayba ibn Muslim, Umayyad governor of Khorasan, had already reduced Bukhara when in 712 he forced the surrender of Samarkand, the richest city of Sogdia. Its prince Ghurak agreed to tribute, the idols of its temples were burned, and a mosque was built and garrisoned. Within a generation the Sogdian merchant cities of the Silk Road had passed into the world of Islam.
    • Muhammad bin Qasim Conquers SindA.D. 712, Debal (Banbhore), Sindh, Pakistan. Sent by the governor al-Hajjaj, the young Arab general Muhammad bin Qasim brought siege engines by sea and stormed the port of Debal, where his troops slew its people for three days. Marching up the Indus, he killed Raja Dahir in battle near Aror in 712, took Brahmanabad and Multan, and sent Dahir's head, a fifth of the plunder, and thousands of captives to Iraq.
