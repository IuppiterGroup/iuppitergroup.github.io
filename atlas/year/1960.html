    The chronicle of these years

    • De Gaulle and the Fifth RepublicA.D. 1958, Paris, France. When the army and the settlers of Algiers rose on 13 May 1958 against a government they feared would abandon Algeria, and paratroops landed in Corsica, the Fourth Republic turned to General de Gaulle. Invested as premier on 1 June, he gave France a constitution with a strong president, approved by referendum on 28 September, and became its first president in January 1959.
    • The Dalai Lama Flees LhasaA.D. 1959, Lhasa, Tibet. Nine years after the People's Liberation Army had marched into eastern Tibet and broken the Tibetan army at Chamdo, crowds surrounded the Norbulingka palace in Lhasa to keep the Fourteenth Dalai Lama from Chinese hands. On the night of 17 March he slipped away disguised as a soldier and made for India. The rising was crushed by artillery, and the old Tibetan government was dissolved.
    • Castro Enters HavanaA.D. 1959, Havana, Cuba. Fidel Castro, a lawyer who had landed from the yacht Granma in 1956 with eighty-two men, waged guerrilla war from the Sierra Maestra until the dictator Fulgencio Batista fled on New Year's Day 1959. Castro entered Havana in triumph on 8 January, shot hundreds of Batista's men, seized American property, and by 1961 had declared Cuba a socialist state allied with Moscow.
    • The Congo IndependentA.D. 1960, Léopoldville, Congo. In the Year of Africa, when seventeen African nations won their freedom, the Belgian Congo became independent on 30 June 1960, and Prime Minister Patrice Lumumba answered King Baudouin's praise of Leopold II with a fierce indictment of colonial rule. Within days the army mutinied and Katanga seceded under Moise Tshombe; Lumumba was deposed, delivered to his enemies, and shot in Katanga in January 1961.
    • SharpevilleA.D. 1960, Sharpeville, South Africa. On 21 March 1960 a crowd of several thousand gathered outside the police station of Sharpeville, near Vereeniging, at the call of the Pan Africanist Congress to protest the pass laws. The police opened fire and killed 69 people, many of them shot in the back. The government declared an emergency and banned the ANC and the PAC, which turned from protest to sabotage and armed struggle.
    • Brasília InauguratedA.D. 1960, Brasília, Brazil. President Juscelino Kubitschek, promising 'fifty years of progress in five,' built a new capital on the empty plateau of Goiás to draw Brazil's people into the interior. Laid out by Lúcio Costa in the shape of an airplane, with palaces and a cathedral by Oscar Niemeyer, Brasília was inaugurated on 21 April 1960, after forty-one months of building.
    • The Berlin WallA.D. 1961, Bernauer Strasse, Berlin. By 1961 some three million East Germans had fled to the West, most through Berlin. In the early hours of 13 August soldiers and workers' militia of the German Democratic Republic strung barbed wire along the sector border, soon replaced by a concrete wall of guard towers and a death strip. At least 140 people died at the Wall before 1989, most of them shot while trying to cross.
    • The Cuban Missile CrisisA.D. 1962, San Cristóbal, Cuba. After the failure of the American-backed invasion at the Bay of Pigs in 1961, Khrushchev secretly sent nuclear missiles to Cuba, and on 14 October 1962 a U-2 photographed their launch sites near San Cristóbal. President Kennedy proclaimed a naval quarantine of the island, and for thirteen days the world stood nearer to nuclear war than ever before or since, until Khrushchev agreed to remove the missiles.
