    The chronicle of these years

    • The Wall Street CrashA.D. 1929, New York, New York. After years of speculation on borrowed money, the New York stock market broke in October 1929; on Black Tuesday, the 29th, some sixteen million shares changed hands as prices collapsed. The crash opened the Great Depression: by 1933 a quarter of American workers were idle and thousands of banks had failed, and the slump, spreading round the world, helped carry Hitler to power.
    • The Salt MarchA.D. 1930, Dandi, Gujarat, India. Mohandas Gandhi set out from his ashram at Sabarmati with seventy-eight followers and walked 385 kilometres in twenty-four days to the sea at Dandi. There on 6 April 1930 he picked up a lump of natural salt in defiance of the government monopoly. Across India tens of thousands followed his example and went to prison, and the world watched.
    • The Statute of WestminsterA.D. 1931, Westminster, London. The Imperial Conference of 1926 under Arthur Balfour declared Britain and the dominions autonomous communities, equal in status and united by allegiance to the Crown. The Statute of Westminster of 11 December 1931 made it law: Canada, South Africa, and the Irish Free State were freed at once from legislation by Westminster, Australia and New Zealand when they adopted it in the 1940s, and the British Commonwealth of Nations took shape.
    • The Mukden IncidentA.D. 1931, Mukden, Liaoning, China. Officers of Japan's Kwantung Army set off a small charge on the South Manchuria Railway outside Mukden and blamed Chinese soldiers. Without orders from Tokyo they seized the city and within months all Manchuria, where in 1932 they set up the puppet state of Manchukuo under Puyi, the last Qing emperor. When the League of Nations condemned it, Japan walked out.
    • The Famine in UkraineA.D. 1932, Kharkov, Ukraine. Stalin's forced collectivization of the peasants and impossible grain quotas brought famine to the Soviet countryside in 1932 and 1933. In Ukraine, where it is remembered as the Holodomor, villages were stripped of food, blacklisted, and sealed, and peasants were forbidden to leave in search of bread; some four million Ukrainians starved, and over a million more died in Kazakhstan.
    • The Kingdom of Saudi ArabiaA.D. 1932, Riyadh, Najd. Ibn Saud's Ikhwan warriors had won him the Hejaz and Mecca in 1924 and 1925, but would not stop at his frontiers; he broke them at Sabilla in 1929. On 23 September 1932 he united his realms by decree as the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia. It was a poor desert kingdom living on the pilgrimage, but in 1933 he granted an oil concession to Standard Oil of California.
    • The Siamese Revolution of 1932A.D. 1932, Bangkok, Siam. At dawn on 24 June 1932 the People's Party, a band of officers and officials schooled in Europe and led by Pridi Banomyong and Phraya Phahon, seized key points in Bangkok while King Prajadhipok was at Hua Hin. The king accepted a constitution and the end of absolute monarchy. Quarrels with the new men followed, and in 1935 he abdicated in England.
    • Hitler Becomes ChancellorA.D. 1933, Reich Chancellery, Berlin. On 30 January 1933 President Hindenburg, persuaded by Franz von Papen that the conservatives could hold him in check, appointed Adolf Hitler chancellor at the head of a coalition cabinet. Within two months the Reichstag fire had served as pretext to suspend civil liberties, and the Enabling Act of 23 March gave Hitler power to make laws without parliament. By summer every other party was gone.
