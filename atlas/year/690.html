    The chronicle of these years

    • Greek Fire Saves Constantinoplec. A.D. 678, Constantinople, Thrace. For several summers the fleets of the Caliph Mu'awiya pressed upon Constantinople from a base at Cyzicus. Against them Constantine IV turned a new weapon, the liquid fire of Kallinikos, an architect who had fled from Syria, pumped through bronze siphons onto the enemy ships. The Arabs withdrew with heavy loss, and the caliph bought a thirty-year peace with tribute.
    • The Martyrdom at KarbalaA.D. 680, Karbala, Iraq. Husayn, grandson of the Prophet, set out from Mecca to lead the Kufans against the new caliph Yazid. On the plain of Karbala, cut off from the Euphrates, his band of some seventy men was surrounded by the governor's troops and slain on the tenth of Muharram, October 680. His head was carried to Damascus. The Shi'a have kept that day of Ashura in mourning ever since.
    • Asparukh Founds the Bulgar RealmA.D. 681, Pliska, Bulgaria. Driven west by the Khazars, Khan Asparukh led his Bulgars over the Danube and in 680 routed the army of Constantine IV in the marshes of the delta. The next year the emperor bought peace with tribute and yielded the land between the Balkan range and the river. There the Bulgars ruled the Slav tribes from Pliska, and a new state stood at Constantinople's doorstep.
    • The Sacred Expedition of JayanasaA.D. 682, Kedukan Bukit, Palembang. An Old Malay inscription found on the Tatang River records that in 682 Dapunta Hyang Jayanasa set out by boat with twenty thousand men on a sacred expedition and founded a settlement, bringing victory and prosperity to Srivijaya. It is the oldest record of the maritime empire that would rule the Straits of Malacca and Sunda for three centuries and more.
    • Wu Zetian Takes the ThroneA.D. 690, Luoyang, Henan, China. Wu Zhao, a concubine of Taizong who became the empress of Gaozong and then ruled through her sons, set aside the last of them and proclaimed herself emperor of a new Zhou dynasty, the only woman ever to rule China in her own name. She favoured Buddhism, made Luoyang her Divine Capital, and widened the examinations, ruling by informers until she was forced from the throne in 705.
    • The Dome of the RockA.D. 691, Temple Mount, Jerusalem. On the Temple Mount the caliph Abd al-Malik raised an octagonal shrine over the rock from which Muslims hold that Muhammad ascended to heaven, completing it in 691 or 692. Its golden dome and mosaics, the work of craftsmen trained in the manner of Constantinople, rivaled the churches of Christendom, and its inscriptions rebuked the Christian doctrine of the Trinity. It is the oldest surviving great monument of Islam.
    • Carthage Falls to the ArabsA.D. 698, Carthage, Africa. Hassan ibn al-Nu'man first stormed Carthage, the last great Roman city of Africa, in 695; a Roman fleet retook it, but in 698 it fell again and for good. The Arabs razed it and built their port at Tunis instead. The Berber resistance under the prophetess the Arabs called al-Kahina was broken soon after, and her sons went over to the conquerors.
