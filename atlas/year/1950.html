    The chronicle of these years

    • Independence and PartitionA.D. 1947, New Delhi, India. At midnight on 14 August 1947 British rule ended and two dominions were born, Pakistan and India, divided by lines Sir Cyril Radcliffe had drawn in five weeks through the Punjab and Bengal. Nehru told the Constituent Assembly that India awoke to life and freedom. Some fifteen million people fled across the new borders, and perhaps a million were killed in the massacres.
    • The State of Israel ProclaimedA.D. 1948, Tel Aviv, Palestine. On 14 May 1948, as the British Mandate expired, David Ben-Gurion read the declaration of independence of the State of Israel in the Tel Aviv museum. The next day the armies of Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Iraq, and Lebanon invaded. By the armistices of 1949 Israel held more than three-quarters of Mandatory Palestine; Jordan held the West Bank and East Jerusalem, and Egypt the Gaza Strip.
    • The People's Republic ProclaimedA.D. 1949, Tiananmen, Peking, China. On 1 October, from the Gate of Heavenly Peace, Mao Zedong proclaimed the founding of the People's Republic of China. The Communists had won the civil war in three years of campaigns; within months they held the whole mainland and began to remake it through land reform, the destruction of the landlord class, and campaigns that cost millions of lives.
    • The North Atlantic TreatyA.D. 1949, Washington, United States. On 4 April 1949 the foreign ministers of the United States, Canada, Britain, France, the Benelux states, Italy, Portugal, Norway, Denmark, and Iceland signed in Washington a treaty declaring that an armed attack against one of them in Europe or North America would be considered an attack against them all. West Germany joined in 1955, and the Soviet bloc answered with the Warsaw Pact.
    • Two German StatesA.D. 1949, Bonn, West Germany. On 23 May 1949 the Basic Law of the Federal Republic of Germany was proclaimed at Bonn, the small Rhineland university town chosen as a provisional capital, and in September Konrad Adenauer became chancellor by a single vote, his own. In the Soviet zone the German Democratic Republic was founded on 7 October, and Germany stayed divided for forty-one years.
    • The Schuman DeclarationA.D. 1950, Quai d'Orsay, Paris. In the Salon de l'Horloge of the French foreign ministry on 9 May 1950, Robert Schuman proposed, on a plan drafted by Jean Monnet, to pool the coal and steel of France and Germany under a common High Authority, making war between them materially impossible. Italy and the Benelux states joined; the European Coal and Steel Community of 1951 was the first of the European Communities.
    • North Korea Invades the SouthA.D. 1950, Kaesong, Korea. At dawn on 25 June, with Stalin's consent, Kim Il Sung's army crossed the 38th parallel behind Soviet tanks and took Seoul in three days. The United Nations sent forces under General MacArthur, whose landing at Incheon in September broke the invaders; when the Americans pressed on to the Yalu, some three hundred thousand Chinese soldiers fell upon them in the winter mountains.
    • The Republic of IndiaA.D. 1950, New Delhi, India. On 26 January 1950 the Constitution of India, drafted under the chairmanship of B. R. Ambedkar, came into force, and India became a sovereign republic with Rajendra Prasad as its first president. With 395 articles it was the longest written constitution of any nation, and it granted the vote to every adult at a stroke.
