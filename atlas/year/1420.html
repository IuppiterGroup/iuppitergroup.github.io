    The chronicle of these years

    • The Knights Broken at GrunwaldA.D. 1410, Grunwald, Prussia. On 15 July 1410, in the fields between Grunwald and Tannenberg, Władysław II Jagiełło and his cousin Vytautas, leading Poles, Lithuanians, Ruthenians, and Tatars, broke the army of the Teutonic Order. Grand Master Ulrich von Jungingen and most of the Order's officers fell. Marienburg held out, but the Order never recovered its strength.
    • Jan Hus Burned at ConstanceA.D. 1415, Constance, Germany. Jan Hus, the Prague preacher who followed Wyclif in attacking indulgences and the wealth of the clergy, came to the Council of Constance under a safe conduct from Sigismund. He was imprisoned, condemned as a heretic, and burned on 6 July 1415. Bohemia rose in fury, and for twenty years the Hussite armies defied the Empire and the Church.
    • Henry V at AgincourtA.D. 1415, Agincourt, Artois. On St. Crispin's Day, 25 October 1415, Henry V's sick and hungry army, perhaps 6,000 men and most of them archers, faced a French host several times its size between two woods. The French men-at-arms, floundering in the mud, were shot down and crushed in their own press. Henry went on to conquer Normandy, and by the Treaty of Troyes was made heir to France.
    • The Forbidden CityA.D. 1421, Peking, China. The Yongle Emperor, who had seized the throne from his nephew in 1402 and left the palace at Nanjing in flames, moved his capital north to Beijing, his old princely seat. There a hundred thousand craftsmen and perhaps a million labourers had raised the Forbidden City, some 980 buildings behind a moat and walls, the home of twenty-four emperors until 1912.
    • Le Loi Drives Out the MingA.D. 1428, Thang Long, Vietnam. Le Loi, a landowner of Lam Son, rose against the Ming in 1418 and fought a guerrilla war for ten years. In 1427 he destroyed a Ming relief army at the pass of Chi Lang, where its general Liu Sheng was killed, and the Ming withdrew. Crowned as Le Thai To, he proclaimed the victory in Nguyen Trai's Great Proclamation upon the Pacification of the Wu.
    • The Triple AllianceA.D. 1428, Azcapotzalco, Mexico. Itzcoatl of Tenochtitlan joined Nezahualcoyotl, the exiled poet-prince of Texcoco, and the lord of Tlacopan to overthrow Maxtla and the Tepanec overlords of Azcapotzalco, to whom the Mexica had paid tribute. The victors formed the Triple Alliance that became the Aztec empire, and Itzcoatl had the old painted books burned so that the history of the Mexica could be written anew.
    • Joan of Arc Relieves OrléansA.D. 1429, Orléans, France. Joan, a peasant girl of Domrémy who heard the voices of saints bidding her save France, came to the Dauphin Charles at Chinon and was sent with a convoy to besieged Orléans. Within nine days of her arrival the English forts were taken and the siege raised on 8 May 1429; in July she stood beside Charles at his crowning in Reims. Captured the next year, she was burned at Rouen in 1431.
    • Min Saw Mon Founds Mrauk UA.D. 1430, Mrauk U, Burma. Min Saw Mon, king of Arakan, driven out by the Burmans of Ava, spent twenty-four years in exile at the court of Bengal and returned with a Bengali army to win back his throne. In 1430 he founded Mrauk U, whose Buddhist kings took Muslim titles and struck coins in Persian, and whose fleets and Portuguese freebooters made it a terror of the Bay of Bengal.
