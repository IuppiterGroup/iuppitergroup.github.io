    The chronicle of these years

    • The Union of KrewoA.D. 1385, Krewo, Lithuania. By the act sealed at Krewo on 14 August 1385, Jogaila, pagan grand duke of Lithuania, pledged to accept baptism with his people, join his lands to Poland, and marry the young Queen Jadwiga. Crowned at Kraków in 1386 as Władysław II, he founded the Jagiellonian dynasty, and the last pagan state of Europe entered Latin Christendom.
    • Timur's Towers of Skulls at IsfahanA.D. 1387, Isfahan, Persia. Isfahan had surrendered to Timur and accepted a garrison when its people rose in November 1387 and killed some three thousand of his soldiers. Timur ordered each division of his army to bring in a quota of heads. Contemporaries counted seventy thousand, piled into towers outside the walls, an example that made other Persian cities open their gates.
    • The Retreat from Wihwa IslandA.D. 1388, Wihwado, Korea. Ordered by King U and the general Choe Yeong to march on Liaodong against the new Ming, the commander Yi Seong-gye halted his army on Wihwa Island in the flooding Yalu and turned back. He seized Kaesong, exiled Choe Yeong, and deposed the king. Four years later he took the throne himself and founded the Joseon dynasty, which ruled Korea for over five centuries.
    • The Field of BlackbirdsA.D. 1389, Kosovo Field, Serbia. On St. Vitus' Day, 15 June 1389, Prince Lazar of Serbia and his allies met Sultan Murad I on the Field of Blackbirds. Murad was slain in his tent, by tradition by the knight Miloš Obilić, and Lazar was taken and beheaded. Both armies were shattered, but Serbia could not replace its dead and became an Ottoman vassal; the battle became the heart of Serbian memory.
    • Isidore Laments the Child LevyA.D. 1395, Thessalonica, Macedonia. In a sermon of 1395 Isidore Glabas, metropolitan of Thessalonica, then under Ottoman rule, lamented the seizure of the city's Christian boys by the sultan's officers. His is the earliest witness to the devshirme: the levy of Christian children from Balkan villages, who were taken from their families, made Muslims, and trained as Janissaries or palace servants. The levy continued for three centuries.
    • The Crusade of NicopolisA.D. 1396, Nicopolis, Bulgaria. King Sigismund of Hungary and the chivalry of France and Burgundy under John of Nevers came to drive the Turks from Europe. On 25 September 1396 at Nicopolis on the Danube the French knights charged headlong against Sultan Bayezid I and were destroyed. Bayezid had some thousands of prisoners slaughtered before him; John of Nevers was ransomed for 200,000 florins.
    • The Kalmar UnionA.D. 1397, Kalmar, Sweden. In June 1397 at Kalmar, Queen Margaret, mistress of Denmark, Norway, and Sweden, had her great-nephew Eric of Pomerania crowned king of all three realms. The union of the Scandinavian crowns under one monarch, from Greenland to Finland, endured with lapses and Swedish rebellions until 1523.
    • Timur Sacks DelhiA.D. 1398, Delhi, India. Timur crossed the Indus and came down on Delhi, ruled by the feeble Tughlaq sultan Nasir al-Din Mahmud. Before the battle he ordered some hundred thousand Hindu captives in his camp put to death lest they rise. He scattered the sultan's army and its war elephants, his troops sacked and slaughtered in the city for days, and he left with masons, artisans, and slaves beyond counting.
