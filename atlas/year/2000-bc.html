    The chronicle of these years

    • The Laws of Ur-Nammuc. 2100 B.C., Ur, Sumer. The oldest law code to survive was issued at Ur by Ur-Nammu or his son Shulgi. Its prologue promises that the orphan will not be given to the rich nor the widow to the powerful, and its laws set fines in silver for injuries where later codes would demand an eye for an eye.
    • Yu the Great Founds the Xiac. 2070 B.C., Yangcheng, China. The Chinese histories tell that Yu the Great tamed the floods of the Yellow River by digging channels to the sea, labouring thirteen years without entering his own gate, and that the throne passed to his son, founding the Xia, the first dynasty in which rule passed from father to son.
    • Mentuhotep II Reunites Egyptc. 2040 B.C., Deir el-Bahari, Thebes, Egypt. Mentuhotep II, prince of Thebes, overcame the kings of Herakleopolis and joined the Two Lands again, opening the Middle Kingdom. He built his terraced mortuary temple in the bay of cliffs at Deir el-Bahari, and in a tomb nearby were found some sixty of his soldiers, slain in battle and buried with honor.
    • The Elamites Sack Urc. 2004 B.C., Ur, Sumer. Weakened by famine and by Amorite tribes pressing in from the west, the empire of Ur fell to an army from Elam and Shimashki, which stormed the city and led King Ibbi-Sin away in chains to Anshan. The Lament for Ur mourned the dead lying in the streets and the abandoned temple of Nanna.
    • The Bantu Begin Their Wanderingsc. 2000 B.C., Cameroon Grassfields, Cameroon. From the hill country between the Cross River and the Cameroon Grassfields, farmers speaking the ancestral Bantu tongue began a slow advance south and east. Over the following two and a half thousand years their descendants, carrying the yam, the hoe, and in time the forge, peopled the Congo forest, the Great Lakes, and the lands beyond the Zambezi, until one family of languages covered nearly half the continent.
    • The Chariots of Sintashtac. 2000 B.C., Sintashta, Russia. On the steppe east of the southern Urals, men of the Sintashta culture built walled towns of bronze-smiths and buried their chiefs with horses and two-wheeled chariots with spoked wheels, the oldest known. Within a few centuries the war chariot had spread to the Near East, India, and China.
    • Abram Goes Out to Canaanc. 1900 B.C., Shechem, Canaan. At the word of the Lord, Abram left Haran with Sarai his wife and Lot his nephew and came into the land of Canaan, to the oak of Moreh at Shechem, where God promised the land to his offspring and Abram built an altar. Jews, Christians, and Muslims alike call him father in faith.
    • The Palace of Knossosc. 1900 B.C., Knossos, Crete. On Crete the Minoans raised palaces at Knossos, Phaistos, and Malia, sprawling courts of storerooms, shrines, and painted halls without walls of defense, the seats of the first civilization of Europe. Their ships carried oil, wine, and fine pottery to Egypt and the Levant, and their scribes wrote in hieroglyphs and in the still unread Linear A.
