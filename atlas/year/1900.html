    The chronicle of these years

    • J'AccuseA.D. 1898, Paris, France. Captain Alfred Dreyfus, a Jewish officer of the General Staff, was condemned in 1894 for betraying secrets to Germany on flimsy evidence and a secret file, and sent to Devil's Island. When a court-martial acquitted the real traitor, Major Esterhazy, Émile Zola published in L'Aurore on 13 January 1898 an open letter to the President headed J'accuse. The Affair split France for a decade; Dreyfus was exonerated in 1906.
    • Dewey at Manila BayA.D. 1898, Manila Bay, Philippines. A week after war broke out between Spain and the United States, Commodore George Dewey's squadron entered Manila Bay by night and on the morning of 1 May 1898 destroyed the Spanish fleet off Cavite without losing a man to enemy fire. On 12 June Aguinaldo proclaimed Philippine independence from his house at Kawit, but in December Spain ceded the islands to the United States for twenty million dollars.
    • OmdurmanA.D. 1898, Kerreri, Sudan. General Herbert Kitchener's Anglo-Egyptian army, brought south by a railway laid across the Nubian desert, met the Khalifa Abdallahi's host of some 50,000 on the plain of Kerreri outside Omdurman on 2 September 1898. Maxim guns, artillery, and gunboats on the Nile cut down some ten thousand of the Khalifa's men for fewer than fifty of their own; the young Winston Churchill rode in the charge of the 21st Lancers.
    • The War with SpainA.D. 1898, Santiago de Cuba, Cuba. Three years into the Cuban war of independence, the battleship Maine blew up in Havana harbor in February 1898, and the United States declared war on Spain. At Santiago, Theodore Roosevelt's Rough Riders and the regulars stormed the San Juan Heights on 1 July, and two days later the American fleet destroyed Admiral Cervera's squadron; Spain gave up Cuba and ceded Puerto Rico, Guam, and the Philippines.
    • The Siege of the LegationsA.D. 1900, Peking, China. The Boxers, peasant bands who believed their rites made them proof against bullets, swept across the north killing missionaries and Chinese Christians by the thousands. In June the Empress Dowager Cixi declared war on the powers, and the legations of Beijing were besieged for fifty-five days until an army of eight nations relieved them and sacked the city.
    • The Commonwealth of AustraliaA.D. 1901, Centennial Park, Sydney. On 1 January 1901 the six colonies of Australia united as a federal Commonwealth, proclaimed before a vast crowd in Sydney's Centennial Park, with Edmund Barton as first prime minister. One of the new Parliament's first acts was the Immigration Restriction Act, foundation of the White Australia policy. A new capital, Canberra, was laid out in the bush from 1913.
    • Ibn Saud Takes RiyadhA.D. 1902, Riyadh, Najd. Abd al-Aziz ibn Saud, a young exile living with his family in Kuwait, rode into Najd with some forty companions. Before dawn on 15 January 1902 his men surprised the Rashidi governor Ajlan outside the Masmak fort in Riyadh and killed him. The town rallied to the house of Saud, and from it Ibn Saud built the kingdom that would bear his family's name.
    • The First Flight at Kitty HawkA.D. 1903, Kitty Hawk, North Carolina. On the windswept dunes of Kill Devil Hills near Kitty Hawk, on 17 December 1903, Orville Wright lifted the brothers' gasoline-engined Flyer into the air for twelve seconds and 120 feet, the first controlled and sustained flight of a powered machine heavier than air. Wilbur's fourth flight that day lasted fifty-nine seconds; within forty years the airplane had transformed war, commerce, and travel.
