    The chronicle of these years

    • The Toltecs at Tulac. A.D. 950, Tula, Mexico. On a ridge above the Tula River, the Toltecs built Tollan, a city of perhaps sixty thousand, whose temple roof was borne by colossal stone warriors with butterfly breastplates and spear-throwers. Later peoples remembered the Toltecs as masters of all the arts and their priest-king Topiltzin Quetzalcoatl as a god; the Aztec kings claimed descent from them.
    • Otto the Great at the LechfeldA.D. 955, Lechfeld, Bavaria. On 10 August 955 a Magyar host besieging Augsburg met Otto I and the levies of every German duchy, marching beneath the Holy Lance. The Magyars were routed and hunted down at the river crossings, and their leaders were hanged at Regensburg. Their raids on the West ended forever, and Otto's victory won him the renown that carried him to Rome.
    • The Mutiny at ChenqiaoA.D. 960, Chenqiao, Henan, China. Marching north against the Khitan, the officers of the Later Zhou palace army halted at Chenqiao, wrapped their commander Zhao Kuangyin in a yellow robe, and hailed him emperor. He returned to Kaifeng, received the throne from the boy emperor, and founded the Song. Later, over wine, he persuaded his generals to retire to rich estates, and the soldier was made servant to the scholar for a thousand years.
    • Otto I Crowned EmperorA.D. 962, St. Peter's, Rome. On 2 February 962 Pope John XII crowned Otto, King of the Germans and of Italy, as emperor in St. Peter's, and the imperial dignity, vacant in the West for nearly forty years, passed to the German kings. The empire later called Holy and Roman would endure until 1806.
    • Sviatoslav Breaks the KhazarsA.D. 965, Sarkel, Khazaria. Sviatoslav of Kiev, who sent his enemies the warning 'I come against you,' fell upon the Khazar khaganate, which had taken tribute from the eastern Slavs. He stormed the fortress of Sarkel on the Don, which the Rus' renamed the White Tower, and ravaged Atil on the Volga. The Jewish khaganate never recovered, and the steppe lay open to the Pechenegs.
    • The Baptism of MieszkoA.D. 966, Greater Poland, Poland. Mieszko I of the Polans, who had married the Christian princess Dobrawa of Bohemia, received baptism at Easter 966 with his court. By taking the faith from Bohemia and Rome rather than at the point of German swords, he set his realm among the Christian states of Latin Europe, and within two years Poland had its first bishop at Poznań.
    • Dinh Bo Linh Unites Dai Co VietA.D. 968, Hoa Lu, Vietnam. After Ngo rule dissolved into the anarchy of the Twelve Warlords, Dinh Bo Linh, a buffalo boy turned captain, subdued them one by one and took the title of emperor. He named his realm Dai Co Viet and fixed his capital among the limestone crags of Hoa Lu, where a cauldron and a caged tiger in his courtyard awaited those who broke his laws.
    • Jawhar Founds CairoA.D. 969, Cairo, Egypt. The Fatimid general Jawhar, a slave of Greek or Slav birth in the service of the caliph al-Mu'izz, took Egypt in July 969 almost without a fight and laid out a walled palace city north of Fustat. It was named al-Qahira, the Victorious. In 973 al-Mu'izz moved his court there from Ifriqiya, and Cairo became the capital of a Shi'ite empire reaching from Sicily to the Hijaz.
