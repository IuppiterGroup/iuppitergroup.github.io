    The chronicle of these years

    • The Tumu CrisisA.D. 1449, Tumu, Hebei, China. Urged on by the eunuch Wang Zhen, the young Zhengtong Emperor led a vast army against the Oirat chief Esen. Turning back in disorder, it was caught without water at the post station of Tumu and destroyed; Wang Zhen was killed and the emperor taken prisoner. The minister Yu Qian set the emperor's brother on the throne and saved Beijing, and the Ming thereafter kept behind their walls.
    • Machu Picchuc. A.D. 1450, Machu Picchu, Peru. On a saddle between two peaks above the gorge of the Urubamba, Pachacuti's builders raised a royal estate of granite temples, palaces, and terraces, with the Intihuatana stone and the curved Temple of the Sun. Abandoned after the Spanish conquest and never found by the conquerors, it was brought to the notice of the world by Hiram Bingham in 1911.
    • The Fall of ConstantinopleA.D. 1453, Constantinople. After fifty-three days of bombardment by the largest cannon the world had yet seen, Sultan Mehmed II storms the Theodosian Walls. Constantine XI, last emperor of the Romans, throws off his imperial insignia and dies fighting in the breach. By the old Roman reckoning it is the year 2206 from the founding of the City.
    • Gutenberg's Biblec. A.D. 1455, Mainz, Germany. In his workshop at Mainz, Johann Gutenberg, a goldsmith by training, joined movable metal type cast in a hand mould, an oil-based ink, and a screw press to print some 180 copies of the Latin Bible. Within fifty years presses in more than two hundred towns had printed millions of books, and the Reformation, the new science, and the modern state would all be carried by type.
    • Hunyadi Saves BelgradeA.D. 1456, Belgrade, Hungary. Three years after taking Constantinople, Mehmed II laid siege to Belgrade, the key to Hungary. John Hunyadi broke the Turkish blockade on the Danube, and on 22 July 1456 crusaders roused by the friar John of Capistrano poured from the fortress and stormed the sultan's camp; Mehmed was wounded and withdrew. Hunyadi died of plague within weeks. The noon bells of Catholic Europe still recall the victory.
    • The Ōnin WarA.D. 1467, Heian-kyō, Kyoto, Japan. A quarrel over the succession to the shogun Ashikaga Yoshimasa set the great lords Hosokawa Katsumoto and Yamana Sōzen at war in the streets of Kyoto. For ten years their armies burned the capital quarter by quarter, while the shogun gave himself to painting, poetry, and the tea ceremony. When it ended, power lay with the provincial lords, and the Age of the Warring States had begun.
    • Sunni Ali Takes TimbuktuA.D. 1468, Timbuktu, Mali. Sunni Ali, the Songhai king of Gao, a peerless horseman and a sorcerer in the eyes of his people and a tyrant in the eyes of the scholars, drove the Tuareg from Timbuktu and sacked the town, slaying and scattering its learned men. Five years later he starved Djenné into surrender, and Songhai replaced Mali as master of the Niger bend.
    • Ferdinand and Isabella WedA.D. 1469, Valladolid, Castile. On 19 October 1469, in a house in Valladolid, Isabella, half-sister and heir of Henry IV of Castile, married Ferdinand, heir of Aragon, without her brother's consent and on the strength of a forged papal dispensation. Ten years later both were reigning; their union of crowns created Spain.
