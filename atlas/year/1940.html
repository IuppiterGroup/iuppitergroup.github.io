    The chronicle of these years

    • The Invasion of PolandA.D. 1939, Westerplatte, Danzig. At dawn on 1 September 1939 the old battleship Schleswig-Holstein opened fire on the Polish depot at Westerplatte in Danzig harbor, and German armies crossed the Polish frontier from three sides. Britain and France declared war on 3 September. On the 17th the Red Army invaded from the east; Warsaw surrendered on the 28th, and Poland was partitioned between the two dictators.
    • The Katyn MassacreA.D. 1940, Katyn Forest, Russia. In April and May 1940, on an order signed by Stalin and the Politburo, the NKVD shot some 22,000 Polish prisoners, army officers, police, landowners, and professional men, in the Katyn forest near Smolensk, at Kalinin, at Kharkov, and other places. The Germans found the graves at Katyn in 1943; Moscow blamed them for the crime until 1990.
    • The Evacuation from DunkirkA.D. 1940, Dunkirk, France. The German armor broke through the Ardennes at Sedan in May 1940 and reached the Channel in ten days, cutting off the British Expeditionary Force and the French northern armies. From 26 May to 4 June destroyers, ferries, and hundreds of small boats brought some 338,000 men off the beaches and mole of Dunkirk, while the French rearguard held the perimeter.
    • The Battle of BritainA.D. 1940, Biggin Hill, Kent. Through the summer of 1940 Göring's Luftwaffe tried to destroy the Royal Air Force as the prelude to invasion. Guided by radar and the operations rooms of Dowding's Fighter Command, the Hurricanes and Spitfires held; on 15 September the great daylight raids on London were beaten off, and Hitler postponed the invasion. The night Blitz on British cities went on until May 1941.
    • Operation BarbarossaA.D. 1941, Brest Fortress, Belarus. Before dawn on 22 June 1941 more than three million German soldiers in three army groups, with their allies, crossed the Soviet frontier from the Baltic to the Black Sea. The garrison of the Brest fortress fought on for weeks, but the Red Army lost millions in vast encirclements at Białystok, Minsk, Smolensk, and Kiev. By December the Germans stood before Moscow and Leningrad.
    • The Attack on Pearl HarborA.D. 1941, Pearl Harbor, Hawaii. Facing an American embargo on oil, Japan struck first. On the morning of 7 December, aircraft from six carriers under Admiral Nagumo Chūichi, carrying out the plan of Admiral Yamamoto Isoroku, sank or crippled eight American battleships at Pearl Harbor and killed over 2,400 Americans. Within hours Japan fell upon Malaya, Hong Kong, and the Philippines, and the war became a world war in full.
    • The Fall of SingaporeA.D. 1942, Singapore. After a campaign of seventy days down the length of Malaya, General Yamashita's army crossed the Johor Strait, and on 15 February 1942 General Arthur Percival surrendered Singapore with some eighty thousand British, Australian, and Indian troops, the greatest capitulation in British history. The belief in European invincibility in Asia died with it, and tens of thousands of Chinese were massacred in the purge that followed.
    • The Surrender at StalingradA.D. 1943, Stalingrad, Russia. Through the autumn of 1942 the German Sixth Army fought for Stalingrad street by street and factory by factory on the Volga. On 19 November Zhukov and Vasilevsky's Operation Uranus broke the Romanian armies on its flanks and closed the ring. Forbidden to break out, the newly made Field Marshal Friedrich Paulus surrendered on 31 January 1943; some 91,000 starving men went into captivity, and few came home.
