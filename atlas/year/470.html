    The chronicle of these years

    • Pope Leo Meets AttilaA.D. 452, Mincio River, Italy. Attila crossed the Julian Alps, razed Aquileia after a long siege, and sacked Milan and the cities of the Po. Near the Mincio he was met by an embassy from Valentinian III led by Pope Leo I, and he turned back from Italy, whether from awe, from plague in his army, or from fear of an Eastern army behind him. He died the next year.
    • The Vandals Sack RomeA.D. 455, Rome, Italy. After the murder of Valentinian III, Genseric sailed from Carthage and took Rome without resistance. At the plea of Pope Leo he spared the people from slaughter and the city from fire, but for fourteen days the Vandals stripped it of its treasures, among them the spoils of the Temple of Jerusalem, and carried off the empress Eudoxia and her daughters.
    • Skandagupta Repels the Hunsc. A.D. 455, Bhitari, Uttar Pradesh, India. Skandagupta, coming to the throne in a season of peril, drove back the Hunas who had poured into India from the northwest. His pillar at Bhitari tells that the earth shook when he closed with them in battle, and that he passed a night sleeping on the bare ground to restore his family's shaken fortune. The empire held, but its treasury was drained.
    • The Painted Caves of Ajantac. A.D. 470, Ajanta, Maharashtra, India. In the reign of the Vakataka emperor Harishena, ministers and vassals carved monasteries and halls of worship into a horseshoe cliff above the Waghora river. Their walls were painted with the lives of the Buddha and tales of his former births, crowded with princes, dancers, and beasts, the finest painting to survive from ancient India. Abandoned soon after, they lay hidden in jungle for thirteen centuries.
    • The Last Emperor of the WestA.D. 476, Ravenna, Italy. The German general Odoacer deposes the boy emperor Romulus Augustulus at Ravenna and sends the imperial regalia to Constantinople. The Western Empire is ended. In the East, Rome endures for nearly a thousand years more.
    • Kashyapa's Rock at Sigiriyac. A.D. 477, Sigiriya, Sri Lanka. Kashyapa, who had seized the throne of Lanka and walled his father Dhatusena up alive, feared his brother's vengeance and built a palace on the summit of a sheer rock nearly two hundred metres high, entered through the paws of a colossal lion. Painted maidens still float on its face. When his brother Moggallana returned with an army from India in 495, Kashyapa took his own life on the field.
    • The Huns Slay Shah PerozA.D. 484, Herat, Afghanistan. Peroz, king of kings of Persia, had once been taken prisoner by the Hephthalites and ransomed. Seeking revenge, he marched against their king Akhshunwar in 484; the Hephthalites lured his army into a concealed trench, and Peroz perished with much of the Persian nobility. For a generation Persia paid tribute to the White Huns, whose power stretched from the Oxus into India.
    • Clovis Defeats SyagriusA.D. 486, Soissons, Gaul. Clovis, the twenty-year-old king of the Salian Franks of Tournai, defeated Syagrius, the last Roman ruler in northern Gaul, at Soissons. Syagrius fled to the Visigoths, who handed him over to be killed. Clovis took his realm between the Somme and the Loire, the foundation of the Frankish kingdom of France.
