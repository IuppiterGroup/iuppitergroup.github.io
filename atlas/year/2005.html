    The chronicle of these years

    • The Attacks of September 11A.D. 2001, New York, New York. On the morning of 11 September 2001, nineteen Islamist hijackers of al-Qaeda seized four American airliners, flew two into the towers of the World Trade Center, which collapsed, and one into the Pentagon; the fourth crashed in Pennsylvania as its passengers fought back. Nearly three thousand died. The United States invaded Afghanistan within a month and Iraq in 2003, beginning a twenty-year war on terror.
    • The Euro in the PocketA.D. 2002, Frankfurt am Main, Germany. Created as the currency of eleven states on 1 January 1999 and managed by the European Central Bank at Frankfurt, the euro became money in the hand on 1 January 2002, when notes and coins replaced the mark, the franc, the lira, the guilder, the peseta, the drachma, and the other national currencies of twelve nations. The deutschmark, emblem of German recovery, passed away.
    • The Bali BombingsA.D. 2002, Kuta, Bali. On 12 October 2002 bombs planted by Jemaah Islamiyah, a jihadist network linked to al-Qaeda that sought an Islamic state across Southeast Asia, tore through two crowded nightclubs at Kuta on Bali. Two hundred and two people were killed, eighty-eight of them Australians. Indonesia's police, with foreign help, hunted the network down over the following decade.
    • The Fall of BaghdadA.D. 2003, Baghdad, Iraq. On 20 March 2003 American and British forces invaded Iraq to destroy weapons of mass destruction that were never found. Baghdad fell on 9 April, when Marines helped a crowd pull down Saddam Hussein's statue in Firdos Square. Saddam was found hiding in a hole near Tikrit that December and hanged in 2006, while Iraq sank into insurgency and a civil war between Sunni and Shi'ite.
    • The War in DarfurA.D. 2003, El Fasher, Sudan. When rebels of the Fur, Masalit, and Zaghawa peoples attacked the airfield at El Fasher in April 2003, Omar al-Bashir's government loosed the Arab Janjaweed militias upon their villages. By the reckoning of the United Nations some 300,000 people died of the massacres, hunger, and disease that followed, and two million were driven from their homes; in 2009 the International Criminal Court issued a warrant for Bashir's arrest.
    • Europe Reunited in the UnionA.D. 2004, Phoenix Park, Dublin. On 1 May 2004 ten states joined the European Union: Poland, Hungary, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Slovenia, Estonia, Latvia, Lithuania, Malta, and Cyprus, welcomed at a ceremony in Dublin under the Irish presidency. Most had entered NATO in 1999 or that March. Romania and Bulgaria followed in 2007 and Croatia in 2013, and the Union counted twenty-eight members.
    • The Indian Ocean TsunamiA.D. 2004, Banda Aceh, Sumatra. On 26 December 2004 an earthquake of magnitude 9.1 beneath the sea off Sumatra loosed waves that struck the coasts of the Indian Ocean from Thailand to Somalia. Aceh suffered worst: some 170,000 died there, many in Banda Aceh. Amid the ruin, the Free Aceh Movement and Jakarta made peace at Helsinki in 2005, ending thirty years of war.
    • North Korea's First Nuclear TestA.D. 2006, Punggye-ri, North Korea. The regime of Kim Jong Il, which had outlived a famine in the 1990s that killed hundreds of thousands, exploded its first nuclear device in a tunnel beneath Mount Mantap at Punggye-ri. Five more tests followed by 2017, the last far more powerful, and North Korea built missiles to carry its weapons across the Pacific.
