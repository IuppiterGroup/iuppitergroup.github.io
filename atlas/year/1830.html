    The chronicle of these years

    • Chao Anouvong's WarA.D. 1827, Vientiane, Laos. Chao Anouvong, king of Vientiane and vassal of Siam, marched on Bangkok in 1827, believing a false rumor that Britain was at war with Siam. Turned back at Khorat, where legend credits Thao Suranari with rallying the captives against him, he was pursued to Vientiane. The Siamese razed his city and carried its people across the Mekong, and Anouvong died a prisoner in Bangkok.
    • The Treaty of TurkmenchayA.D. 1828, Turkmenchay, Persia. After Russian armies under Paskevich took Erivan and Tabriz, Fath-Ali Shah made peace at the village of Turkmenchay on 21 February 1828. Persia gave up the khanates of Erivan and Nakhichevan, paid an indemnity of twenty million roubles, and granted Russian subjects extraterritorial rights. The Caucasus north of the Araxes was lost for good, and Armenians from Persia were resettled in the ceded lands.
    • The Three Glorious DaysA.D. 1830, Paris, France. When Charles X and his minister Polignac issued ordinances in July 1830 muzzling the press and dissolving the new Chamber, Paris rose behind barricades for three days, 27 to 29 July, and the royal troops gave way. The old king abdicated and sailed for England; the liberal deputies, with Lafayette's blessing, gave the crown to his cousin Louis Philippe, Duke of Orléans, as King of the French.
    • The Opera Riot in BrusselsA.D. 1830, Brussels, Belgium. On 25 August 1830 a performance of Auber's La Muette de Portici at the Théâtre de la Monnaie, with its duet Amour sacré de la patrie, sent the Brussels audience into the streets. The riot became a revolt against William I's Dutch rule; Dutch troops were driven out of the city in September, a national congress declared independence, and the powers in London recognized a neutral Belgium.
    • The November Rising in WarsawA.D. 1830, Belweder Palace, Warsaw. On the night of 29 November 1830 cadets of the infantry school attacked the Belweder Palace, residence of the Grand Duke Constantine, and Warsaw rose against Russian rule. In January the Sejm dethroned Nicholas I as King of Poland. The Polish army fought well at Olszynka Grochowska, but Field Marshal Paskevich stormed Warsaw in September 1831, and the Great Emigration carried the Polish cause to Paris.
    • The French Take AlgiersA.D. 1830, Algiers, Algeria. Provoked by the blow of a fly-whisk that the dey Hussein struck the French consul in 1827, Charles X sent an expedition that landed at Sidi Ferruch in June 1830. Algiers capitulated on 5 July, and the dey sailed into exile. The last of the corsair regencies fell, and France began a conquest, resisted for fifteen years by the emir Abd al-Qadir, and a rule that endured for 132 years.
    • Van den Bosch's Cultivation SystemA.D. 1830, Buitenzorg, Java. To pay for the Java War and the revolt of Belgium, Governor-General Johannes van den Bosch required Javanese villages to set aside a fifth of their land, or sixty-six days' labor a year, to grow coffee, sugar, and indigo for the government at fixed prices. The system filled the Dutch treasury for forty years and paid for its railways, but brought famine to Cirebon and Demak in the 1840s.
    • Slavery Abolished in the British EmpireA.D. 1833, Westminster, London. The Slavery Abolition Act, passed in August 1833 a few weeks after Wilberforce's death, freed some 800,000 slaves in the British West Indies, the Cape, and Mauritius from 1 August 1834, though most were bound as apprentices until 1838. Parliament voted twenty million pounds, about two fifths of the government's yearly spending, to compensate the slaveholders.
