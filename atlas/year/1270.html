    The chronicle of these years

    • The Mongols Sack BaghdadA.D. 1258, Baghdad, Iraq. Hulagu, brother of the Great Khan Möngke, having reduced the Assassin castles, summoned the caliph al-Musta'sim to submit. When he temporized, the Mongols stormed Baghdad in February 1258 and sacked it for a week; the dead were reckoned in the hundreds of thousands. The last Abbasid caliph of Baghdad was rolled in a carpet and trampled by horses, and the Tigris, it was said, ran black with the ink of its libraries.
    • The Battle of Ain JalutA.D. 1260, Ain Jalut, Galilee. When the death of the Great Khan drew Hulagu east with most of his army, the Mamluk sultan Qutuz marched out of Egypt against the force left in Syria under Kitbuqa. On 3 September 1260, at the Spring of Goliath in the valley of Jezreel, the Mamluks drew the Mongols into an ambush and destroyed them; Kitbuqa was slain. The westward tide of the Mongol conquest was turned for good.
    • Baybars Destroys AntiochA.D. 1268, Antioch, Syria. On 18 May 1268 the Mamluk sultan Baybars stormed Antioch, held by the Franks for 170 years. The gates were shut that none might escape; the men were put to the sword and the women and children sold, so many that the price of a captive fell to a few dirhams. Baybars wrote to the absent prince Bohemond VI to describe the slaughter. The great city never recovered.
    • The Marinids Take MarrakeshA.D. 1269, Marrakesh, Morocco. The Banu Marin, Zenata nomads who had come up from the desert fringes as the Almohads weakened after Las Navas, took Fez in 1248. In September 1269 Abu Yusuf Ya'qub entered Marrakesh, and the last Almohad caliph was slain in flight. The Marinids ruled Morocco from Fez, raised there a new royal city and splendid madrasas, and crossed often into Spain to aid Granada.
    • Saint Louis Dies Before TunisA.D. 1270, Carthage, Tunisia. Louis IX of France, hoping, it was said, to convert the Hafsid sultan al-Mustansir, turned his second crusade against Tunis and landed at the ruins of Carthage in July 1270. Dysentery swept the camp in the African heat; the king died on 25 August, murmuring the name of Jerusalem. His brother Charles of Anjou made terms with the sultan and sailed home.
    • The Solomonic Line RestoredA.D. 1270, Amhara, Ethiopia. Yekuno Amlak, an Amhara noble who traced his descent to Solomon and the Queen of Sheba through Menelik I, overthrew the last Zagwe king with the support of the monks of Lake Hayq and Shewa, and took the throne of Ethiopia. His Solomonic dynasty, whose title the Kebra Nagast, set down soon after, would enshrine, reigned with few interruptions until 1974.
    • Kublai Proclaims the YuanA.D. 1271, Khanbaliq, China. Kublai, who had won a four-year succession war against his brother Ariq Böke, named his dynasty Yuan, the Origin, from the Book of Changes, and was raising a new capital, Dadu, the Khanbaliq of the Turks and of Marco Polo, beside the ruins of Zhongdu. He ruled China as emperor and the Mongols as Great Khan, though the western khanates went their own way.
    • Rudolf of Habsburg on the MarchfeldA.D. 1278, Dürnkrut, Austria. Ottokar II of Bohemia, the iron and golden king whose lands stretched from the Bohemian forests to the Adriatic, refused to yield Austria to the new German king, Rudolf of Habsburg. On 26 August 1278 at Dürnkrut on the Marchfeld, Rudolf's reserve, held back against the customs of chivalry, struck the Bohemian flank, and Ottokar was slain. Austria was the Habsburgs' for 640 years.
