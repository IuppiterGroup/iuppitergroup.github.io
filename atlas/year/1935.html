    The chronicle of these years

    • Hitler Becomes ChancellorA.D. 1933, Reich Chancellery, Berlin. On 30 January 1933 President Hindenburg, persuaded by Franz von Papen that the conservatives could hold him in check, appointed Adolf Hitler chancellor at the head of a coalition cabinet. Within two months the Reichstag fire had served as pretext to suspend civil liberties, and the Enabling Act of 23 March gave Hitler power to make laws without parliament. By summer every other party was gone.
    • The Long MarchA.D. 1934, Ruijin, Jiangxi, China. Encircled by Chiang Kai-shek's blockhouses, some eighty-six thousand men of the Red Army broke out of the Jiangxi soviet at Ruijin. Over a year of marching through the mountains of the southwest, across the chains of the Luding bridge and the grasslands of the Tibetan borderlands, they lost all but a few thousand. At Zunyi Mao Zedong won the leadership, and in Shaanxi the survivors made their new base.
    • The Generals Rise against the RepublicA.D. 1936, Melilla, Spanish Morocco. After the murder of the monarchist leader José Calvo Sotelo by Republican police, the army garrisons rose on 17 July 1936, beginning at Melilla in Morocco. General Francisco Franco flew from the Canaries to command the Army of Africa, which German and Italian aircraft ferried across the Strait. The rising failed in Madrid and Barcelona, and Spain was split in a civil war of three years.
    • The Xi'an IncidentA.D. 1936, Lintong, Shaanxi, China. Chiang Kai-shek flew to Xi'an to press the Manchurian marshal Zhang Xueliang to destroy the Communists. Instead Zhang's men seized Chiang at the hot springs of Huaqing, where he had fled up the hillside in his nightshirt, and held him until he agreed to end the civil war and unite against Japan. Zhang went with him to Nanjing and spent the next half century under arrest.
    • The Italians Enter Addis AbabaA.D. 1936, Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. Mussolini's armies invaded Ethiopia from Eritrea and Somaliland in October 1935, bombing Haile Selassie's armies and villages and spraying them with mustard gas. Marshal Badoglio entered Addis Ababa on 5 May 1936, the emperor having gone into exile to plead in vain before the League of Nations; five years to the day later, British and Ethiopian forces brought him home.
    • The Great TerrorA.D. 1937, Lubyanka, Moscow. After the show trial of Zinoviev and Kamenev in 1936, the purge directed by Stalin and his commissar Nikolai Yezhov swept the party, the army, and the people. Marshal Tukhachevsky and most of the high command were shot in 1937, Bukharin after the third Moscow trial in 1938; by the NKVD's own figures some 680,000 were executed in those two years, and hundreds of thousands more sent to the camps.
    • The Marco Polo Bridge IncidentA.D. 1937, Wanping, Beijing, China. On the night of 7 July a Japanese unit on manoeuvres near the Marco Polo Bridge outside Beiping reported a soldier missing and demanded to search the walled town of Wanping. Shots were exchanged, neither side would yield, and the skirmish grew into full war between Japan and China, the beginning, in Asia, of the Second World War.
    • The Invasion of PolandA.D. 1939, Westerplatte, Danzig. At dawn on 1 September 1939 the old battleship Schleswig-Holstein opened fire on the Polish depot at Westerplatte in Danzig harbor, and German armies crossed the Polish frontier from three sides. Britain and France declared war on 3 September. On the 17th the Red Army invaded from the east; Warsaw surrendered on the 28th, and Poland was partitioned between the two dictators.
