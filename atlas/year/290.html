    The chronicle of these years

    • Aurelian Takes PalmyraA.D. 272, Palmyra, Syria. Zenobia, widow of Odaenathus, had made Palmyra the seat of an eastern empire reaching from Egypt to Ancyra, proclaiming her son Vaballathus Augustus. Aurelian defeated her armies at Immae and Emesa and besieged Palmyra; the queen fled on a camel toward the Euphrates and was taken. She walked in golden chains in his triumph.
    • Diocletian's TetrarchyA.D. 293, Nicomedia, Bithynia. Diocletian, a Dalmatian soldier who had ended half a century of chaos, divided the burden of empire among four rulers: himself and Maximian as Augusti, Galerius and Constantius as Caesars. He doubled the provinces, separated civil from military command, fixed prices by edict, and ruled from Nicomedia with the ceremony of an Eastern court.
    • Armenia Becomes a Christian Kingdomc. A.D. 301, Vagharshapat, Armenia. Gregory the Illuminator, after years in a pit at Khor Virap, cured King Tiridates III of a madness and baptized him with his court; Armenian tradition sets this in 301. Armenia became the first kingdom to adopt Christianity, and Gregory built the cathedral of Etchmiadzin at Vagharshapat.
    • The Great PersecutionA.D. 303, Nicomedia, Bithynia. On the feast of the Terminalia Diocletian and Galerius had the church at Nicomedia pulled down and issued edicts ordering the scriptures burned, the churches destroyed, and the clergy imprisoned, and at last that all must sacrifice. In the East the persecution raged for eight years, the fiercest the Church ever suffered, until Galerius on his deathbed granted toleration in 311.
    • The Sack of LuoyangA.D. 311, Luoyang, Henan, China. The Xiongnu state of Han Zhao, founded by Liu Yuan amid the War of the Eight Princes, sent its armies against the Jin capital. Luoyang was stormed and burned, tens of thousands were slaughtered, and Emperor Huai was carried off to pour wine at the banquets of Liu Cong before his execution. The northern plain passed to the non-Chinese peoples for nearly three centuries.
    • In This Sign, ConquerA.D. 312, The Milvian Bridge, Rome. Marching on Rome, Constantine sees a sign in the heavens and orders the Chi-Rho painted on his soldiers’ shields. At the Milvian Bridge his rival Maxentius is driven into the Tiber and drowned. The first Christian emperor now holds the West, and within a dozen years he will hold it all.
    • The Edict of MilanA.D. 313, Mediolanum, Italy. Constantine and Licinius, meeting at Milan to marry Licinius to Constantine's sister, agreed to grant Christians and all others freedom to follow the religion of their choice and to restore the property taken from the churches. The persecutions were over, and the Church, endowed by Constantine with the Lateran and great basilicas, entered the public life of the empire.
