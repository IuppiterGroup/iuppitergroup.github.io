    The chronicle of these years

    • Alfred Victorious at EdingtonA.D. 878, Edington, England. Surprised at Chippenham in midwinter by Guthrum's Danes, Alfred of Wessex took refuge in the marshes of Athelney. In May 878 he gathered the men of Somerset, Wiltshire, and Hampshire and routed the Danes at Edington. Guthrum accepted baptism with Alfred as his godfather, and England was soon divided between Wessex and the Danelaw.
    • Huang Chao Takes Chang'anA.D. 881, Chang'an, Shaanxi, China. Huang Chao, a salt smuggler who had failed the examinations, led a rebel host the length of China, massacring the foreign merchants of Canton in 879, and entered Chang'an in triumph. He proclaimed himself emperor of Qi, but his men pillaged the city and slaughtered its officials. The Tang outlived his defeat only as a shell, ruled by its generals.
    • Oleg Takes KievA.D. 882, Kiev, Rus'. Oleg, kinsman and successor of Rurik, came down the Dnieper from Novgorod, lured the Varangian lords Askold and Dir out of Kiev by a ruse, and slew them. Making Kiev his seat, 'the mother of the cities of Rus',' he joined north and south along the great river road from the Varangians to the Greeks, and the Kievan state was born.
    • Yasovarman Founds YasodharapuraA.D. 889, Phnom Bakheng, Cambodia. Yasovarman I, crowned in 889, left Hariharalaya and laid out a new capital, Yasodharapura, about the hill of Phnom Bakheng, whose summit he crowned with a temple-mountain of 108 towers. East of it he dug a reservoir seven kilometers long. The city that grew here, called Angkor by later ages, remained the seat of the Khmer kings for more than five hundred years.
    • The Magyars Cross the Carpathiansc. A.D. 895, Verecke Pass, Carpathians. Driven from the Pontic steppe by the Pechenegs, the seven tribes of the Magyars under Árpád crossed the Carpathian passes into the plain of the Danube and the Tisza. They broke Great Moravia, and for half a century their horsemen rode out to plunder Germany, Italy, and France, until the Lechfeld taught them to settle as a Christian people.
    • The Bronzes of Igbo-Ukwuc. A.D. 900, Igbo-Ukwu, Nigeria. East of the lower Niger, the smiths of an Igbo priest-kingdom cast by the lost-wax method bronzes of a delicacy unmatched in their age: shell-shaped bowls, ornate pendants, and a ceremonial pot wrapped in a cast rope. Beside them lay a dignitary buried seated, among more than a hundred thousand beads, many of them glass brought across the Sahara from distant lands.
