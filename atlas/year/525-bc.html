    The chronicle of these years

    • Bimbisara Builds Magadhac. 540 B.C., Rajgir, Bihar, India. From Rajagriha, ringed by five hills and a wall of rough stone some forty kilometres long, King Bimbisara of the Haryanka line made Magadha the rising power of the Ganges. He annexed Anga and its port of Champa, bound Kosala and the Licchavis to him by marriage, and welcomed both the Buddha and Mahavira to his city.
    • Cyrus Enters Babylon539 B.C., Babylon, Mesopotamia. After defeating the Babylonian army at Opis, the troops of Cyrus entered Babylon without a battle, and Cyrus came as a liberator who restored the gods carried off by Nabonidus, as his clay cylinder proclaims. He let the exiles of Judah go home to rebuild the Temple in Jerusalem, and the whole Fertile Crescent was united in the Persian empire, the largest the world had yet seen.
    • The Enlightenment at Bodh Gayac. 530 B.C., Bodh Gaya, Bihar, India. Siddhartha Gautama, a prince of the Shakya clan who had left his home to seek release from suffering, sat beneath a pipal tree on the bank of the Niranjana and there attained enlightenment. As the Buddha he preached his first sermon in the deer park at Sarnath, setting forth the Four Noble Truths and the Middle Way. His teaching would spread across Asia.
    • Mahavira Passes at Pavac. 527 B.C., Pawapuri, Bihar, India. Vardhamana, a prince of the Jnatrika clan near Vaishali, had renounced his station, wandered for twelve years in naked austerity, and won the title Mahavira, the Great Hero. As the twenty-fourth Tirthankara he taught non-violence toward every living thing, truth, and the giving up of possessions. He died at Pava in the year Jain tradition reckons as 527 B.C.
    • Cambyses Conquers Egypt525 B.C., Pelusium, Egypt. Cambyses, son of Cyrus, crossed the Sinai with water supplied by Arab allies, defeated the young pharaoh Psamtik III at Pelusium, and took Memphis. Egypt became a satrapy of Persia; Psamtik was later put to death for plotting revolt, and Herodotus, three generations afterward, saw the bones of the fallen still lying on the field.
    • Darius Takes the Indusc. 518 B.C., Gandhara, Pakistan. Darius the Great carried Persian rule over Gandhara and down the Indus, sending Scylax of Caryanda to sail the river to the sea. Hindush, the Indus country, became the richest province of the empire: Herodotus says it paid 360 talents of gold dust a year, nearly a third of all the empire's tribute. Indian archers in cotton later marched with Xerxes into Greece.
    • Rome Expels Its Last King509 B.C., Forum, Rome. After the rape of the virtuous Lucretia by the king's son, Lucius Junius Brutus led the Romans in driving out Tarquin the Proud and all his house. They swore never again to suffer a king, and in his place elected two consuls for a year at a time. In the same year, tradition says, the temple of Jupiter on the Capitol was dedicated.
    • Cleisthenes Gives Athens Democracy508 B.C., Athens, Greece. When the tyrant's family had been expelled and the Spartan king Cleomenes tried to set up an oligarchy, the Athenian people rose and recalled Cleisthenes. He broke the power of the old clans by dividing the citizens into ten new tribes drawn from the city, the coast, and the inland, and founded the council of five hundred: the government of the people, demokratia, was born.
