    The chronicle of these years

    • The Fall of the Berlin WallA.D. 1989, Bornholmer Strasse, Berlin. After a summer of flight through Hungary and an autumn of mass demonstrations in Leipzig, the East German party spokesman Günter Schabowski announced on the evening of 9 November 1989, by mistake, that citizens might cross the border at once. Crowds massed at the checkpoints; at Bornholmer Strasse the guards opened the gate before midnight, and Berliners danced on the Wall.
    • The Velvet RevolutionA.D. 1989, Wenceslas Square, Prague. When riot police beat a student march in Prague on 17 November 1989, the city rose in nightly demonstrations of hundreds of thousands in Wenceslas Square, where Dubček reappeared beside the playwright Václav Havel of Civic Forum. A general strike on 27 November ended communist rule without a shot, and on 29 December the Federal Assembly elected Havel president.
    • The Fall of CeaușescuA.D. 1989, Târgoviște, Romania. The attempted arrest of the Hungarian pastor László Tőkés at Timișoara set off a rising in mid-December 1989 that the Securitate met with gunfire. On 21 December Nicolae Ceaușescu was jeered by the crowd he had summoned in Bucharest, and he fled by helicopter the next day. Captured, he and his wife Elena were tried by a drumhead court at Târgoviște and shot on Christmas Day.
    • Tiananmen SquareA.D. 1989, Tiananmen, Peking, China. After the death of the reformer Hu Yaobang, students occupied Tiananmen Square for seven weeks, raising a white Goddess of Democracy to face Mao's portrait. On the night of 3 to 4 June the army cleared the streets of Beijing with tanks and rifle fire, killing hundreds, perhaps thousands. The party chose economic reform without political freedom.
    • Germany ReunitedA.D. 1990, Reichstag, Berlin. After the East German elections of March 1990 and a currency union in July, the Treaty on the Final Settlement signed in Moscow in September by the two German states and the four wartime allies restored full sovereignty to a united Germany. At midnight on 3 October 1990 the black, red, and gold flag rose before the Reichstag, and the five eastern states joined the Federal Republic.
    • Iraq Invades KuwaitA.D. 1990, Kuwait City, Kuwait. Accusing Kuwait of stealing its oil and driving down prices, Saddam Hussein sent his army over the border on 2 August 1990 and overran the emirate in a day; the emir fled to Saudi Arabia, and Kuwait was declared Iraq's nineteenth province. Fearing for the Saudi oil fields, the United States assembled a coalition of some thirty-five nations and over half a million American troops in the desert.
    • Mandela Walks FreeA.D. 1990, Cape Town, South Africa. President F. W. de Klerk lifted the ban on the ANC and on 11 February 1990 released Nelson Mandela after twenty-seven years in prison; that evening Mandela spoke to a vast crowd from the balcony of Cape Town's City Hall. Four years of negotiation, shadowed by bloody strife in Natal and the townships, brought apartheid to an end.
    • The End of the Soviet UnionA.D. 1991, Kremlin, Moscow. On 8 December 1991, at a hunting lodge in the Belovezha Forest, the leaders of Russia, Ukraine, and Belarus declared that the USSR had ceased to exist and founded a Commonwealth of Independent States. Gorbachev resigned on Christmas Day; that evening the red flag came down over the Kremlin, and fifteen republics stood as independent states.
