    The chronicle of these years

    • The First Emperor221 B.C., Xianyang, China. King Zheng of Qin swallows the last of the Warring States and takes a new title, Qin Shi Huang, the First Emperor. He standardizes script, coinage, weights, and axle widths, joins the northern walls, and goes to his tomb guarded by an army of terracotta soldiers.
    • Cannae216 B.C., Apulia, Italy. Hannibal draws a Roman army of some eighty thousand into his center at Cannae, closes the wings, and annihilates it in a single day, one of the bloodiest in the history of war. Rome refuses to ask for terms. It raises new legions and fights on.
    • The First Emperor Dies at Shaqiu210 B.C., Shaqiu, Hebei, China. Seeking the elixir of immortality on a tour of the east, the First Emperor died at Shaqiu. The eunuch Zhao Gao and the chancellor Li Si hid his death, carting salted fish beside his litter to cover the stench, and forged an edict ordering the heir Fusu to kill himself. The younger son Huhai took the throne as Second Emperor, and the dynasty began its swift collapse.
    • Chen Sheng Rises at Dazexiang209 B.C., Dazexiang, Anhui, China. Two conscript leaders, Chen Sheng and Wu Guang, held up by rain on the road to the frontier, knew that lateness meant death under the laws of Qin. They killed their officers and raised the cry: are kings and nobles a breed apart? Their rising lasted barely six months, but it set the whole empire ablaze.
    • Modu Becomes Chanyu209 B.C., Mongolian Steppe, Mongolia. Modu, son of the Xiongnu chanyu Touman, trained his horsemen to shoot wherever his whistling arrow flew, testing them on his own horse and wife and killing those who held back. On a hunt he loosed an arrow at his father, and his men cut Touman down. Modu then crushed the Donghu, drove back the Yuezhi, and built the first great empire of the steppe.
    • Xiang Yu Burns Xianyang206 B.C., Xianyang, Shaanxi, China. Liu Bang reached the Qin capital first and took the surrender of Ziying, but yielded it to the stronger Xiang Yu, escaping with his life from the feast at Hong Gate. Xiang Yu killed Ziying, burned the palaces, which smouldered, it is said, for three months, and carved the empire into eighteen kingdoms, taking for himself the title Hegemon-King of Western Chu.
    • Scipio Defeats Hannibal at Zama202 B.C., Zama, Africa. Publius Cornelius Scipio, who had conquered Spain, carried the war to Africa and allied himself with the Numidian prince Masinissa. At Zama he let Hannibal's elephants pass through lanes in his lines, and the Numidian and Roman horse, returning from the pursuit, fell on the Carthaginian rear. Carthage gave up its fleet and its empire, and Scipio was named Africanus.
    • Gaixia and the Founding of Han202 B.C., Gaixia, Anhui, China. Surrounded at Gaixia, Xiang Yu heard the songs of Chu rising from the Han camps on every side and knew his homeland had gone over to the enemy. He broke out with eight hundred horsemen, made his last stand at the Wu River, and cut his own throat. Liu Bang, once a village constable, was proclaimed emperor, founder of the dynasty that gave the Han Chinese their name.
