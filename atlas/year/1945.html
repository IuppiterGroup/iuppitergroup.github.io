    The chronicle of these years

    • Operation BarbarossaA.D. 1941, Brest Fortress, Belarus. Before dawn on 22 June 1941 more than three million German soldiers in three army groups, with their allies, crossed the Soviet frontier from the Baltic to the Black Sea. The garrison of the Brest fortress fought on for weeks, but the Red Army lost millions in vast encirclements at Białystok, Minsk, Smolensk, and Kiev. By December the Germans stood before Moscow and Leningrad.
    • The Fall of SingaporeA.D. 1942, Singapore. After a campaign of seventy days down the length of Malaya, General Yamashita's army crossed the Johor Strait, and on 15 February 1942 General Arthur Percival surrendered Singapore with some eighty thousand British, Australian, and Indian troops, the greatest capitulation in British history. The belief in European invincibility in Asia died with it, and tens of thousands of Chinese were massacred in the purge that followed.
    • The Surrender at StalingradA.D. 1943, Stalingrad, Russia. Through the autumn of 1942 the German Sixth Army fought for Stalingrad street by street and factory by factory on the Volga. On 19 November Zhukov and Vasilevsky's Operation Uranus broke the Romanian armies on its flanks and closed the ring. Forbidden to break out, the newly made Field Marshal Friedrich Paulus surrendered on 31 January 1943; some 91,000 starving men went into captivity, and few came home.
    • The Surrender of GermanyA.D. 1945, Karlshorst, Berlin. With the Red Army fighting through the ruins of Berlin, Hitler shot himself in his bunker on 30 April 1945, and two days later the city's garrison surrendered. Grand Admiral Dönitz's envoys signed an unconditional surrender at Reims on 7 May, ratified before Marshal Zhukov at Karlshorst late on 8 May. The war in Europe was over; it had cost some forty million European lives.
    • The Atomic Bomb on HiroshimaA.D. 1945, Hiroshima, Japan. On 6 August an American B-29 dropped a single uranium bomb on Hiroshima, destroying the city and killing some seventy thousand people at once, with tens of thousands more dying by the year's end. Three days later a second bomb fell on Nagasaki, and the Soviet Union invaded Manchuria. On 15 August Emperor Hirohito broadcast the surrender, and on 2 September it was signed in Tokyo Bay.
    • The Trinity TestA.D. 1945, Trinity Site, New Mexico. At 5:29 in the morning of 16 July 1945, in the desert of the Jornada del Muerto, the scientists of the Manhattan Project under J. Robert Oppenheimer exploded the first atomic bomb, with the force of some twenty thousand tons of TNT. Oppenheimer recalled the words of the Bhagavad Gita, 'Now I am become Death, the destroyer of worlds'; within a month the bombs fell on Hiroshima and Nagasaki.
    • Independence and PartitionA.D. 1947, New Delhi, India. At midnight on 14 August 1947 British rule ended and two dominions were born, Pakistan and India, divided by lines Sir Cyril Radcliffe had drawn in five weeks through the Punjab and Bengal. Nehru told the Constituent Assembly that India awoke to life and freedom. Some fifteen million people fled across the new borders, and perhaps a million were killed in the massacres.
    • The State of Israel ProclaimedA.D. 1948, Tel Aviv, Palestine. On 14 May 1948, as the British Mandate expired, David Ben-Gurion read the declaration of independence of the State of Israel in the Tel Aviv museum. The next day the armies of Egypt, Transjordan, Syria, Iraq, and Lebanon invaded. By the armistices of 1949 Israel held more than three-quarters of Mandatory Palestine; Jordan held the West Bank and East Jerusalem, and Egypt the Gaza Strip.
