    The chronicle of these years

    • The Union of LublinA.D. 1569, Lublin, Poland. On 1 July 1569, under pressure of the long war with Muscovy, the Polish and Lithuanian nobles sealed at Lublin a lasting union of their states into one Commonwealth of Both Nations, with a common king elected by all the nobility and a common Diet. Lithuania's Ukrainian lands passed to the Polish crown, and the Commonwealth was among the largest states of Europe.
    • Bayinnaung Takes AyutthayaA.D. 1569, Ayutthaya, Thailand. Bayinnaung, who styled himself king of kings, took Ayutthaya after a siege of ten months and set Maha Thammarachathirat on its throne as his vassal. His empire, the largest Southeast Asia had ever seen, stretched from Manipur to Lan Xang; yet when he died in 1581 it was held together by little more than his own will.
    • LepantoA.D. 1571, Gulf of Patras, Greece. The galleys of the Holy League under Don John of Austria shatter the Ottoman fleet at Lepanto, the last great battle of oared ships. A young soldier named Cervantes, who lost the use of his left hand there, called it the noblest occasion the centuries had ever seen.
    • Legazpi Founds Spanish ManilaA.D. 1571, Manila, Philippines. Having driven Rajah Sulayman from his stockade on the Pasig, Legazpi proclaimed Manila the capital of the Spanish Philippines on 24 June 1571. From 1573 the Manila galleons carried Chinese silk and porcelain to Acapulco and returned with Mexican silver: for the first time the trade of the Americas, Asia, and Europe was joined in one ring around the globe.
    • Akbar Builds Fatehpur SikriA.D. 1571, Fatehpur Sikri, Uttar Pradesh, India. In thanksgiving for the birth of a son foretold by the Sufi Shaikh Salim Chishti, Akbar built a red sandstone city on the ridge at Sikri and, after conquering Gujarat, named it Fatehpur, City of Victory. In its House of Worship he heard Muslim divines, Brahmins, Jains, Zoroastrians, and Jesuit fathers dispute. He left it within fifteen years.
    • The Massacre of St. BartholomewA.D. 1572, Louvre, Paris. Days after the wedding of the Protestant Henry of Navarre to the king's sister, on the night of 23 to 24 August 1572, the court of Charles IX and Catherine de' Medici had the Huguenot leader Admiral Coligny murdered, and the Catholic mob of Paris turned on the Huguenots gathered for the wedding. Some three thousand died in Paris and thousands more in the provinces.
    • The Battle of the Three KingsA.D. 1578, Ksar el-Kebir, Morocco. King Sebastian of Portugal landed in Morocco to restore the deposed Saadi sultan al-Mutawakkil against his uncle Abd al-Malik. On 4 August 1578 near Ksar el-Kebir the Portuguese army was enveloped and destroyed; all three kings died, Sebastian vanishing in the melee. Abd al-Malik's brother Ahmad al-Mansur grew rich on the ransoms of Portuguese nobles, and Portugal soon passed to Philip II of Spain.
    • The Title of Dalai LamaA.D. 1578, Kokonor, Qinghai, China. Altan Khan met Sonam Gyatso, head of the Gelug order, in the Kokonor country and gave him the title Dalai Lama, Ocean of Wisdom, while the lama hailed Altan as a reincarnation of Kublai. The title was carried back to his two predecessors, so that Sonam Gyatso became the Third Dalai Lama. The Mongols turned to the Gelug faith, and their swords would one day make it master of Tibet.
