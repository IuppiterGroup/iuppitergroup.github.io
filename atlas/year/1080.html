    The chronicle of these years

    • HastingsA.D. 1066, Sussex, England. William, Duke of Normandy, breaks the Saxon shield wall at Hastings after a long day of slaughter. King Harold falls, by legend with an arrow in his eye. On Christmas Day William is crowned at Westminster, and the lords of England will speak French for three centuries.
    • Vijayabahu Frees LankaA.D. 1070, Polonnaruwa, Sri Lanka. Vijayabahu I, a prince of Ruhuna, waged war on the Chola occupiers for years and in 1070 drove them from Lanka, while the Chola realm was torn by a disputed succession. He kept Polonnaruwa as his capital, restored the Buddhist order by bringing monks from Burma to renew the ordination, and reigned in all for more than half a century.
    • The Battle of ManzikertA.D. 1071, Manzikert, Armenia. On 26 August 1071 the Seljuk sultan Alp Arslan met Emperor Romanos IV Diogenes near Manzikert, north of Lake Van. Feigned flight drew the Roman host apart, the rearguard under Andronikos Doukas deserted, and the emperor was taken prisoner, the first to fall into a Muslim ruler's hands. Alp Arslan released him on easy terms, but Turkmen bands poured into Anatolia, and Asia Minor began its long change into Turkey.
    • Henry IV at CanossaA.D. 1077, Canossa, Italy. Excommunicated by Gregory VII and deserted by his princes, the German king Henry IV crossed the Alps in midwinter and stood three days in the snow, barefoot and in penitent's garb, before the castle of Countess Matilda of Tuscany, where the pope had taken refuge. Gregory lifted the ban, but the image of a king humbled before a pope outlived them both.
    • Alfonso VI Takes ToledoA.D. 1085, Toledo, Castile. On 25 May 1085 Alfonso VI of León and Castile entered Toledo, the ancient capital of the Visigoths, after years of pressure on its taifa king. Christian Spain now held the line of the Tagus and the heart of the peninsula, and Toledo's scholars, Christian, Muslim, and Jewish, would turn the Arabic learning of Aristotle and Ptolemy into Latin.
    • The Almoravids at SagrajasA.D. 1086, Sagrajas, near Badajoz, Spain. Frightened by Alfonso VI's capture of Toledo, the taifa kings of al-Andalus summoned Yusuf ibn Tashfin, the veiled Saharan emir of the Almoravids. On 23 October 1086, at Sagrajas, called by the Arabs al-Zallaqa, his Berber and Black African troops broke the Castilian army, and Alfonso fled wounded. Yusuf soon deposed the taifa kings themselves and made al-Andalus a province of his Moroccan empire.
    • Hasan-i Sabbah Seizes AlamutA.D. 1090, Alamut, Daylam, Persia. The Isma'ili missionary Hasan-i Sabbah won over the garrison of the eagle's nest castle of Alamut in the Elburz mountains in 1090. From it he led the Nizari Isma'ilis, called by their enemies hashishiyya, whence our word assassin, in a long war against the Seljuks waged with daggers rather than armies. Their castles held out until Hulagu's Mongols reduced them in 1256.
    • Urban II Preaches the CrusadeA.D. 1095, Clermont, Auvergne. On 27 November 1095, in a field outside Clermont, Pope Urban II called upon the knights of France to go to the aid of the Christians of the East and free Jerusalem from the Turks, promising remission of sins to all who died on the way. The crowd answered, 'God wills it.' Within a year tens of thousands had sewn the cross upon their garments.
