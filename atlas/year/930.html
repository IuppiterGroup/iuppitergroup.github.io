    The chronicle of these years

    • Simeon Triumphs at AchelousA.D. 917, Achelous, Bulgaria. Simeon of Bulgaria, schooled at Constantinople and called half-Greek, sought the imperial crown itself. On 20 August 917 his army fell on the Byzantine host of Leo Phokas by the river Achelous near Anchialos and destroyed it in one of the bloodiest battles of the age. For a decade Simeon was master of the Balkans, styling himself emperor of the Bulgarians and the Romans.
    • Wang Geon Founds GoryeoA.D. 918, Kaesong, Korea. The officers of the tyrant Gung Ye, who called himself a living Maitreya and had murdered his own wife and sons, overthrew him and raised the general Wang Geon as king. Wang named his kingdom Goryeo after old Goguryeo, the name from which Korea comes, and set his capital at his home of Songak, later Kaesong. In 935 Silla yielded to him, and in 936 he united the peninsula.
    • Ibn Fadlan among the BulgarsA.D. 922, Bolghar, Volga. An embassy from the caliph al-Muqtadir reached the Bulgar king Almış on the middle Volga in May 922, bringing a teacher of the law and a builder of mosques. The king embraced Islam and had the caliph's name recited at the Friday prayer. Ibn Fadlan, the embassy's secretary, left a vivid account of the journey, with his famed description of a Rus chieftain's ship burial.
    • Abd al-Rahman III Takes the Caliph's TitleA.D. 929, Córdoba, Spain. In January 929 the emir Abd al-Rahman III, who had brought rebellious al-Andalus to heel, proclaimed himself caliph and Commander of the Faithful in answer to the Fatimids across the strait. Under him and his son al-Hakam II Córdoba became the richest city of the West, with a library said to hold 400,000 volumes and embassies from Constantinople and from the German king Otto I.
    • The Qarmatians Carry Off the Black StoneA.D. 930, Mecca, Arabia. Abu Tahir al-Jannabi, ruler of the Qarmatian state of Bahrain in eastern Arabia, fell upon Mecca during the pilgrimage of 930. His men slaughtered thousands of pilgrims, threw their bodies into the well of Zamzam, and tore the Black Stone from the Kaaba. They kept it at al-Ahsa for over twenty years before returning it in 951.
    • The Sixteen Prefectures CededA.D. 936, Youzhou, Hebei, China. Shi Jingtang, a Shatuo general rebelling against Later Tang, bought the help of the Khitan emperor Yelü Deguang by calling himself his son and ceding sixteen prefectures south of the mountains, among them Youzhou, the later Beijing, and Yunzhou, the later Datong. With them went the passes that guarded the North China plain, which no Chinese dynasty held again for over four centuries.
    • Athelstan at BrunanburhA.D. 937, Brunanburh, England. Athelstan of Wessex, first king to rule all the English, met a grand alliance of Olaf Guthfrithson of Dublin, Constantine II of the Scots, and Owain of Strathclyde. In the greatest slaughter yet seen on the island, the Chronicle sang, the invaders were broken. The field is still disputed, perhaps on the Wirral, but the victory confirmed the making of England.
    • Ngo Quyen at the Bach Dang RiverA.D. 938, Bach Dang River, Vietnam. Ngo Quyen planted iron-tipped stakes in the bed of the Bach Dang and, when the tide ran in, lured the war junks of the Southern Han upstream. As the water fell the fleet was impaled and broken, and the Han prince Liu Hongcao was slain. The victory ended a thousand years of Chinese rule, and Ngo Quyen made himself king at Co Loa.
