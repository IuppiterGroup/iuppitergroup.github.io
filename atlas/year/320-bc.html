    The chronicle of these years

    • Alexander at the Granicus334 B.C., Granicus River, Mysia. Alexander crossed the Hellespont with some 35,000 men, cast a spear into the soil of Asia, and honored the tomb of Achilles at Troy. At the river Granicus he charged at the head of his Companion cavalry across the stream against the Persian satraps, was nearly killed until Cleitus the Black cut down his attacker, and won the first of his great victories.
    • The Battle of Issus333 B.C., Issus, Cilicia. Darius III came down behind Alexander with the royal army and met him on the narrow coastal plain of Issus. Alexander's cavalry broke the Persian left and drove at the king, who fled in his chariot, leaving behind his mother, his wife, and his daughters, whom Alexander treated with courtesy. The road to Syria and Egypt lay open.
    • Alexander at Gaugamela331 B.C., Gaugamela, Assyria. Alexander of Macedon, outnumbered several times over, drives his Companion cavalry straight at Darius III. The Great King flees the field for the second time, and the Persian Empire, master of the East for two centuries, falls to a man of twenty-five.
    • Alexander Founds Alexandria331 B.C., Alexandria, Egypt. Welcomed in Egypt as a deliverer from the Persians, Alexander chose a site between Lake Mareotis and the sea opposite the island of Pharos and marked out a city, tracing its streets with barley meal. He went on to the oracle of Ammon at Siwa, which hailed him as the son of the god. Alexandria became the greatest Greek city in the world.
    • Alexander at the Hydaspes326 B.C., Jhelum River, Pakistan. With the river swollen by rain and melting snows, Alexander slipped across the Hydaspes by night and fell upon King Porus, whose line was anchored by some two hundred elephants. The beasts trampled friend and foe alike before the phalanx prevailed. Porus, a giant of a man, was brought in wounded and asked how he would be treated: as a king, he answered.
    • The Army Turns Back at the Hyphasis326 B.C., Beas River, Punjab, India. On the banks of the Hyphasis, with the Ganges plain and the Nanda empire before them, Alexander's veterans refused to march further. Eight years from Macedon, worn by monsoon rains and battle, they heard his pleading in silence, and Coenus spoke for them. Alexander raised twelve great altars to the gods and turned back toward the Indus and the sea.
    • Alexander Dies in Babylon323 B.C., Babylon, Mesopotamia. Planning a voyage around Arabia, Alexander fell into a fever after a night of drinking and died in the palace of Nebuchadnezzar on 10 or 11 June, not yet thirty-three. Asked to whom he left his empire, he was said to answer, 'To the strongest.' His generals divided it in a generation of wars, and Ptolemy carried off his body to Egypt.
    • Chandragupta Founds the Mauryan Empirec. 321 B.C., Pataliputra, Bihar, India. With the counsel of the Brahmin Chanakya, the young Chandragupta Maurya raised an army, cleared the Macedonian garrisons from the Punjab, and overthrew Dhana Nanda at Pataliputra. From that city he ruled the first empire to embrace nearly all of northern India, governed through a great standing army, a host of salaried officials, and a corps of spies.
