    The chronicle of these years

    • The Three Families Destroy Zhi453 B.C., Jinyang, Shanxi, China. Zhi Bo, strongest of the ministers who ruled Jin, besieged Zhao Xiangzi in Jinyang and flooded the city until frogs bred in the cooking pots. But the Han and Wei clans secretly turned against him, broke the dikes onto his own camp, and with Zhao destroyed him. The three families divided Jin among themselves, and the age of the Warring States began.
    • The Twelve Tables451 B.C., Forum, Rome. To end the patricians' hold on unwritten law, the Romans suspended the consuls and chose ten men, the decemvirs, to write the law down. Their Twelve Tables, set up in bronze in the Forum, governed debt, family, property, and crime, and Roman boys learned them by heart for centuries: the root of the civil law of Europe.
    • The Parthenon Rises447 B.C., Acropolis, Athens. At the urging of Pericles, and paid in part with the tribute of the Delian League, the architects Ictinus and Callicrates began a new temple of Athena on the Acropolis, with Phidias directing its sculpture and making its statue of the goddess in gold and ivory. Finished in 432, the Parthenon remains the standard of classical architecture.
    • The Peloponnesian War Begins431 B.C., Plataea, Greece. In the spring of 431 three hundred Thebans slipped into Plataea by night, and the long-dreaded war between Athens and Sparta broke out. King Archidamus invaded Attica, while Pericles brought the country people inside the Long Walls and trusted to the fleet. Thucydides, an Athenian, began his history of the war at once, believing it the greatest ever fought.
