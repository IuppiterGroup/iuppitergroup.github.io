    The chronicle of these years

    • The Siege of PlevnaA.D. 1877, Plevna, Bulgaria. After the Ottoman suppression of the Bulgarian April Rising of 1876, in which thousands of Christians were massacred at Batak and elsewhere, Russia went to war with Turkey. Osman Pasha's entrenched garrison at Plevna threw back three Russian and Romanian assaults; starved out, it surrendered on 10 December 1877, and the Russians were soon at the gates of Constantinople.
    • The Satsuma RebellionA.D. 1877, Kagoshima, Japan. Saigō Takamori, the great hero of the Restoration, led the disaffected samurai of Satsuma against the government that had taken their swords and stipends. They besieged Kumamoto castle in vain; the new conscript army of peasants relieved it, drove them back, and cornered the last of them on the hill of Shiroyama above Kagoshima, where Saigō fell. The age of the samurai ended with him.
    • The Congress of BerlinA.D. 1878, Radziwill Palace, Berlin. Alarmed by the Treaty of San Stefano and its great Russian-sponsored Bulgaria, the powers met in Berlin in June and July 1878 under Bismarck, the honest broker. Romania, Serbia, and Montenegro became independent; Bulgaria was cut to about a third and left under the Sultan's suzerainty; Austria-Hungary occupied Bosnia and Herzegovina, and Disraeli brought home Cyprus and peace with honor.
    • IsandlwanaA.D. 1879, Isandlwana, South Africa. Lord Chelmsford invaded Zululand to break the power of King Cetshwayo and left his camp beneath the crag of Isandlwana lightly guarded. On 22 January some 20,000 Zulu warriors overran it and killed more than 1,300 of its defenders, Britain's most humiliating defeat of the Victorian age; that night some 150 men held the mission station at Rorke's Drift against thousands.
    • The British at Tel el-KebirA.D. 1882, Tel el-Kebir, Egypt. Colonel Ahmed Urabi led a movement of Egyptian officers against the khedive Tewfik and the European controllers of Egypt's debts. After riots in Alexandria, the British bombarded the city, landed an army on the Suez Canal, and on 13 September 1882 stormed Urabi's lines at Tel el-Kebir after a night march. Britain occupied Egypt for seventy-four years.
    • The Eruption of KrakatoaA.D. 1883, Krakatoa, Sunda Strait. On 27 August 1883 the volcano of Krakatoa in the Sunda Strait destroyed itself in four tremendous explosions, the last heard on Rodrigues in the Indian Ocean, three thousand miles away. Its waves, more than thirty meters high, swept away 165 villages on the coasts of Java and Sumatra and some 36,000 people; the telegraph carried the news around the world within hours.
    • The Berlin Conference on AfricaA.D. 1884, Wilhelmstrasse, Berlin. From November 1884 to February 1885 the delegates of fourteen powers met in Bismarck's residence on the Wilhelmstrasse to settle the rules for claiming Africa. The General Act required effective occupation of coasts claimed, declared free trade on the Congo and the Niger, and in effect recognized King Leopold II's International Association as the Congo Free State, the King's personal domain.
