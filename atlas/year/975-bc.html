    The chronicle of these years

    • The Phoenician Alphabetc. 1000 B.C., Byblos, Phoenicia. At Byblos the sarcophagus of King Ahiram bears an inscription in twenty-two letters, each standing for a consonant, the script the Phoenicians carried in their ships to every shore of the Mediterranean. From it came the Aramaic and Hebrew letters, and through the Greeks, who added vowels, the Etruscan and Latin alphabets in which most of the world now writes.
    • David Takes Jerusalemc. 1000 B.C., Jerusalem, Judah. David, son of Jesse of Bethlehem, who had fought the Philistines in the service of King Saul, was anointed king over Judah and then over all Israel. He took the Jebusite stronghold of Jerusalem, made it the City of David, and brought into it the Ark of the Covenant, making it the royal and holy center of his people for all time.
    • The Kurus Rule the Doabc. 1000 B.C., Hastinapur, Uttar Pradesh, India. As iron came into use the Vedic tribes pushed east into the plain between the Yamuna and the Ganges. There the Kuru kings united the clans, fixed the great sacrifices in their solemn order, and gathered the hymns into the collections of the Vedas. Hastinapura, remembered in the Mahabharata as the Kuru capital, lay at the heart of this first true Vedic kingdom.
    • Solomon Builds the Templec. 960 B.C., Jerusalem, Israel. Solomon, son of David, built the Temple of the Lord on Mount Moriah with cedar from Lebanon and craftsmen sent by Hiram, king of Tyre, and set the Ark of the Covenant in its inner sanctuary. His reign, remembered for its wisdom, its wealth, and the fleets sent to Ophir, marked the height of the united kingdom of Israel.
