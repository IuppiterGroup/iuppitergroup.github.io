    The chronicle of these years

    • The Ottoman Sultanate AbolishedA.D. 1922, Ankara, Turkey. On 1 November 1922 the Grand National Assembly at Ankara, led by Mustafa Kemal, abolished the Ottoman sultanate, and on 17 November Mehmed VI, last of the thirty-six sultans of the house of Osman, left Constantinople on a British warship. His cousin Abdulmejid was left as caliph alone, until the caliphate itself was abolished in March 1924. Six centuries of Ottoman rule were at an end.
    • The Irish Free StateA.D. 1922, Dublin Castle, Dublin. The Anglo-Irish Treaty signed in London in December 1921 by Michael Collins and Arthur Griffith made twenty-six counties a self-governing dominion, and on 16 January 1922 Collins took over Dublin Castle from the last Lord Lieutenant. De Valera rejected the oath to the Crown; civil war followed, Collins was killed in an ambush at Béal na Bláth in August, and the Free State won by May 1923.
    • The March on RomeA.D. 1922, Rome, Italy. As some thirty thousand Fascist blackshirts converged on Rome in the rain on 28 October 1922, King Victor Emmanuel III refused to sign his government's decree of martial law. The next day he summoned Benito Mussolini, who came down from Milan by sleeping car and on 30 October became, at thirty-nine, the youngest prime minister Italy had yet had.
    • The Union of Soviet Socialist RepublicsA.D. 1922, Bolshoi Theater, Moscow. On 30 December 1922 the First Congress of Soviets of the USSR, meeting in the Bolshoi Theater in Moscow, approved the treaty uniting the Russian, Ukrainian, Belorussian, and Transcaucasian soviet republics in a federal union. Its republics had the right on paper to secede; in fact all answered to the Communist Party, whose general secretary since April was Joseph Stalin.
    • The Turkish Republic ProclaimedA.D. 1923, Ankara, Turkey. The Treaty of Lausanne, signed in July 1923, recognized a Turkish state in Anatolia and eastern Thrace. On 29 October 1923 the assembly proclaimed the Republic of Turkey with Ankara as its capital and Mustafa Kemal as president. In the years that followed he closed the Sufi lodges, replaced the sharia with European codes, gave women the vote, and put Turkish into the Latin alphabet.
    • The Death of LeninA.D. 1924, Gorki, near Moscow. Paralyzed by strokes, Lenin died at Gorki outside Moscow on 21 January 1924, leaving a testament that warned against Stalin's rudeness and power. Over his widow's protests his body was embalmed and laid in a mausoleum on Red Square, Petrograd was renamed Leningrad, and within five years Stalin had outmaneuvered Trotsky, Zinoviev, Kamenev, and Bukharin for the succession.
    • Reza Khan Takes the Peacock ThroneA.D. 1925, Tehran, Persia. Reza Khan, an officer of the Persian Cossack Brigade who had seized Tehran in a coup in February 1921, made himself war minister and then prime minister. In October 1925 the Majlis deposed the absent Ahmad Shah, last of the Qajars, and in December a constituent assembly gave the crown to Reza Shah Pahlavi. He drove Persia toward modernity by force, and in 1935 bade the world call it Iran.
    • The Wall Street CrashA.D. 1929, New York, New York. After years of speculation on borrowed money, the New York stock market broke in October 1929; on Black Tuesday, the 29th, some sixteen million shares changed hands as prices collapsed. The crash opened the Great Depression: by 1933 a quarter of American workers were idle and thousands of banks had failed, and the slump, spreading round the world, helped carry Hitler to power.
