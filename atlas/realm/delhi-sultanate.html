    Delhi Sultanate

    1333 to 1343. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1333; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The succession of Muslim sultanates that ruled northern India from Delhi between 1206 and 1526 under the Mamluk, Khalji, Tughlaq, Sayyid, and Lodi dynasties. It held off the Mongols, carried its arms to the far south, raised the Qutb Minar, and took Hindu captives into slavery in great numbers. Timur shattered it in 1398, and Babur ended it at Panipat.

    Government
    Sultanate (Turkic and Afghan military monarchy)
    Faith
    Sunni Islam; Hindu majority
    Capitals
    Lahore (1206 to 1211); Delhi (1211 to 1327); Daulatabad (1327 to 1335); Delhi (1335 to 1506); Agra (1506 to 1526)
    Greatest extent
    About 2.4 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1333
