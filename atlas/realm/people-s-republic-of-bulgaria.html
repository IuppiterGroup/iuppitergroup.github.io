    People's Republic of Bulgaria

    1946 to 1990. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1947; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Communist people's republic proclaimed in September 1946 under Georgi Dimitrov, the most loyal of the Soviet satellites; Todor Zhivkov led it for thirty-five years from 1954, and from 1984 tried to force its Turkish minority to take Slavic names, driving some 300,000 to flee in 1989. He was deposed that November.

    Government
    Communist one-party state
    Faith
    Bulgarian Orthodox Christianity, under state atheism
    Capital
    Sofia (1946 to 1990)
    Greatest extent
    About 111 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1947

    Rulers of the People's Republic of Bulgaria

    • Georgi Dimitrov (prime minister) 1946 to 1949
    • Vasil Kolarov (prime minister) 1949 to 1950
    • Valko Chervenkov (party leader and prime minister) 1950 to 1956
    • Todor Zhivkov (party leader, then head of state) 1954 to 1989
    • Anton Yugov (prime minister) 1956 to 1962
    • Petar Mladenov (party leader and head of state) 1989 to 1990
