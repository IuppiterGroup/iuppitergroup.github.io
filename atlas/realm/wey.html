    Wey

    750 B.C. to 243 B.C.. Shown above as it stood in 750 B.C.; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    A Zhou state founded for a brother of King Wu in the old Shang heartland near Chaoge. Overrun by the Di in 660 B.C., it was restored by Qi and dwindled into a small principality, which Qin spared as a vassal until 209 B.C., the last of the Zhou states to perish.

    Government
    Feudal state
    Faith
    Ancestor worship
    Capitals
    Chaoge (750 B.C. to 660 B.C.); Chuqiu (658 B.C. to 630 B.C.); Diqiu (629 B.C. to 242 B.C.); Yewang (241 B.C. to 209 B.C.)
    Greatest extent
    About 51 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for 750 B.C.

    Rulers of the Wey

    • Duke Wu of Wey 812 B.C. to 758 B.C.
    • Duke Yi of Wey 668 B.C. to 660 B.C.
    • Duke Wen of Wey 659 B.C. to 635 B.C.
    • Duke Ling of Wey 534 B.C. to 493 B.C.
    • Lord Jue of Wey 229 B.C. to 209 B.C.
