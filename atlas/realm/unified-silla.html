    Unified Silla

    682 to 935. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 682; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    Silla after its conquest of Baekje and Goguryeo and its expulsion of the Tang, ruling the peninsula south of the Taedong from Gyeongju, then one of the great cities of Asia. Its golden age of Buddhist art, of Bulguksa and Seokguram, gave way in the ninth century to rebellion, and in 935 its last king yielded to Goryeo.

    Government
    Monarchy with a bone-rank aristocracy
    Faith
    Buddhism
    Capital
    Gyeongju (668 to 935)
    Greatest extent
    About 138 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 682

    • Heungdeok of Silla 826 to 836
    • Queen Jinseong 887 to 897
    • Gyeongae of Silla 924 to 927
    • Gyeongsun of Silla 927 to 935
