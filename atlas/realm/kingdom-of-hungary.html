    Kingdom of Hungary

    1000 to 1943. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1487; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Christian kingdom of the Magyars founded by St. Stephen in 1000, ruling the Carpathian basin, Croatia, and Transylvania, a bulwark of Latin Christendom against the steppe and the Turks. After Mohács in 1526 it was split among Habsburgs, Ottomans, and Transylvania, then won back by the Habsburgs and joined with Austria in the Dual Monarchy of 1867.

    Government
    Monarchy (elective in practice, hereditary in the Habsburg house from 1687)
    Faith
    Catholic Christianity, with a large Protestant minority from the sixteenth century
    Capitals
    Esztergom (1000 to 1256); Buda (1256 to 1323); Visegrád (1323 to 1408); Buda (1408 to 1541); Pressburg (1536 to 1783); Buda (1783 to 1873); Budapest (1873 to 1943)
    Greatest extent
    About 465 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1487
