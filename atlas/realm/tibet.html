    Tibet

    1363 to 1952. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1683; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    Tibet from the fall of Sakya rule to the coming of the Chinese army, governed in turn by the Phagmodrupa princes, the Rinpungpa and Tsangpa lords of Tsang, and from 1642 by the Dalai Lamas of the Gelug church under Mongol and later Qing protection. The Thirteenth Dalai Lama declared independence in 1913; Chinese troops entered in 1950.

    Government
    Princely rule, then the theocracy of the Dalai Lamas (Ganden Phodrang)
    Faith
    Tibetan Buddhism
    Capitals
    Nedong (1354 to 1481); Rinpung (1481 to 1565); Shigatse (1565 to 1642); Lhasa (1642 to 1952)
    Greatest extent
    About 1.6 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1683
