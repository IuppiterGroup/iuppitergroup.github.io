    Macedonian Empire

    675 B.C. to 292 B.C.. Shown above as it stood in 323 B.C.; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The whole realm of the Argead kings of Macedon: the kingdom Philip II made master of Greece, and the empire his son Alexander won from the Danube to the Indus and the Nile, held after Alexander's death in 323 B.C. in the name of his heirs until the Successors divided it among themselves.

    Government
    Hereditary monarchy with the assembled army
    Faith
    Greek religion
    Capitals
    Aegae (650 B.C. to 400 B.C.); Pella (400 B.C. to 331 B.C.); Babylon (331 B.C. to 321 B.C.); Pella (321 B.C. to 294 B.C.)
    Greatest extent
    About 5.0 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for 323 B.C.
