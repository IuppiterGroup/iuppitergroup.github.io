    French Mandate for Syria and Lebanon

    1920 to 1945. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1920; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Levant states placed under French mandate by the League of Nations in 1920 after Gouraud's army drove King Faisal from Damascus; France carved out Greater Lebanon, crushed the Druze revolt of 1925 to 1927, and lost the territories to British and Free French arms in 1941, leaving in 1946.

    Government
    League of Nations mandate under a French high commissioner
    Faith
    Sunni Islam, with Maronite, Orthodox, Druze, Alawite, and Shia communities
    Capital
    Beirut (1920 to 1946)
    Greatest extent
    About 214 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1920

    Rulers of the French Mandate for Syria and Lebanon

    • Henri Gouraud (high commissioner) 1919 to 1923
    • Maxime Weygand (high commissioner) 1923 to 1924
    • Maurice Sarrail (high commissioner) 1924 to 1925
    • Henry de Jouvenel (high commissioner) 1925 to 1926
    • Henri Ponsot (high commissioner) 1926 to 1933
    • Damien de Martel (high commissioner) 1933 to 1938
    • Gabriel Puaux (high commissioner) 1939 to 1940
    • Henri Dentz (high commissioner) 1940 to 1941
    • Georges Catroux (Free French delegate-general) 1941 to 1943
