    Johor Sultanate

    1512 to 1861. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1512; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The successor of Malacca, founded by the sons of Sultan Mahmud Shah after the Portuguese took the city in 1511, and ruled from shifting capitals on the Johor River and in the Riau islands. Allied with the Dutch to take Malacca in 1641, it lost its royal line in 1699 and fell under Bugis warlords in the eighteenth century.

    Government
    Sultanate with bendahara, temenggong, and later Bugis viceroys
    Faith
    Sunni Islam
    Capitals
    Johor Lama (1528 to 1587); Batu Sawar (1587 to 1613); Kota Tinggi (1685 to 1699); Riau (Bintan) (1722 to 1812)
    Greatest extent
    About 225 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1512

    Rulers of the Johor Sultanate

    • Mahmud Shah I 1511 to 1528
    • Alauddin Riayat Shah II 1528 to 1564
    • Abdul Jalil Shah III 1623 to 1677
    • Mahmud Shah II 1685 to 1699
    • Abdul Jalil IV 1699 to 1720
    • Sulaiman Badrul Alam Shah 1722 to 1760
    • Mahmud Shah III 1761 to 1812
    • Hussein Shah 1819 to 1835
