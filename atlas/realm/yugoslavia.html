    Yugoslavia

    1919 to 1945. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1945; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Kingdom of Serbs, Croats, and Slovenes proclaimed under the Serbian dynasty on 1 December 1918 and renamed Yugoslavia when Alexander I took dictatorial power in 1929; dominated by Serbs and resented by Croats, it was invaded by the Axis in April 1941 and partitioned, the young King Peter II ruling in exile.

    Government
    Constitutional monarchy; royal dictatorship from 1929
    Faith
    Serbian Orthodox, Roman Catholic, and Muslim
    Capital
    Belgrade (1918 to 1941)
    Greatest extent
    About 251 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1945

    Rulers of the Yugoslavia

    • Peter I 1918 to 1921
    • Alexander I (regent, then king) 1918 to 1934
    • Nikola Pašić (prime minister) 1921 to 1926
    • Prince Paul (regent) 1934 to 1941
    • Peter II 1934 to 1945
    • Milan Stojadinović (prime minister) 1935 to 1939
    • Dragiša Cvetković (prime minister) 1939 to 1941
    • Dušan Simović (prime minister) 1941 to 1942
