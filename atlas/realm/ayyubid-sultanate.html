    Ayyubid Sultanate

    1177 to 1249. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1192; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Kurdish dynasty of Saladin, son of Ayyub, who ended the Fatimid caliphate in 1171, united Egypt and Syria, and retook Jerusalem from the Crusaders in 1187. His heirs divided his empire into family principalities at Cairo, Damascus, Aleppo, and elsewhere, until the Mamluk guards of the last sultan seized Egypt in 1250.

    Government
    Sultanate and family confederation of principalities
    Faith
    Sunni Islam
    Capitals
    Cairo (1171 to 1250); Damascus (1174 to 1260); Aleppo (1183 to 1260)
    Greatest extent
    About 2.0 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1192
