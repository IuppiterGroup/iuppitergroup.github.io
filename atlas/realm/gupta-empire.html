    Gupta Empire

    324 to 554. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 410; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The empire of India's classical age, founded by Chandragupta I and enlarged by Samudragupta and Chandragupta II from the Ganges to the Narmada and the Arabian Sea. Its centuries saw Kalidasa's poetry, Aryabhata's astronomy, the decimal numerals, Nalanda, and the shaping of Hindu temple art. Hun invasions and ambitious vassals broke it in the sixth century.

    Government
    Monarchy (King of Kings with vassal kings)
    Faith
    Hinduism (Vaishnava), with Buddhism and Jainism
    Capital
    Pataliputra (319 to 550)
    Greatest extent
    About 2.5 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 410

    • Skandagupta 455 to 467

    • Kumaragupta II 473 to 476
    • Budhagupta 476 to 495
    • Narasimhagupta Baladitya 495 to 530
    • Kumaragupta III 530 to 540
    • Vishnugupta 540 to 550
