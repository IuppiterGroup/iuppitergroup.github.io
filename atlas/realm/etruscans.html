    Etruscans

    750 B.C. to 265 B.C.. Shown above as it stood in 500 B.C.; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Etruscans of central Italy, a people of non-Indo-European speech joined in a league of twelve cities, among them Tarquinii, Caere, Veii, Volsinii, and Clusium. Masters of bronze, painted tombs, and the art of divination, they ruled Rome under the Tarquins and Campania for a time, and were absorbed by Rome by 264 B.C.

    Government
    League of city-states under kings, later magistrates (zilath)
    Faith
    Etruscan religion (Tinia, Uni, Menrva; haruspicy)
    Greatest extent
    About 67 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for 500 B.C.

    Rulers of the Etruscans

    • Lars Porsenna (Clusium) 508 B.C.
