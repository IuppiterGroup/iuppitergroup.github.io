    Lý dynasty

    1010 to 1225. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1206; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The first great Vietnamese dynasty, founded by Ly Cong Uan in 1009, which moved the capital to Thang Long, built the Temple of Literature, held the first civil examinations, and beat back the Song and the Cham. Devoutly Buddhist, it ended in 1225 when its last ruler, the girl empress Ly Chieu Hoang, yielded the throne to her Tran husband.

    Government
    Monarchy (emperor)
    Faith
    Mahayana Buddhism with Confucian statecraft
    Capitals
    Hoa Lu (1009 to 1010); Thang Long (1010 to 1225)
    Greatest extent
    About 135 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1206

    Rulers of the Lý dynasty

    • Ly Thai To 1009 to 1028
    • Ly Thai Tong 1028 to 1054
    • Ly Thanh Tong 1054 to 1072
    • Ly Nhan Tong 1072 to 1127
    • Ly Anh Tong 1138 to 1175
    • Ly Cao Tong 1175 to 1210
    • Ly Hue Tong 1211 to 1224
    • Ly Chieu Hoang 1224 to 1225
