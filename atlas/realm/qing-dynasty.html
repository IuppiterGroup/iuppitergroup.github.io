    Qing Dynasty

    1636 to 1911. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1794; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Manchu dynasty proclaimed by Hong Taiji in 1636, which conquered China after 1644 and under the Kangxi, Yongzheng, and Qianlong emperors ruled the largest empire in Chinese history, from Mongolia and Tibet to Xinjiang and Taiwan. Weakened by the Opium Wars, the Taiping rebellion, and defeat by Japan, it fell in the revolution of 1911.

    Government
    Imperial monarchy of the Manchu banners with a Chinese bureaucracy
    Faith
    Confucianism, Buddhism, Daoism, and Manchu shamanism
    Capitals
    Mukden (1636 to 1644); Beijing (1644 to 1912)
    Greatest extent
    About 12 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1794

    Rulers of the Qing Dynasty

    • Hong Taiji 1626 to 1643
    • Shunzhi Emperor 1643 to 1661
    • Dorgon (regent) 1643 to 1650
    • Kangxi Emperor 1661 to 1722
    • Yongzheng Emperor 1722 to 1735
    • Qianlong Emperor 1735 to 1796
    • Jiaqing Emperor 1796 to 1820
    • Daoguang Emperor 1820 to 1850
    • Xianfeng Emperor 1850 to 1861
    • Tongzhi Emperor 1861 to 1875
    • Empress Dowager Cixi (regent) 1861 to 1908
    • Guangxu Emperor 1875 to 1908
    • Xuantong Emperor (Puyi) 1908 to 1912
