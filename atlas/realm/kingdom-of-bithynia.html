    Kingdom of Bithynia

    333 B.C. to 67 B.C.. Shown above as it stood in 77 B.C.; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The kingdom of the Thracian Bithynians on the Asian shore of the Bosporus and the Sea of Marmara, which kept its freedom against Alexander's Successors, invited the Galatians into Asia, and gave refuge to Hannibal. Its last king, Nicomedes IV, bequeathed it to Rome in 74 B.C.

    Government
    Hellenistic monarchy
    Faith
    Greek and Thracian religion
    Capital
    Nicomedia (264 B.C. to 74 B.C.)
    Greatest extent
    About 31 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for 77 B.C.

    Rulers of the Kingdom of Bithynia

    • Zipoetes I 326 B.C. to 278 B.C.
    • Nicomedes I 278 B.C. to 255 B.C.
    • Ziaelas 255 B.C. to 228 B.C.
    • Prusias I 228 B.C. to 182 B.C.
    • Prusias II 182 B.C. to 149 B.C.
    • Nicomedes II 149 B.C. to 127 B.C.
    • Nicomedes III 127 B.C. to 94 B.C.
    • Nicomedes IV 94 B.C. to 74 B.C.
