    French Africa

    1846 to 1957. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1944; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    France's African empire: Algeria from 1830, Senegal and the vast federations of West and Equatorial Africa, the protectorates of Tunisia and Morocco, and Madagascar, ruled from Paris through governors-general at Algiers, Dakar, and Brazzaville; Equatorial Africa rallied to de Gaulle in 1940, and nearly all of it became independent between 1956 and 1962.

    Government
    Colonies, protectorates, and the French departments of Algeria under governors-general
    Faith
    Islam, traditional religions, and Christian missions; Roman Catholicism among settlers
    Capitals
    Algiers (1846 to 1957); Saint-Louis (1895 to 1902); Dakar (1902 to 1957); Brazzaville (1910 to 1957)
    Greatest extent
    About 12 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1944
