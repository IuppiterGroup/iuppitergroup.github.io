    New Kingdom of Egypt

    1550 B.C. to 1001 B.C.. Shown above as it stood in 1200 B.C.; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    Egypt at the height of its power, from the expulsion of the Hyksos to the last Ramesside kings: an empire from the Euphrates to the Fourth Cataract, the age of Hatshepsut, Thutmose III, Akhenaten, Tutankhamun, and Ramesses II, of the temples of Karnak and Luxor and the tombs of the Valley of the Kings.

    Government
    Divine monarchy (pharaoh)
    Faith
    Egyptian religion (Amun-Ra); the Aten alone under Akhenaten
    Capitals
    Thebes (1550 B.C. to 1346 B.C.); Akhetaten (1346 B.C. to 1332 B.C.); Memphis (1332 B.C. to 1279 B.C.); Pi-Ramesses (1279 B.C. to 1069 B.C.)
    Greatest extent
    About 704 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for 1200 B.C.
