    Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth

    1572 to 1793. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1612; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Commonwealth of Both Nations formed at Lublin in 1569, an elective monarchy in which the nobility, a tenth of the people, chose its kings and wielded the liberum veto in its Sejm. It humbled Muscovy and saved Vienna, but wars, Cossack risings, and anarchy left it prey to its neighbors, who partitioned it away between 1772 and 1795.

    Government
    Elective monarchy (republic of the nobility)
    Faith
    Catholic Christianity, with Orthodox, Protestant, and Jewish subjects
    Capitals
    Kraków (1572 to 1596); Warsaw (1596 to 1795)
    Greatest extent
    About 943 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1612
