    Neo-Assyrian Empire

    900 B.C. to 601 B.C.. Shown above as it stood in 675 B.C.; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The greatest of the Mesopotamian empires, built by the warrior kings of Ashur from Ashurnasirpal II to Ashurbanipal. Ruling from Kalhu and Nineveh by deportation and terror, it held the Near East from Egypt to the Zagros, until the Medes and Babylonians destroyed Nineveh in 612 B.C.

    Government
    Absolute monarchy with provincial governors
    Faith
    Assyrian religion (Ashur)
    Capitals
    Assur (911 B.C. to 879 B.C.); Kalhu (879 B.C. to 706 B.C.); Dur-Sharrukin (706 B.C. to 705 B.C.); Nineveh (705 B.C. to 612 B.C.); Harran (612 B.C. to 609 B.C.)
    Greatest extent
    About 1.2 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for 675 B.C.
