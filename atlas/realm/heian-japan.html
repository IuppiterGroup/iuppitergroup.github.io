    Heian Japan

    793 to 1187. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 814; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    Japan of the Heian age, when the imperial court at Kyoto, ruled in fact by the Fujiwara regents and later by retired emperors, created the kana scripts, the Pillow Book, and the Tale of Genji. In the provinces the warrior bands grew strong, and the Genpei War between the Taira and the Minamoto ended the age.

    Government
    Imperial monarchy under Fujiwara regents and cloistered emperors
    Faith
    Buddhism of the Tendai, Shingon, and Pure Land schools, and Shinto
    Capital
    Heian-kyō (794 to 1185)
    Greatest extent
    About 226 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 814

    Rulers of the Heian Japan

    • Emperor Kanmu 781 to 806
    • Emperor Saga 809 to 823
    • Fujiwara no Yoshifusa (regent) 858 to 872
    • Emperor Daigo 897 to 930
    • Fujiwara no Michinaga (regent) 995 to 1027
    • Emperor Shirakawa (reigning and cloistered) 1073 to 1129
    • Emperor Go-Shirakawa (reigning and cloistered) 1155 to 1192
    • Taira no Kiyomori (chancellor) 1167 to 1181
