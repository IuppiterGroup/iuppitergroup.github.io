    Goguryeo

    36 B.C. to 681. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 480; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The northern Korean kingdom founded by Jumong, which grew from the mountains of the middle Yalu to rule Manchuria and the northern peninsula. Its horsemen and mountain fortresses beat back the armies of Sui and Tang, until Tang and Silla together destroyed it in 668.

    Government
    Monarchy
    Faith
    Shamanism, Buddhism from 372, and Confucian learning
    Capitals
    Jolbon (37 B.C. to 3); Gungnae (3 to 427); Pyongyang (427 to 668)
    Greatest extent
    About 401 thousand sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 480

    Rulers of the Goguryeo

    • Dongmyeong of Goguryeo (Jumong) 37 B.C. to 19 B.C.
    • Taejo of Goguryeo 53 to 146
    • Sosurim of Goguryeo 371 to 384
    • Gwanggaeto the Great 391 to 413
    • Jangsu of Goguryeo 413 to 491
    • Yeongyang of Goguryeo 590 to 618
    • Yeon Gaesomun (dictator) 642 to 666
    • Bojang of Goguryeo 642 to 668
