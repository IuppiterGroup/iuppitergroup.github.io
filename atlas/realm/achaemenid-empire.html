    Achaemenid Empire

    550 B.C. to 327 B.C.. Shown above as it stood in 480 B.C.; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The Persian empire founded by Cyrus the Great and ordered by Darius I into satrapies joined by royal roads, the largest state the world had yet seen, from the Aegean and Egypt to the Indus. Tolerant of the gods of its subjects, it failed to conquer Greece and fell to Alexander the Great.

    Government
    Absolute monarchy (King of Kings) with satraps
    Faith
    Zoroastrianism (Ahura Mazda), tolerant of subject cults
    Capitals
    Pasargadae (550 B.C. to 522 B.C.); Susa (522 B.C. to 330 B.C.); Persepolis (518 B.C. to 330 B.C.)
    Greatest extent
    About 5.6 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for 480 B.C.
