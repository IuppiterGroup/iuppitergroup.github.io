    Inca Empire

    1440 to 1571. Shown above as it stood in A.D. 1497; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    Tawantinsuyu, 'the four parts together,' the empire raised from Cusco by Pachacuti and Topa Inca, which within a century ruled some ten million people along 2,500 miles of the Andes, bound by roads, storehouses, and the knotted cords of the quipu. Pizarro's seizure of Atahualpa in 1532 broke it; the Inca of Vilcabamba held out until 1572.

    Government
    Divine monarchy (the Sapa Inca)
    Faith
    Inca religion (Inti, the Sun, and Viracocha)
    Capitals
    Cusco (1438 to 1533); Vilcabamba (1539 to 1572)
    Greatest extent
    About 2.0 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for A.D. 1497

    Rulers of the Inca Empire

    • Pachacuti 1438 to 1471
    • Topa Inca Yupanqui 1471 to 1493
    • Huayna Capac 1493 to 1527
    • Huascar 1527 to 1532
    • Atahualpa 1532 to 1533
    • Tupac Huallpa 1533
    • Manco Inca 1533 to 1544
    • Paullu Inca (Spanish-installed, at Cusco) 1537 to 1549
    • Sayri Tupac 1544 to 1560
    • Titu Cusi Yupanqui 1560 to 1571
    • Tupac Amaru 1571 to 1572
