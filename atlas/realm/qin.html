    Qin

    750 B.C. to 219 B.C.. Shown above as it stood in 221 B.C.; drag the timeline to watch it rise and fall.

    The westernmost of the Zhou states, granted the old Zhou homeland in 770 B.C. and hardened by long wars with the Rong. Remade by the laws of Shang Yang into a machine of farming and war, it conquered the other six Warring States and united China in 221 B.C.

    Government
    Feudal duchy, kingdom from 325 B.C.; centralized Legalist state
    Faith
    Worship of Heaven, the Five Emperors, and the ancestors
    Capitals
    Qianwei (762 B.C. to 715 B.C.); Pingyang (714 B.C. to 678 B.C.); Yong (677 B.C. to 384 B.C.); Yueyang (383 B.C. to 350 B.C.); Xianyang (350 B.C. to 221 B.C.)
    Greatest extent
    About 1.5 million sq. km, as drawn on this atlas for 221 B.C.
